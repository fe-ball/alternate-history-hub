# 戦争・動員台帳 1939-10-11 → 1941-12-07 — Relaunch R3

> **Canonical coverage:** through 1941-12-07 before Pearl Harbor second-wave resolution
> **Status:** campaign outcomes below are CLOSED / central unless marked PROVISIONAL.
> **Core correction:** Chinese avoidance is not modeled only as “fewer deaths.” Japanese gains in daytime space, engineering time, transport preservation and forward-base work are explicitly recycled into later operations.

## 1. Operational accounting rule

For each campaign track:
1. direct personnel/equipment loss
2. adaptation tax on the weaker side
3. Japanese CAP/recon/escort/sortie tax
4. Japanese recovery gain from reduced ground resistance
5. engineering/transport/base investment that carries into the next campaign

Chinese adaptation may reduce direct casualties by:
- dispersion
- night movement
- route changes
- avoiding daylight concentration
- withholding aircraft except on important days

But it pays through:
- slower concentration
- lower daytime transport efficiency
- harder artillery massing
- more abandoned heavy equipment/communications/transport
- operational fatigue

Japan may convert reduced ammunition/vehicle loss and safer daytime workspace into:
- roads
- small bridges
- rail/signal repair
- airfields
- forward fuel/ammunition dumps
- communications
- reconnaissance outposts

Large bridges, locomotives, weather, mountain terrain and specialist engineering clocks remain hard gates.

## 2. 1939 winter offensive / South Guangxi

### 1939 winter offensive
- broad historical result remains: serious transport/operational disruption, not decisive Chinese destruction
- stronger Japanese air pressure causes more dispersion/night movement
- Chinese personnel loss is not multiplied; equipment/transport/coordination tax rises
- reduced Japanese ground loss is partly reinvested into transport and field infrastructure

### South Guangxi / Nanning / Kunlun / Binyang
- Nanning bridgehead is held and improved more efficiently
- Chinese counteroffensives still occur; Kunlun/Binyang fighting is not erased
- by 1940 spring Japanese roads, storage, communications and airfield support around Nanning are better than the old working line
- after northern French Indochina changes the strategic supply route, permanent Nanning retention loses value as in history
- withdrawal in late 1940 remains; R2 recovers a somewhat larger share of vehicles, engineering stores, fuel, spares and communications equipment rather than converting the area into a permanent super-base

## 3. Zaoyang–Yichang 1940

- Zhang Zizhong dies 1940-05-16
- Chinese main forces preserve more personnel than an annihilation model would imply
- Japanese air/recon superiority increases Chinese loss of guns, vehicles, communications, horses and accumulated stores
- Yichang falls in mid-June and is **retained** in the R2 central line
- Japanese operational gain is not only the city: the logistics tail and air-base system mature faster

Working correction versus prior line around 1940-06-18:
- effective engineering work: +15–25%
- last-mile Yichang throughput: +10–20%
- vehicle serviceability: +8–15%
- forward aviation support capacity: +20–30%
- Chinese daylight transport efficiency: roughly -15–25%
- Chinese heavy-force concentration speed: roughly -15–30%

Yichang becomes a usable forward base roughly 7–12 days earlier than the previous working line, not instantly on capture day.

## 4. Hundred Regiments Offensive

- opening surprise remains; better aircraft do not magically reveal the secret concentration
- Japanese rail/bridge/communications damage still occurs
- R2 recovery after the shock is faster:
  - major repair time about 15–25% shorter
  - counter-sweep reaction about 7–10 days earlier
  - isolated-garrison supply interruption about 10–20% shorter
- this consumes part of the earlier logistics surplus
- Chinese guerrilla/raiding adaptation imposes a continuing **transport/engineer security tax**; Japanese advantages do not compound without limit

## 5. South Henan 1941

Central R2:
- operation starts around mid-January, modestly earlier than the broad historical schedule
- Chinese main force again avoids a decisive frontal destruction
- Ki-46 reconnaissance makes wingward movement harder to conceal, but does not provide omniscience
- Japanese forces avoid excessive pursuit once the main force is recognized as displaced rather than routed
- personnel loss is lower on both sides than a deep-pursuit model
- Chinese heavy equipment/communications/transport loss is relatively higher
- Japanese vehicle/engineer/command structure is better preserved
- major forces are back in reorganization areas by mid-February

## 6. Shanggao 1941

Central R2:
- attack begins about 1941-03-06/07
- Chinese encirclement concept still develops
- Japanese reconnaissance/experience identifies the danger earlier
- the historical-style deep isolation of 34th Division is avoided
- attack is cut short around the useful line; withdrawal begins before the pocket closes
- China retains Shanggao and can claim a defensive success
- Japan fails to destroy the Chinese main force, but avoids the high-cost encirclement/breakout

Working-index result versus a historical-style/old-working Shanggao = 100:
- Japanese personnel loss: 50–65
- Japanese gun/vehicle/signal loss: 50–65
- Chinese personnel loss: 80–90
- Chinese gun/vehicle/signal loss: 105–115
- campaign duration: 65–75
- Japanese post-operation rebuild time: 60–70

Chinese adaptation afterward shifts further toward night attacks on Japanese roads, bridges, engineers, dumps and transport rather than relying on the Japanese main force to enter a deep pocket.

## 7. Second Changsha 1941

- national strategic approval timing is not freely advanced by better logistics
- attack begins only modestly earlier, around mid-September working central
- force count is not inflated into a magically larger army; the gain is thicker ammunition, bridging, vehicles and air support
- Japanese command has learned that an empty front does not mean Chinese collapse
- R2 reaches the Changsha area but treats the operation as a raid-in-force / destruction and displacement operation rather than a permanent occupation attempt
- withdrawal is ordered earlier once Chinese flank concentration is recognized
- China finally retains Changsha
- Japanese losses and post-operation recovery burden are materially lower; Chinese personnel survives better but transport/equipment/communications losses accumulate

## 8. 1941 autumn total Japanese army / southern draw

Working force ledger around 1941-10-10:
- total IJA: about **2.05–2.15 million**, center about 2.10 million
- Kwantung/Manchuria: about **650–680k**
- China theater physically present: about **650–700k**
- southern-forward regions already physically occupied/forward: about **160–200k**
- southern operations already reserved/committed (including units not yet moved): about **300–350k**
- French Indochina itself: about **50k-class**
- remainder: home islands, Korea, Sakhalin, training/replacement, aviation, depots, hospitals, new formations, etc.

These are **working operational-accounting values**, not exact census totals.

R2 does not create free extra divisions:
- China losses are somewhat lower
- veteran/support/vehicle/engineer serviceability is somewhat higher
- the state then recaptures part of that surplus for southern operations
- the first things pulled are often good vehicles, engineers, bridge units, airfield construction, signals, maintenance and aviation fuel, not merely infantry headcount

By late 1941, China’s earlier “faster operation cycle” advantage reaches a ceiling because national strategic resources are being pulled south.

## 9. External perception by late 1941

### US / UK
- multiple reports indicate unusually fast and capable Japanese fighters
- specialist aviation/intelligence circles treat some reports seriously
- wider strategic assessment discounts them through uncertainty, type confusion, production doubts, racist assumptions, and a broadly correct judgement that Japan is weaker in long-term national and blue-water sustainment
- key error: using the correct long-war judgement to discount Japan’s opening operational striking power
- Japanese blue-water fleet train, repair, replacement, tanker protection, ASW and long-range sustainment weaknesses remain real

### USSR
- direct combat experience at Nomonhan gives Soviet aviation specialists a much more concrete estimate of Japanese fighter quality
- the USSR does not know the full Owari product map or future carrier strength
- it keeps somewhat more confidence/weight in Far East air defense and does not freely advertise detailed assessments to the Western allies

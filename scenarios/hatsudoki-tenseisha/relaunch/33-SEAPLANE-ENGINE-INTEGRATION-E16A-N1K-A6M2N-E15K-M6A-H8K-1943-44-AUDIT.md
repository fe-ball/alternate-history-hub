### 1.1 O3 岳 14-cylinder: why an apparently right-sized 1,320–1,450PS engine isn't free

[11] preserves the O3/岳 commercial **145×160mm ×14-cylinder / 1,320–1,450PS family** (research toward 1,550–1,650PS separately). **It is a legitimate third choice** between the historical Kinsei54 1,300hp and R3 Subaru22 1,650PS, especially when considering single-engine reconnaissance seaplanes. But do not choose it just to standardize a label:
- For **E16A1**: its existing Kinsei51/54 mount, propeller, oil/cowl diameter, folding-wing balance, float handling and 1943 Aichi production drawings are far more valuable to meet the early 1943Q4 timing than a hypothetical O3 swap. Absent a specifically cheaper certified engine package, **Kinsei54 wins**.
- For **E13A**: long-range three-crew endurance and an established Kinsei43 production/repair chain argue for no rebuild; O3 may improve power without improving hours on station after extra fuel and cowl drag.
- For **F1M2**: 875hp Zuisei13 light observation craft is simply in a different structural/size class; O3 and O4 violate the low-cost spotter premise.
- For **M6A**: the folding pressure-hangar airframe is shaped around compact Atsuta inline; an O3 radial still requires new cowling and stowage measurements.
- For a **new dedicated O3 float scout type after 1944**: a hypothetical future branch could be studied, but there is no existing accepted airframe and it would conflict with already funded E16A/float operations.

**Engine selection principle:** choose **Mitsubishi Kinsei/Kasei or Zuisei** where an airframe and field logistics are already qualified. Use Owari **O4 only where R3 had already paid for a distinctly O4-derived A6M fighter structure**, and **O5 only where the extra horsepower can justify the mass, scarce engine budget and hydro/prop qualification**.

## 2. High-value incompatibilities to keep visible

### 2.1 A6M2-N/O4 R3 ≠ historical A6M2-N Rufe just improved a bit

Historical Rufe was a float conversion of the **Sakae ~940hp A6M2**. R3 [11] already builds a substantially different **O4/昴 1,600–1,650PS Zero** as 1941 frontline. Developing a floatplane from *that* stronger airframe is not a bolt-on-and-fly historical Nakajima package: center float lifting/torque, nacelle, cooling, prop spray, fuel, trim, directional stability, handling in gusty water, airframe loads and salt corrosion all have to be re-proved.

Thus **retain the previously decided O4 family as intended new-build powerplant**, **but keep qualification dates/mass/stall/takeoff/wave limits OPEN**; we cannot infer the same 1942 production start or 327 real-world historical units. A separate **original Sakae A6M2-N** may survive as inherited/test/early interim aircraft if physically manufactured; no automatic fleetwide retrofit.

### 2.2 E16A mission/engine branch should not be unnecessarily enlarged

The original E16A1 is already designed around **Mitsubishi Kinsei 51/54 ~1,300hp**. R3 advantage derives from earlier detection/inspection of float strut & dive-brake flaws, a physically prioritized Aichi pilot batch, more reliable radio/maintenance and an experienced carrier/seaplane support network. Switching to O4/昴 1,650PS **reopens** engine mounts, vibration, radiator/cowling/exhaust interference with float supports, CG and catapult/4t-crane loaded mass, and destroys the goal of early 1943Q4 use.

**Recommended separable next design:** Kinsei 62-powered **E16A2**, ~1,560hp, perhaps tested **1944**; this is historically attested as a single incomplete variant, not automatic production. Quantify sea-level/better climb and added prop/strength/fuel vs all-up mass and crew/floats. **Avoid claiming 1943 December a full 1,560hp E16A2 regiment.**

### 2.3 Kyofu Kasei vs O5 — economics and full waterline weight

A 2,000PS engine has a marked rated-power advantage over 1,460–1,530hp Kasei, but **the waterborne acceleration/drag and buoyancy margin are not proportional to engine horsepower**. Kyofu's original contra-rotating gearbox was abandoned in favor of a normal three-blade prop to improve reliability. O5 swap re-opens torque/water-run safety, submerged prop tip margin, forward structure, engine-mount loads, float struts and recovery equipment. The 1943 program can be funded as **15–25 engine R&D** not separate high-rate production. Given Japan has many demanders for O5, **Kasei 13/15 conventional-prop Kyofu is the first actual deployable float fighter**; O5 Kyofu may not be net worthwhile even if it flies.

### 2.4 E15K Shiun: reject the “more hp makes an escape aircraft” trap

Historical prototype E15K **first flew December 1941**; main difficulty with retractable outrigger floats and emergency main-float jettison plus unusual prop; late Kasei 24 1,850hp was already substantial. Field application still suffered from mechanical complexity and exposure to faster Allied interceptors, with **only ~15 built historically**. R3 broadly improved opposing aircraft too (including British 1943 Spitfire), so giving it O5 power without fixing floats/prop and proving escape advantage is poor use of scarce O5. Keep as limited experimental project, allocate its crews/production only after cost-benefit comparison to E16A and E13A. No invented reliable throwaway float.

### 2.5 Seiran M6A vs floatplane pool

M6A is a **submarine-delivered folded airplane**, not interchangeable with Hyūga scout aircraft. The program's *effective combat calendar* is bounded by watertight hangar radius, folding wings, carrier submarine completion, preheat and four-crew assembly, catapult strength and sortie retrieval doctrine. **Atsuta liquid-cooled size/shape** is part of that architecture. Improving Atsuta reliability through factory quality checks may help, but a large O4/O5 radial redesign trades reliability against submarine geometry and engineering years. **No Seiran Benghal 1943 H-hour**; keep it in its own special submarine program. Smithsonian establishes first prototype **Oct 1943 completion and Nov 1943 flight testing**; no 1943 combat squadron.

### 2.6 H8K2 four engines means four-way national opportunity cost

The historical H8K2 already used four **1,850hp Kasei 22** engines. Conversion to four **2,000PS O5** engines is a little extra *nominal* output for many additional scarce engines, nacelle and spares complexity, and corrosion/water-service proof. Prefer heavy flying-boat **coastal search/convoy relay, defensible bases, fuel and radar** over competing with fighters for O5. National H6K/H8K sorties are a **separate** aircraft wallet from the Bengal 24 coastal single-engine waterplanes plus Hyūga 14 nominal.

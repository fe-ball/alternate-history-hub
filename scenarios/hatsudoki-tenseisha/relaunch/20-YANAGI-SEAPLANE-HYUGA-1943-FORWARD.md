## 0. 今回の重要な読み替え

### 「raid後の水上航空」
ここでいう価値は、日本側raid後の追撃ではない。

**敵carrier / land-based raidを受け、陸上滑走路・格納庫・燃料施設の一部が一時停止した後でも、別泊地に分散した水上航空が索敵・再接触・限定迎撃・救難・連絡を再開できること**を指す。

したがって水上航空の評価軸は:
- first-wave CAP strength
- max speed
だけでなく、
- runway independence
- dispersal by inlet / anchorage
- independent fuel / ammunition / radio
- launch / recovery equipment
- post-raid reconstitution time
- contact handoff to another base / carrier / submarine
を含む。

## 3. Enterprise capture → Yanagi OPSEC effect

Enterprise papers do not reveal HYPO worksheets or JN-25 method.

They do provide:
- evidence of unusually accurate US pre-battle intelligence
- limited "code / codebreakers" source-category hint
- an institutional reason to suspect that operational communications can expose major plans

Therefore R3 Japanese response can improve **procedure** without magically solving cryptography:

- avoid detailed exact ETA / route in diplomatic messages
- broaden rendezvous windows
- pre-brief fallback rendezvous
- shorten departure / arrival confirmation
- restrict local port movement reports
- increase submarine radio silence
- compartment cargo / mission knowledge

effect:
- later Yanagi missions are less likely to be defeated by precise foreknowledge
- they remain vulnerable to broad Allied ASW patrol, radar, HFDF, escort carrier and aircraft search

Therefore historical later sinkings are **not auto-fixed**, but neither are they automatically avoided.

## 9. "raidを受けた後でも航空戦を続ける" base doctrine

A forward base is not considered aviation-resilient merely because it has a floatplane unit.

Minimum system:
- alternate inlet / anchorage separated from main runway
- dispersed fuel drums / tanks and ammunition
- separate radio / generator
- ramps / beaching gear / dollies
- cranes / repair equipment
- small-craft rescue / recovery
- weather / tide observation
- local AA / camouflage / revetment where possible

post-raid mission priority:
1. rescue / damage report / local contact
2. re-establish reconnaissance
3. determine enemy withdrawal vector
4. hand contact to surviving land aircraft / carrier / submarine / another base
5. local CAP / limited strike only if conditions justify it

goal:
**enemy first raid should not buy several hours of uncontested blindness merely by crater-ing the runway.**

This is not immunity:
- inlet can be strafed / bombed
- weather can stop water operations
- fuel / crane / radio can be destroyed
- concentrated floatplanes are vulnerable

resilience comes from separation / distribution, not from float status alone.

## 14. later island-relief / vertical-support concept

This mission is **later than initial 1943 completion** and is triggered when Allied raids / blockade / base isolation become more serious.

R3 value is higher than historical because:
- Japanese forward bases are more completely fortified / stocked
- those bases therefore remain worth rescuing / reconstituting
- healthy Japanese main carrier force is strategically valuable and costly to tie to every local relief
- a smaller force capable of restoring aircraft / engineers / high-value stores can preserve a fortress without committing the entire carrier fleet

Hyuga can become the heavy node of a limited relief package:
- seaplane fighter / reconnaissance detachment
- aviation fuel / ammunition / engine / radio / radar spare parts
- engineers / repair specialists / signals
- Daihatsu / small-craft transfer
- limited high-priority troops
- rapid reconnaissance / local air cover during unloading

The concept is:
**small manpower, dense capability / stores, rapid reconstitution**
rather than mass infantry lift.

exact:
- troops carried
- cargo tons
- sortie / unloading time
- escort requirement
remain OPEN and must be tied to ship stability / storage / combat-aircraft complement / destination harbor conditions.

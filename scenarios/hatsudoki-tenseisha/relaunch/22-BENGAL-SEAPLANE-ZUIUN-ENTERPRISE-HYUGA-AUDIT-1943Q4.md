## 0. Findings / decisions

1. **CLOSED: E16A 瑞雲 is an independent pre-Enterprise development.** Aichi's AM-22 / 16-Shi concept, twin floats, dive-brake concept and folding arrangement pre-date Japan's R3 capture of Enterprise on 1942-06-04. Do **not** assign the E16A's invention, airframe lineage or Kinsei engine to US capture.
2. **CLOSED: Enterprise effect on 瑞雲 is limited but real at the methods/integration level.** Recoverable SBD operational and hardware evidence informs dive-brake testing, vibration investigation, bomb-release inspection, maintenance, engine-hours/part tracking and aircraft radio/EMI installation. R3 also has Japanese domestic testing and Owari-related installation/QC, which must remain separate contributors. SBD perforated-brake ideas and the E16A's historically independently evolved slotted/perforated arrangements must not be treated as exclusive proof of direct copying.
3. **CLOSED: No magical E16A speed, engine, armament or production leap from Enterprise.** The main physical constraints remain float drag/strength, dives' buffet and stability, flap reliability, catapult/recovery qualification, specialized floatplane crews and supply support. E16A's 1943 autumn small issue is PROVISIONAL, not automatically a mature 1943 air regiment.
4. **CLOSED: A6M2-N and N1K1 Kyōfū cannot be counted as routinely catapult-launchable Hyūga fighters on weight alone.** The historical Rufe was not designed for catapult launching; Kyōfū's shipboard acceleration/attachment/crane operation also needs a type-specific tested installation. The previous candidate of 6–8 Hyūga float fighters in routine immediate CAP is **withdrawn** pending structural/sea-test proof. A battleship can in sheltered water lower one to water for takeoff or ferry aircraft to a forward seaplane base, but that is not continuous combat CAP from a fast/maneuvering warship.
5. **CLOSED: Hyūga's 1943Q4 operational role remains reconnaissance, spotting, maritime patrol, forward-base restoration and high-value logistics—not a small fleet carrier.** Long-term physical ~18–22 aircraft class in [20] remains a design upper band; this audit deliberately **underloads the December assault package** until float-fighter compatibility, training and recovery are proven.
6. **CLOSED: Coastal seaplane system and Hyūga aircraft get separate ledger accounts, with the ship's complement actually subtracted from the national IJN aviation pool.** Do not double-count naval cruisers' embarked scout planes, H6K/H8K flying boats, or carrier-based aircraft.

## 1. Enterprise-to-E16A causal audit

| Technology / capability | Source and transfer pathway | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Floatplane concept, hydrodynamics, two floats, dive brakes on float struts | Pre-June-1942 domestic Aichi AM-22/E16A, progressive Japanese trials | **NOT an Enterprise invention — CLOSED** |
| Flutter/buffet/dive-brake reliability | Partial SBD remains, SBD manuals/inspection data, dive/servicing practices; Japanese E16A rig and flight tests still essential | **Process benefit plausible — CLOSED scope, OPEN quantified time saved** |
| Bomb displacement/release safety | Enterprise SBD documents/components, domestic ordnance qualification | **Technical comparison benefit — CLOSED scope** |
| Folding wing geometry | E16A own folding design predates capture; F4F-4 internal hinge/locking practice usable for structural inspection and handling comparison | **No retroactive E16A design origin; limited service-life/maintenance feedback — CLOSED** |
| Radio and electrical interference | F4F/US carrier examples of shielding, bonding, separation, filtration, chassis ground; Owari engine/installation QC and Japanese radio manufacturers | **Installation/checklist feedback 1942H2–43 — CLOSED direction, no miraculous radar** |
| Production/ready-rate | Carrier squadron maintenance logs, spares issue/checklists, new-type unit documentation | **Ready-rate support only where parts, mechanics, training exist; do NOT blindly add 3–7 points to E16A** |
| Engine and performance | Mitsubishi Kinsei development / Aichi aircraft maturation | **No Enterprise-derived engine conversion, magical performance uplift or mass production** |

**Important timing:** E16A first prototype was ready/flying in May 1942; the 1942H2 US evidence arrives **after** fundamental design. Historical acceptance is August 1943 after prolonged problems with dive-brake buffeting and stability. R3 may use captured US evidence to prioritize comparative testing and inspection, but does not claim a definite number of months saved. R3 autumn 1943 limited allocation remains a test-gated candidate. Aichi's success still needs domestic proving, revised floats/brakes, tools and pilots.

**Launch and recovery rule (CLOSED):**
- E13A/F1M have historical shipboard catapult precedents. E16A has an appropriate historical catapult concept, but ship/aircrew handling still needs 1943Q4 familiarization in this R3 timetable.
- Sea-state, loaded weight, tailwind/crosswind, catapult saddle/attachment, cradles, hangar trolley, crane and fueling are separate gates. The 5t launch envelope and 6t elevator **do not** establish structural qualification for all float fighters.
- In combat, outbound scout/spotters preferably recover at the **shore/seaplane-tender base** after the first pass. Open-sea crane recovery amid ship evasive maneuvering or artillery fire is not the default. Recover to Hyūga only after a genuinely suitable sheltered sea state and security situation.
- Do not treat two catapults as a fully cycling two-aircraft-per-minute carrier deck. Aircraft handling, arming, launching, and recovery constrain available sorties. A single operational E16A does not make the ship a strike carrier.
- The ship's support cargo, embarked Daihatsu, floatplanes and ammunition/fuel cannot each be assigned their independent theoretical full maximum simultaneously. Amphibious/cargo displacement and stowage audit remains OPEN.

**Alternative special trial branch (OPEN):** If additional wartime engineering trials prove a certified A6M2-N or N1K1 catapult variant, quantify structural reinforcements, aircraft loss, crew training and ship/shore recovery before adding it. Never imply that simply placing the fighter aboard satisfies readiness.

## 5. Combat application and limits

- British Spitfire CAP is principally fought by Ki-63 / IJN land-based Zero / carrier fighters, **not** by floatplanes.
- Water fighters can menace isolated slow reconnaissance, low-level attackers, or weakened stragglers; intermittent successes do not grant regional air supremacy.
- E13A/F1M and the limited E16A emphasize detecting attack routes, artillery corrections, convoy rescue, and restoring scouting after runway damage. They remain vulnerable to fighters and weather.
- Hyūga and Nisshin are not counted twice as aviation squadron carriers and fully loaded amphibious ships.
- The ship-heavy winter option has no automatic success; 38th Division southern pressure, British GCI/Spitfire defense, river soundings and RAF attacks remain independent campaign gates.

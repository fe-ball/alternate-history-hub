## 0. Why the original British losses are not simply multiplied

Official British history Woodburn Kirby, *The War Against Japan*, V, Appendix 31, reports 1st Arakan losses **916 KIA + 2,889 WIA + 1,252 MIA = 5,057**. Crucial source footnote says this ledger **includes 965 casualties from the 1942 monsoon and operations after 15 May 1943 through September** for reporting convenience; **5,057 − 965 = 4,092** approximates the strictly reported 23 Oct 1942–15 May 1943 operations, but the class allocation (KIA vs WIA vs missing) within 965 is not known and must not be invented. Do not compare R3 first battle 1:1 with the broad 5,057 or use it as a literal R3 list.

The frequently cited historic Japanese **611 killed, ~1,165 wounded = ~1,776 battle casualties**, Allen's official Japanese figures as summarized by the *Pacific War Online Encyclopedia*, are a separate historical estimate, not an R3 actual count.

R3 **early warning/preparation and rapid coastal movement** can shorten fruitless British Donbaik-type frontal assaults relative to actual history, *reducing* early attrition; **deeper Japanese pursuit beyond historical Maungdaw to Cox's Bazar** increases retreat/support-force losses. Most British infantry formations **escape north** (no invented multiple-division POW mass capture), but do not all retain vehicles, guns, HQ coherence or experienced cadres.

## 1. Proposed R3 casualties — ground forces ONLY; one bounded first-campaign accounting period

**Accounting period:** first Japanese coastal counterstroke through Cox capture and British retreat/line-stabilization in 1943Q1/early spring; exact calendar closure date OPEN. **Do not add summer–autumn 1943 patrol/malaria air combat losses into this same row.** Later 1943 skirmish attrition must be a second, dated wallet.

| Ground-force loss class | British/Commonwealth | Japanese Army + attached ground landing/engineer units |
|---|---:|---:|
| KIA reported on battle closure | **1,150** | **700** |
| WIA reported | **3,600** | **1,850** |
| MIA reported incl possible POW | **1,750** | **100** |
| **Total unique reported casualties** | **6,500** | **2,650** |

**PROVISIONAL plausible bands, if British retreat earlier/more orderly or envelopment nearly completes:**
- British **5,000–8,000** ground casualties; a narrow ~5,000 assumes fast successful extrication, ~8,000 assumes additional rear-guard/boat-landed flank losses but **still no wholesale divisional encirclement**.
- Japanese **1,800–3,500** ground casualties; a larger brigade-scale northward amphibious effort and exposed Cox consolidation increases own exposure to British artillery, RAF and malarial coastal transport.
- **6,500 / 2,650 center ≠ mechanically scaling 5,057 / 1,776**; the phases and objectives have been replaced rather than copied.
- British report class membership at battle closure is **not** equivalent to end-1943 permanent personnel deficit. Wounded may return; missing may later resolve as POW, dead or stragglers.

| Phase | British casualties | Physical reasoning |
|---|---:|---|
| Initial British offensive, partial frontal assaults, Japanese defense | **1,100** | Shorter/more interrupted historical-style Donbaik–Rathedaung assaults, not whole historical months of repeated failure |

Administrative parent units of 14th vs 26th Indian Division are **NOT separately fixed**: in actual history several 26th brigades were loaned to 14th and a 9-brigade conglomerate existed, so allocating casualties to both "parent division" and "tactical HQ" would double-count. Until an R3 OOB is recovered, treat **~4,000–4,800 casualties under the battle's main frontal/flanking tactical formations** and **~1,700–2,500 under relieving/rearguard/coastal support** as a **qualitative exposure split, not two fixed parent-division rolls**.

### 1.2 British MIA resolution / wounds by next winter — example survival ledger

Of the **1,750 initially reported missing**:
- **850** actually captured/POW by Japan after later reconciliation.
- **350** later confirmed dead (initially MIA, *not a second casualty event*).
- **550** rejoined / was found on Allied side after dispersal and evacuation.

Of **3,600 WIA**, use a **working 2,700 returned to service by Nov/Dec 1943** and **900 still hospitalized/unfit/transferred from front**. These return proportions must be reconciled with actual injury severities and medical capacity, not taken as automatic near-full return of the same unit.

Thus original **6,500 reported casualty incidents** resolve to a **notional ~3,250 permanently or still unavailable by next winter** = **1,150 immediately KIA + 350 later confirmed KIA + 850 POW + 900 still unavailable WIA**. Original MIA return **550** and healed WIA return **2,700** **do not get recounted as new recruits**. Replacement drafts are separately recorded and may have lower jungle training/leadership.

**Disease/non-battle removals are a SEPARATE ledger**, not folded into the 6,500: e.g. propose at peak monsoon 1943 an additional **1,000–2,000 temporary sick/unfit across the Arakan field command**, with autumn returns and anti-malaria interventions. Do not sum this peak snapshot with cumulative battle casualties as if all were permanent unique men.

### 1.3 Japanese wounds and replacement ledger

The **700 KIA, 1,850 WIA, 100 MIA** are a proposed R3 *army-ground* balance. For winter Q4:
- WIA **~1,350–1,500 return to some form of duty** after recovery; remaining **~350–500** not immediately front-ready. Whether some are sent to other theaters, hospitals and non-combat postings is separate.
- MIA final outcome OPEN: do not assume all 100 are POW or KIA.
- Rough next-winter residual **~1,100–1,300 unavailable from the first battle**, excluding disease and later skirmish casualties, before accounting for newly trained replacements.
- Japanese 55th Division's special leaders, landing-craft crews and engineers have their own finite **qualitative exhaustion and retraining cost**; complete restoration by late 1943 is not automatic, even if raw infantry strength is replenished.

## 4. Continuity bridge to 1943 Nov–Dec British 5th/7th/26th divisions

**Historical operations:** The old 14th Indian Division HQ was withdrawn and turned to training; many attached battalions and specialist crews passed to 26th and other formations; new 5th / 7th Indian Divisions arrived for the 1943Q4 Arakan line. The 14th and 26th original losses are not automatically replenished **and then counted again as brand-new 5th/7th personnel**.

The first-campaign British residual **~3,250 still unavailable from combat** gives the correct order of magnitude for drafts/foreign-theater relief; the survivors include former brigade formations with some artillery and experienced cadres but low cohesion. R3 British summer–autumn continuation can be modelled as:
1. **14th Indian HQ / shattered tactical subunits:** removed, retrained or used as cadre/draft supply; do **not** resurrect as an additional front-line full division alongside 5th, 7th and 26th.
2. **26th Indian Division and brigades:** restored rear-area command and a **defensive reserve**, but some original losses, worn equipment and disease depress early ready strength. Assign its exact Dec readiness after reinforcements and forward road/air constraints.
3. **5th and 7th Indian Divisions:** largely **distinct newly arrived formations** representing Britain's operational replacement/initiative, with Mediterranean voyages and jungle adaptation gates; their theoretical paper strengths are not evidence all rifle companies are at the front.
4. **81st West African and other formations:** separate added options, subject to India/Assam staffing and transport; do not use them to fill every British hole simultaneously.
5. **RAF 224 Group:** type changes and reinforcements replace aircraft destroyed during the first campaign, but **not** old pilot experience without retraining; R3 loss of Cox/Ramu RAF forward strips constrains fighter endurance and ground support.

**Decision effect:** Britain can plausibly *want* a Cox recapture offensive and eventually assemble a corps-sized fresh field force, yet remain unwilling to uncover Chittagong road/port defense and anti-landing reserve after the earlier coastal reversal. RAF fighter upgrade makes Japanese shipping/raid activity more costly by December, but readiness still depends on location, fuel, ground warning and serviceable aircraft.

## 5. Sensitivity checks before promoting casualty CENTRAL to CLOSED

1. **Was the British field army nearly enveloped?** If no, prefer the **5–7k** British range; 8k would require multiple badly isolated rear units and added time to capture Cox. Do not invent 5,000 POW.
2. **How early did Japan counterattack?** Earlier than historical months of Donbaik frontal assault means some British **frontal losses must decrease**, even if retreat losses rise.
3. **What actual landing lift reached the coast?** [17] middle-A a few battalions vs middle-B 5,000–10,000 men greatly alters British losses; no full invasion transport magically from Nisshin alone.
4. **How many British troops escaped with their guns?** Cox town acquisition and its port supply capability are separate from permanently captured British stock.
5. **R3 April–November 1943 patrol casualties and sick:** these are **future separate expenses**; do not fuse them with the first battle or let them vanish.
6. **Was the captured British brigade identified historically?** Treat the unnamed brigade-equivalent catastrophic heavy-kit loss as a model until actual prior-R3 OOB identity is recovered.
7. **Do later source notes reveal already-agreed losses?** In that case reconcile them by unit and date instead of adding or multiplying this proposal.

- 中国軍が四川盆地側へ戦線短縮し、日本側はここから先で敵内線 / 自軍外線の不利が増える
- 「勝っているから追う」ことで第二期をなし崩しに開始するのを避ける

## 7. US air-power condition entering summer 1943

Important distinction:
- US national aircraft / pilot production is not exhausted
- Pacific frontline combat-ready formations were badly stressed in 1942H1
- R3 **does not** have historical Guadalcanal / Santa Cruz continuous 1942H2 attrition

Therefore:
- USN carrier aviation suffers catastrophic **deck loss** in 1942H1 but preserves many surviving pilots / maintainers
- USAAF Fifth Air Force / SWPA suffers poor serviceability / long-range strain / local combat losses from loss of Port Moresby and strong Japanese interception
- SOPAC aviation is healthier than historical Guadalcanal path but tied to defense of Nouméa / Fiji / Samoa / Australia line
- by 1943, American problem is increasingly **allocation / deployment / logistics**, not absolute absence of trained manpower

This creates the political possibility of China reinforcement:
- aircraft exist
- trained personnel increasingly exist
- but combat-ready groups must be taken from another theater or given higher shipping / fuel priority

## 9. US fast-carrier learning clock

R3 qualitative timeline:
- spring 1943: still clear strategic carrier inferiority
- summer: new Essex / CVL force enters Pacific and begins to make concentrated raiding possible
- late summer / autumn: enough decks to fight locally, but crew / task-group / replenishment maturity is still being learned
- 1944 spring–early summer: first period in which US planning can expect major quantitative / operational superiority even if Japan concentrates the main carrier force

Thus 1943 summer USN central policy:
**fight, learn, threaten, but do not yet voluntarily create a decisive-battle obligation.**

The objective of summer raid pressure is not:
- immediate invasion
- guaranteed Five-Go cancellation
- automatic Japanese-carrier destruction

It is:
- strategic friction
- intelligence
- operational learning
- forcing Japanese reserves / carrier readiness
- showing China / domestic politics that the Pacific Fleet is acting

### Japan
- China ground front appears nearly static on maps but is actively preparing the finishing phase

- China requests become emergency-level
- US can reinforce China, but only by paying Hump / India / other-theater opportunity cost
- new US carrier force can now conduct meaningful raids
- US is not yet comfortable forcing a decisive carrier battle
- therefore the likely first answer is **China air reinforcement + Pacific carrier pressure**, not one giant amphibious counteroffensive

### critical convergence
The same late-August / early-September window contains:
- Five-Go Phase-II X-day preparation
- first serious US fast-carrier pressure
- Zuiryū limited readiness
- Japanese next-generation naval-aircraft transition toward later frontline availability

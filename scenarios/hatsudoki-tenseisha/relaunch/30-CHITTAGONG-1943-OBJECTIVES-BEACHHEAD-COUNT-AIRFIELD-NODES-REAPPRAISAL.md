## 1. Map geography: cut a corridor, not an entire defended theater with one explosion

- **Sitakunda–Kumira are NORTH of Chittagong, between the Bay/Sandwip Channel and the Sitakunda hill/anticline system.** The prewar Chittagong–Feni/Comilla Assam–Bengal railway traverses the area. A prewar road/trunk-route also existed; modern four-lane Dhaka–Chittagong highway engineering, modern levees, industrial embankments and visitor piers **cannot** be assumed present in 1943.
- Coastal plain contains muddy/silty tidal flats and channels, and a constrained approach east to the hill/road/rail corridor. A coast pin is not a verified 1943 high-tide Daihatsu landing and heavy-gun route; contemporary hydrographic survey and road reconnaissance are needed.
- **A single destroyed rail bridge** may stop trains temporarily, but does not imply the **parallel road** is sealed, rail irreparable, all alternative eastern routes blocked, or **Chittagong port's sea access** cut. By contrast a **defended rail+road corridor occupation** can impair British north/south switching and force diversion of reserves.
- Britain's **Cox recapture southern axis** near Dohazari faces Japanese Cox sector but there may be a *long march and transport gap* before a Japanese 38th/55th ground column is positioned to threaten Chittagong in H+72. No “synchronized southern pincer” without pre-positioned R3 troops, road readiness and travel clock.

## 3. One amphibious lodgment ≠ one defended point

For **one well-surveyed Sitakunda/Kumira-area beach system** (site NOT chosen):
1. **S — shore-logistics anchor:** physically secure boat-landing approaches, saltwater/shore AA, supply and medevac.
2. **R — road-and-rail corridor interdiction:** attain **both** relevant transport links or a verified single choke cutting both; otherwise specify *which* is broken and for how long.
3. **N — north-facing blocking security:** prevent Feni-side infantry/vehicles re-opening the corridor quickly.
4. **C — Chittagong-facing blocking security:** delay city/26th Division reaction to the bridgehead.
5. **Reserve — mobile battalion/company and command, engineer, anti-tank/AT, mortar and radio:** ability to meet threats on one side without uncovering the other.

**This is one main beachhead and four mission-functions, not four separate seaborne landing sites.** Beach positions can form one defended perimeter if distances/roads/fields of fire permit; do not invent exact lengths or km. More than one beach *within a contiguous landing complex* may be used as sub-sectors if mutually supportable and boats can transfer. A distant **independent** second shore head consumes its own security, AA, artillery, communications and seaward logistics, often weakening G2.

**Critical first-wave force check:** [29] central first wave has **1,540 infantry/crew/support total** + mixed stores and 4 possible gun packages. If these 1,540 must hold shore, R, N, C and mobile reserve simultaneously, battalion-scale infantry frontage becomes thin **even before taking casualties, sappers and unloading fatigue**. Thus **a first-wave rail-cutting party is feasible to investigate; a fully defensible two-sided G2 perimeter is NOT proven by that wave alone**. Even 2,800 troops after two ideal waves remain vulnerable to 26th/RAF until artillery/anti-tank/AA and follow-on infantry have really landed.

## 8. Additional 1943 choke-point found: **Kalurghat railway bridge** is NOT Sitakunda

**Historical evidence:** Kalurghat bridge spans **Karnaphuli** southeast/south of the Chittagong city centre and carried the **Chittagong–Dohazari railway**. It was built 1930 and train service commissioned **1931**. **1962** conversion into a permanent rail/road bridge means **not a default 1943 all-weather motor roadway**; one history says an interim WWII wartime road deck was fitted and another says road traffic 1962 onwards. Treat possible temporary wartime military motor deck as **SOURCE CONFLICT / OPEN**, require military engineer records. Sources:
- https://www.thedailystar.net/old-site/2005/02/02/d502023501128.htm (1930 rail, 1931 commission, 1962 rail-and-road conversion)
- https://www.observerbd.com/news.php?id=381656 (same)
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalurghat_Bridge (reports a temporary wartime motor deck, source needs verification).
- https://www.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/zmj89nzyv1 (road motor vehicle use begins 1962 in account).

**Three different 1943 British transport wallets around Chittagong:**
1. **North rail+trunk road corridor** via Sitakunda/Kumira → Feni/Comilla: movement of reserves, supplies, strategic withdrawal toward Bengal.
2. **South railway** via Karnaphuli/Kalurghat → Dohazari, with separate southbound road/ferries/river approaches: army supply for the former Cox front (in R3 British forward line is north of Japanese-held Cox).
3. **Seaward Chittagong port / coastal craft**, plus airfield runway/fuel nodes: persists even if a north rail track temporarily fails; closure needs actual maritime/air interdiction.

**Consequence for objectives:** Northern amphibious G2 closure may seriously impede reinforcements but **cannot by itself physically cut the southern army's railway to Dohazari** or close the port. A Japanese south-front movement threatening Kalurghat is a **distinct task** and may take many days from the Cox sector; its feasibility cannot be handed as a free H+12 effect. If the Japanese desire true multi-axis isolation, require multiple independent effects with real troop/naval-air resources, *not* necessarily multiple amphibious beachheads.

**First design implication:** Rather than immediately multiply landing beaches, select a **primary communication function** for the amphibious force (north line), a **separate physically costed ground-front pressure mission** against the south line, and a **separate naval/air-interdiction burden** on port/coastal supply. Only their interaction may make British XV Corps collapse plausible. If the ground line is too far away, downgrade expectation to delaying British plans without forcing corps rout.

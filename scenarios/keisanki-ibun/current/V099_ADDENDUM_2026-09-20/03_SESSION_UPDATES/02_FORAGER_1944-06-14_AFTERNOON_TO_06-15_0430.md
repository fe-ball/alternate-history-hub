# FORAGER execution — 14 Jun afternoon to 15 Jun 04:30
Status: DERIVED / WORKING EVENT LINE

## 14 Jun afternoon — Oldendorf close bombardment
U.S. old battleships, cruisers and destroyers move into more methodical close fire-support work.

Branch Japanese behavior:
- do not answer with every coast gun;
- short firing windows from selected batteries;
- reverse-slope / alternate positions preserve mortars, field artillery, reserves and tank waiting areas;
- distributed ammunition and C2 reduce single-point collapse;
- road blockage, access loss and telephone cuts remain serious.

Working result:
- visible/known coast and AA positions take heavy mission-kill and meaningful permanent loss;
- masked/reverse-slope/cave positions take much lower permanent loss;
- additional Japanese military/associated casualties: **300–470 class**;
- 9th Tank Regiment: **0–1 irreversible**, **1–2 temporary mission-kill** additional;
- exposed logistics and road network suffer materially;
- evening island-wide ground combat value: roughly **75–84% class**;
- beach-front direct-fire capacity is degraded substantially more than inland defense depth.

U.S. BDA:
- materially overstates permanent neutralization;
- many silenced or access-cut positions are scored as destroyed.

Surface exchange:
- California receives local/moderate coast-fire damage but remains in campaign;
- Tennessee light-to-moderate damage class and remains in support;
- one destroyer light/moderate local damage class;
- no U.S. major campaign-out.

- invasion of the Marianas becomes CONFIRMED;

- one meaningful torpedo attack is made against the invasion/transport system;

- local U.S. convoy maneuver/reordering delay occurs;
- no U.S. major hull or transport loss;
- no confirmed Japanese submarine loss in this night window.

Assessment:
- tactical ship-kill result favors U.S. ASW/escort;
- information result favors Japanese screen;
- U.S. escort effort is slightly diverted into additional ASW sweeps, but not enough to derail the landing.

# 1. Three mandatory personnel-loss measures

## C-GROSS — gross physical battle casualties

Includes:
- KIA;
- DOW;
- MIA/POW where applicable;
- severe WIA;
- moderate WIA;
- light WIA.

This is the ordinary broad casualty-roll measure.

Current working Saipan U.S. total:
**~15,000–18,000**

Center:
**~16,000–17,000**

This number remains usable only when explicitly labeled C-GROSS.

---

## C-OUT — campaign-out loss

Personnel who, after being hit, do not return to useful Saipan combat duty before organized resistance ends.

Includes:
- all dead/presumed dead;
- severe surviving WIA;
- evacuated moderate WIA whose RTD clock exceeds the remaining campaign;
- persistent neuropsychiatric/neurological cases;
- selected MIA/POW.

Excludes:
- light WIA returning within hours/days;
- short concussion/exhaustion returnees;
- men temporarily held out but back before the battle closes.

Working Saipan total:
**~9,000–11,500**

Center:
**~10,000–10,700**

This is the best measure for:
> “how many U.S. personnel did Saipan remove from the current operation?”

It is still broader than the user's “ぶっ殺し数評価.”

---

## C-KILL — 「米兵ぶっ殺し数評価」 / near-permanent manpower destruction

Operational interpretation:

Count one-for-one:
- KIA;
- DOW;
- MIA/presumed dead;
- surviving severe wounds that remove the man from useful combat service for many months / effectively for the relevant operational horizon.

Do **not** count one-for-one:
- moderate multi-week WIA;
- light WIA;
- short-term combat exhaustion;
- temporary concussion/hearing impairment;
unless they cross the long-duration/no-realistic-return threshold.

This is **not literal death count**.
It is:
> **death + near-permanent destruction of combat manpower.**

Working Saipan total:
**~5,300–7,000**

Center:
**~6,000–6,400**

This is the number that should be used when asking:
> “How many U.S. soldiers has Japan effectively ‘killed’ as durable manpower, rather than merely wounded temporarily?”

# 3. Why C-KILL is much lower than C-GROSS

File 69 already separates:
- dead;
- severe WIA/no return this campaign;
- moderate WIA;
- light WIA;
- neuropsychiatric temporary loss.

In its 20-Jun model, dead + severe-no-return is only roughly the mid-30-percent class of gross physical casualties.

The exact ratio varies by event:
- close assault / overrun creates a higher severe/fatal share;
- dispersed artillery/harassment creates more returnable wounded;
- specialist losses can matter more than headcount.

Therefore the final campaign C-KILL should not be generated by applying one universal casualty multiplier, but a broad **~35–40% of C-GROSS** is a reasonable campaign-scale check.

That gives the selected **~5.3–7.0k** class.

# 7. Do not confuse “current unfit” with either cumulative metric

At a checkpoint:

### Current unfit
How many living U.S. personnel are unavailable **right now**.

This includes temporary cases and therefore can be large.

It is a stock.

### C-GROSS
All physical battle casualties accumulated so far.

It is a cumulative flow.

### C-OUT
Those casualties who will not return before Saipan closes.

It is a cumulative operational removal measure.

### C-KILL
Dead + near-permanent/long-duration combat-manpower destruction.

It is the user's durable-loss / “ぶっ殺し数評価” measure.

These four figures must never be substituted for one another.

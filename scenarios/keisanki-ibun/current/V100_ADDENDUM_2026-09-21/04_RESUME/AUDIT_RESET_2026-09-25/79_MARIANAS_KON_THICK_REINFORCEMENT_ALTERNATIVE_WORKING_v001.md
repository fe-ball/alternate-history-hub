## 1. Finding

Yes, there is a credible concern that the current selected line is **under-lifted relative to the scale of fleet commitment**.

This is NOT a sunk-cost argument.

The correct ex-ante question is:
once A-Go / Marianas is identified as the decisive theater, should Army reinforcement / amphibious lift have been mobilized in parallel with the carrier / battle-fleet movement?

If the answer is yes, the relevant alternative must be planned around **13–17 Jun**, not improvised at 21:30 on 20 Jun.

## 2. Historical precedent — Operation KON

Historical June 1944 Operation KON against Biak already demonstrates the Japanese institutional concept.

Known configurations included:
- Aoba + Kinu + destroyers / minelayers embarking roughly **2,300 troops class**;
- a separate T.127 / minelayer movement attempting another **~800 troops**;
- distant cover by Fuso / Myoko / Haguro and destroyers;
- later Yamato / Musashi / Noshiro / destroyers allocated toward the Biak relief system.

The KON force was interrupted / displaced by the Marianas crisis.

Therefore a Branch alternative does not need to invent:
- cruiser troop transport;
- minelayer / landing-ship troop transport;
- heavy-battleship cover for island reinforcement;
- Army/Navy joint reinforcement planning.

It only needs to ask whether the already mobilized KON-type lift should be **redirected north toward the Marianas** instead of dissolved back into fleet roles.

## 3. Transit feasibility — broad physical check

Approximate great-circle distances to Saipan:

- Batjan -> Saipan: ~1,440 nmi
- Davao -> Saipan: ~1,280 nmi
- Sorong -> Saipan: ~1,290 nmi
- Palau -> Saipan: ~806 nmi
- Yap -> Saipan: ~563 nmi

At 20 kt class:
- Batjan/Sorong -> Saipan ~64–72 h;
- Palau ~40 h;
- Yap ~28 h.

Therefore a decision around 13–14 Jun can physically place a fast transport / cruiser-transport echelon in Marianas staging geometry before 20/21 Jun.

This does not prove every named hull / troop unit is available.
It proves the timing is not intrinsically impossible.

A named-hull / unit / fuel audit is still required.

## 4. What “thicker” should mean

Do NOT make the operation thicker by sending:
- one giant slow convoy;
- a division-sized counterlanding directly into U.S.-held beaches;
- every available tender;
- battleships loaded with troops at the expense of battle function.

Make it thicker by creating **multiple fast lift nodes**.

Goal:
if one transport element is lost / turned away, the entire reinforcement does not collapse.

## 8. Ground employment

Fresh arrivals should not be ordered into an immediate mass night charge.

First 6–12 h priorities:
- secure receiving route;
- replace exhausted signals / artillery / engineer personnel;
- rebuild a mobile reserve;
- take over selected defensive sectors;
- free surviving veteran garrison cells for counterattack / infiltration.

Then, if the ground state still supports it:
- 21 Jun bounded counteroffensive around Aslito / central approaches;
- objective = push U.S. support / observation back and prolong airfield denial;
- not retake the entire invasion beachhead.

This turns fresh blood into **depth and reserve**, not immediate attritional waste.

## 11. Risk diversification

The purpose is not to “hide” risk.

It is to stop one Nisshin hit from collapsing the operational payoff.

Use:
- two or more transport axes / groups;
- cross-loaded infantry / specialists / ammunition;
- separate receiving points;
- separate abort decisions.

If one group is destroyed / turns back:
another can still create ground value.

This is the real meaning of making the bet “thicker.”

---

## 12. Why not a full slow convoy

Historical Guadalcanal shows the upper failure mode:
a large multi-transport convoy carrying many thousands of troops can be devastated by air attack even with substantial naval cover.

In the Marianas:
- U.S. radar is better;
- U.S. carrier / CVE air remains much stronger;
- daylight search is deadly;
- unloading time is long.

Therefore:
**5,000–10,000+ men in slow freighters / transports is not the selected thick-bet concept.**

It would force the fleet to hold sea space too long and likely cross into daylight.

That increases core-fleet risk faster than delivered combat value.

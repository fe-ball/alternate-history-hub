# 0. Historical hard calibration

On 13 Jun:
- Lee's seven fast battleships and destroyers detached for the first scheduled bombardment of Saipan and Tinian;
- firing was from long range because unswept shoal/mine risk constrained approach;
- the fast battleship force was optimized/trained for fleet action, not precision shore bombardment;
- contemporary/retrospective assessments judged much of the Tinian fire ineffective or misdirected against soft targets rather than concealed gun positions.

However:
Iowa's action report records:
- numerous fires around Ushi Point airfields from 16-inch and 5-inch fire;
- one 16-inch salvo detonating an ammunition dump.

Therefore:
> **do not model the bombardment as harmless.**
>
> **Do not model it as systematic heavy-battery destruction either.**

# 2. U.S. target knowledge and allocation

By 13 Jun the U.S. has:
- prewar/intelligence target maps;
- two days of carrier-air observation;
- AA muzzle-flash / firing-location clues;
- airfield and major installation coordinates.

But:
- exact concealed apertures / alternate OPs remain imperfectly known;
- some positions are dummy / empty / alternate;
- fast-BB spotters are not specialized shore-fire experts.

Selected Tinian target priority:

1. Ushi Point airfield / northern military complex;
2. known/suspected AA concentrations;
3. visible coastal positions;
4. ammunition / buildings / roads;
5. opportunity targets from spotter reports.

Do not assume the whole battleship group concentrates exclusively on Tinian.
Saipan remains the principal invasion objective.

# 3. Airfield / soft-installation effect

16-inch HC + 5-inch fire is highly destructive where it lands.

Selected:

## Ushi airfield complex
- new runway / taxi cratering;
- hangar / building fires;
- vehicles / exposed stores destroyed;
- power / servicing interruptions;
- local communications cut.

Operational effect:
- **major daytime sortie-generation disruption**
- principal surface:
  **4–8 h severe interruption class**
- alternate/diversion use remains possible at lower throughput.

This is stronger than 11-Jun F6F strafing and comparable to / additive with 12-Jun bombing.

---

# 4. Ammunition / logistics effect

Historical calibration includes an Ushi-area ammunition-dump explosion.

Branch distribution reduces the consequence of one dump loss.

Selected:
- **one local ammunition / ordnance node loss-class event is credible**
- secondary fires / cook-off possible;
- no island-wide ammunition catastrophe.

Effect:
- local AA / airfield ordnance supply disrupted;
- resupply routes activated;
- distributed stores prevent total paralysis.

# 5. AA effect

## 25-mm
Visible/light positions are vulnerable to 5-inch / fragments.

Selected:
- **2–5 additional mount positions destroyed / long-out**
- **3–6 suppressed / crew-degraded**

## 75-mm
- **0–1 gun direct/near-direct long-out center**
- battery command / wire can be disrupted
- several-hour suppression plausible.

## 120-mm DP
- **0–1 gun permanent/long-out center**
- **1–3 positions temporary suppression / comm/access damage**

Heavy-gun crew shelters reduce personnel loss from near misses.

# 6. Fixed 140-mm / 6-inch coastal batteries

This is the key correction.

Fast-battleship long-range bombardment suffers from:
- target-identification uncertainty;
- shallow trajectory;
- unswept-water stand-off;
- spotter limitations;
- concealed/revetted batteries.

Branch adds:
- stronger overhead / splinter protection for crew/support;
- distributed ammunition;
- alternate OP;
- dummy positions;
- protected communications.

Therefore selected:

## 140-mm groups
Permanent gun destruction:
**0–2 guns island-wide center band**

Additional:
- **2–5 guns/positions suppressed or communications/access impaired for hours**

No whole-group annihilation center.

## Tinian Town 6-inch
Not a priority Saipan-support target on this day.
Permanent loss:
**0 center**

It benefits strongly from concealment/cave geometry.

---

# 7. Mobile 75-mm artillery / candidate positions

Large-area shelling can:
- collapse exposed field positions;
- cut roads;
- damage ready ammunition;
- kill crews.

But Branch candidate-position network matters.

Selected:
- current visible positions suffer;
- some prepared empty/dummy sites absorb fire;
- surviving guns move at night to C2/C3 alternates.

Permanent mobile-gun loss:
**1–3 guns class**
across the island from this bombardment is a reasonable center band.

More important:
- several batteries lose hours to movement / wire / ammunition disruption.

Do not erase the mobile-artillery network.

---

# 8. Radar / C2

Large shelling can break:
- exposed antennas;
- wire;
- generators;
- local command buildings.

Selected:
- one local warning / communication path heavily disrupted;
- primary island warning picture becomes intermittent;
- alternate visual / radio / wire routes prevent total blindness.

Operational:
**C2 efficiency AMBER for ~4–10 h**
with partial night restoration.

---

# 9. Japanese return fire

The fast battleships remain at long range / outside the easiest effective coastal-gun envelope.

Historical calibration:
Japanese return fire was ineffective against the fast battleship group.

Branch:
- better precomputed fire data;
- better observation / target-board discipline;
- Tinian 140-mm range can threaten some support/screen geometry;
- but major BBs at long stand-off remain difficult targets.

Selected:
- Japanese batteries fire bounded harassment / ranging where arc permits;
- no fast-battleship hit center;
- **DD/screen near-miss / maneuver tax** is plausible;
- firing reveals positions and invites counterbattery.

Therefore:
do not award a spectacular Japanese anti-BB result on 13 Jun.

# 11. Operational result

The 13-Jun fast-battleship bombardment is a U.S. success against:
- buildings;
- exposed stores;
- airfield surfaces;
- visible AA;
- roads / communications;
- morale / work tempo.

It is only a partial success against:
- protected heavy batteries;
- protected ammunition network;
- prepared alternate artillery positions.

Thus the proper Branch result is:

> **Tinian's aviation output is pushed down another step, while its cross-channel and anti-ship artillery system remains dangerous enough to require continued suppression on 14–15 Jun.**

This explains why the U.S. cannot simply stop allocating air/naval fire to Tinian after 13 Jun.

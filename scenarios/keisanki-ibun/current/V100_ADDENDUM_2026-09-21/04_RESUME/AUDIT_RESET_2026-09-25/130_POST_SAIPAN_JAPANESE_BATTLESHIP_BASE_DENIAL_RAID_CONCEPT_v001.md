replace the implausibly expensive idea of a mass Bonin-to-Saipan counterinvasion with a more credible Japanese operational response:
a short, heavily preplanned battleship bombardment intended to delay Saipan base conversion and force U.S. naval cover to remain concentrated.

---

# 0. Core judgment

After Saipan falls, Japan does not need to retake the island to obtain strategic value.

A more efficient mission is:

> **enter briefly, bombard fixed high-value U.S. base functions, and withdraw before TF58/Lee can convert contact into a decisive fleet battle.**

Success condition:
- Aslito sortie generation delayed;
- fuel/ordnance/maintenance disrupted;
- Tanapag/shore throughput disrupted;
- LVT/vehicle/engineer concentrations damaged;
- radar/AA/communications forced to relocate/repair;
- U.S. ground formations kept on security alert;
- TF58/BB/DD remain tied to Marianas cover.

Territorial gain is unnecessary.

---

# 1. Why fixed-base bombardment fits the surviving Japanese system

Japan retains:
- prewar / wartime Japanese maps of Saipan;
- detailed knowledge of Aslito and Japanese-built infrastructure;
- continuing Tinian observation;
- local air/water-air reconnaissance;
- Type 100 reconnaissance aircraft / photo interpretation capability in the wider system;
- previously built common-grid and fire-data practice in the Branch.

Therefore the target problem is much easier than:
- hunting mobile U.S. carriers;
- placing thousands of troops ashore under opposition.

Primary targets can be preassigned as area boxes.

---

# 2. Target priority

## A — Aslito air-base functions
Priority:
1. fuel storage / fuel handling;
2. ordnance / ready ammunition;
3. maintenance / repair shops;
4. parked aircraft / dispersal;
5. runway/taxiway chokepoints;
6. fighter-direction / communications / power;
7. engineering plant.

Do not waste the whole heavy battery on runway surface alone.
Runways are repairable.
Fuel, aircraft, skilled ground crews, repair plant and ordnance have longer functional recovery tails.

## B — Tanapag / unloading and repair system
- landing craft concentration;
- piers / lighterage;
- beach-control;
- vehicle parks;
- ammunition/fuel transfer;
- repair/salvage.

## C — artillery / FDC / radar / AA
Use only fresh or pre-registered boxes.
Do not assume precise night destruction of mobile batteries.

## D — troop concentrations
Lower priority unless:
- photographed;
- fixed;
- dense;
- obviously preparing the next assault.

# 3. Ammunition doctrine

Use correct functional mix.

## Type 0 Common / HE
**PRIMARY BASE-DESTRUCTION ROUND**

Best for:
- buildings;
- revetments;
- AA positions;
- vehicle/engineering parks;
- runway/taxiway cratering;
- exposed logistics;
- general blast/fragmentation.

## Type 3 Common incendiary-shrapnel
**SELECTIVE AREA / FIRE ROUND**

Best for:
- aircraft parks;
- fuel/flammable handling;
- tents / timber structures;
- trucks;
- exposed ammunition handling;
- repair yards;
- soft dense support areas.

Do not credit Type 3 with magical hardened-target destruction.

## Type 1 AP
**RESERVE / HARD-TARGET / SURFACE-BATTLE ROUND**

Use selectively for:
- hardened concrete;
- heavy revetment / bunker;
- deep structural targets where delayed penetration is useful.

Keep a substantial AP-ready reserve because:
- Lee;
- U.S. CA/DD;
- other battleship contacts
may force the mission to transition instantly from bombardment to fleet combat.

Do not center indiscriminate Type-1 expenditure on soft base targets.

# 4. Firing architecture

Do not bring every Japanese battleship into one close bombardment box.

Preferred layered architecture:

### Raid / bombardment layer
Fast or tactically suitable BB group:
- 35.6-cm / 41-cm class provides sustained fixed-box fires;
- cruisers and secondaries add HE volume if geometry safely closes.

### Heavy overwatch
Yamato/Musashi or other elite heavy units:
- short high-value 46-cm pulse;
- anti-surface reserve;
- do not remain close merely to add shell count.

### Screen
Modern DD margin is scarce.
Use enough:
- forward contact;
- torpedo counter-interdiction;
- ASW;
- damaged-ship assistance;
- withdrawal screen.

Do not repeat the earlier error of spending high-grade DDs as cargo hulls.

# 5. Duration

This is a raid.

Center:
**~30–60 min heavy-fire window**
depending:
- surprise;
- U.S. radar contact;
- Lee position;
- local air state;
- submarine warning;
- target freshness.

A longer 60–90 min event is an upper-tail opportunity, not the default.

Break-off criterion:
- U.S. battle line / carrier strike geometry becomes favorable;
- target set is already serviced;
- Japanese screen starts paying unacceptable damage;
- daylight recovery/search clock closes.

# 6. Historical calibration

The October 1942 Kongō/Haruna bombardment of Henderson Field demonstrates that two battleships firing nearly 1,000 14-inch shells can severely degrade a concentrated airfield system.

Saipan in July 1944 is harder:
- stronger U.S. radar;
- more ships;
- more artillery;
- better repair;
- deeper logistics.

But Branch advantages for Japan:
- U.S. TF58 is materially damaged;
- Japan already demonstrated successful fleet-created access windows;
- Tinian remains a nearby ISR/support node;
- Aslito is a fixed, expanding and highly valuable target;
- Japan knows the preexisting base geography.

Therefore:
**a repeat-Henderson effect is not assumed, but a multi-day base-function delay is credible if the raid obtains surprise/limited interference.**

---

# 7. Expected effect bands

Do not center total destruction of Aslito.

A successful raid may produce:

- local Aslito sortie-generation loss:
  **~12–36 h severe disruption**
- selected fuel/ordnance/maintenance functions:
  **~2–5 day repair/replenishment tail**
- Tanapag / landing-craft / engineering throughput:
  **~12–48 h major friction**
- local radar/communications/AA:
  **hours to ~1–2 days**, depending redundancy
- U.S. casualties:
  significant but secondary to functional damage.

Upper tail:
a fuel/ordnance fire or dense parked-aircraft hit can extend individual functional deficits beyond these windows.

These are working operational bands, not shell-by-shell calculations.

---

# 8. Strategic value

The raid is useful even if:
- Saipan remains U.S.;
- Aslito reopens quickly;
- no U.S. capital ship is sunk.

Because it forces:
- TF58 to remain nearby;
- Lee / BB cover to remain available;
- DD/search/ASW effort to remain dense;
- Tinian invasion preparations to compete with base defense;
- U.S. repair/engineering stock to be consumed again.

Thus:
> **bombardment threat can generate strategic delay without a territorial objective.**

---

# 9. Comparison with Bonin mass counterinvasion

## Mass counterinvasion
- huge troop lift;
- opposed landing;
- repeated convoy exposure;
- high escort requirement;
- very low strategic efficiency unless surprise is exceptional.

## Battleship denial raid
- uses surviving fleet power directly;
- targets fixed high-value functions;
- needs only a short access window;
- no need to sustain a beachhead;
- can withdraw after one mission cycle.

Therefore:
**battleship base-denial raid is the much stronger Japanese center option.**

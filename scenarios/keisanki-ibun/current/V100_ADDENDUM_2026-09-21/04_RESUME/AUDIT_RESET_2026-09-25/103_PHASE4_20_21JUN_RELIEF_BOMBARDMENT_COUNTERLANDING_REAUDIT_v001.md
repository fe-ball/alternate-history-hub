# 10. OCL center decision — NO-GO before the receiving cycle

A large/medium true counterlanding is **not selected** in the center.

Reason:

The same large local-craft system is needed almost immediately for:
- D2/D3 DD ship-to-boat transfer;
- Nisshin heavy/bulk transfer;
- multiple receiving lanes;
- breakdown replacement;
- casualty handling;
- Tinian / lateral tasks.

The bombardment / ground exploitation already produces:
- local U.S. line distortion;
- artillery/FDC recovery delay;
- reserve fixation.

Using heavy craft to put several hundred Japanese veterans behind the U.S. line would risk:
- losing craft to U.S. radar/naval/landing-craft fire;
- delaying their return to receiving stations;
- reducing Nisshin useful-ashore cargo;
- complicating friendly-fire/no-fire geometry;
- creating an isolated force with uncertain land link-up.

### Operational judgement
On this night:
**one additional 100–300-man coastal attack is less valuable than preserving the craft throughput that lands ~hundreds of reinforcements plus several hundred tons of ammunition/engineering/signal/repair cargo.**

Therefore:
- **large OCL-1 = NO-GO center**
- **OCL-2 contested-shore = NO-GO**
- **OCL-3 Tinian strike = NO-GO center**

This is an explicit decision, not a missing doctrine.

# 0. Core finding

Japanese target knowledge must be split into four layers:

## K0 — permanent terrain / former Japanese infrastructure
Examples:
- roads;
- narrow-gauge rail;
- villages;
- airfield geometry;
- harbor;
- sugar facilities;
- ridges/ravines;
- bridges/defiles;
- former Japanese dumps/buildings.

Confidence:
**VERY HIGH**
throughout the campaign.

These are home-ground features.

## K1 — persistent U.S. functional zones
Examples:
- artillery area;
- vehicle park;
- Aslito support zone;
- road-control / traffic concentration;
- beach/unloading zone;
- large aid/collecting zone.

Confidence:
**HIGH when repeatedly observed**
but exact contents can move.

## K2 — current mobile tactical positions
Examples:
- battalion boundary;
- current reserve assembly;
- exact battery location;
- exact CP/FDC;
- tank concentration.

Confidence:
**MEDIUM/HIGH only while fresh.**
Can become stale within hours.

## K3 — transient opportunity
Examples:
- relief/handoff seam;
- road congestion caused by U1/U2;
- temporary artillery displacement;
- reserve moving through a junction;
- exact local gap.

Confidence:
**must be confirmed near H-hour.**

The final operation should use:
- K0/K1 for plan architecture;
- K2 for target refinement;
- K3 only after late confirmation.

# 1. Pre-invasion knowledge — 14/15 Jun

Japanese command knows Saipan's physical terrain extremely well.

Available:
- own road and rail system;
- Aslito runways/taxiways/facilities;
- Tanapag harbor and Flores Point;
- Garapan;
- Chalan Kanoa;
- sugar-field tracks;
- central ridges/ravines;
- prepared firing/observation positions;
- beach exits;
- likely U.S. inland routes.

Pre-invasion fire planning already includes:
- landing beaches;
- beach exits;
- road junctions;
- likely artillery positions;
- tank assembly lanes;
- Aslito approaches.

What it does **not** know:
- actual U.S. post-landing artillery sites;
- actual CP locations;
- actual division boundaries after combat begins;
- future base-support layout.

Thus:
**terrain picture = excellent**
**enemy-layout picture = predictive, not current.**

# 4. “Knowing the U.S. base layout” at Aslito

Do not describe this as a binary known/unknown.

Japan originally built and operated Aslito.
Therefore it knows:
- original runways;
- taxiways;
- drainage;
- former buildings;
- access roads;
- terrain masking;
- likely service areas.

As U.S. engineering changes the field, Japan can observe:
- new mat/repair activity;
- traffic;
- parked-aircraft concentrations when visible;
- smoke/fuel/ammunition activity;
- AA firing;
- night lighting/blackout patterns;
- road congestion.

What becomes uncertain:
- exact new dispersal/revetment use;
- exact U.S. maintenance layout;
- small fuel/ammo caches;
- exact operational command location.

Thus on 26–30 Jun:
> **Japan understands the field's functional anatomy very well, but not every current organ.**

This is sufficient for:
- area interdiction;
- route interdiction;
- work interruption;
not precision destruction of an unseen headquarters.

# 5. 27–30 Jun — U1 makes the target picture temporarily richer

A reinforcement/relief cycle creates observable signatures:

- landing-craft/transport activity;
- extra trucks;
- route-control posts;
- traffic pauses;
- unusual radio activity;
- battalion-sized assembly;
- outgoing old-unit traffic;
- medical/resupply movement.

The Branch observation system does not need to identify:
> “this is 1/22 Marines.”

It only needs to conclude:
> “a fresh battalion-sized packet is moving through route X toward sector Y and an old formation is trying to leave.”

That is a much more realistic intelligence claim.

Target confidence around the active handoff:
- route: **HIGH**
- staging box: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
- handoff sector: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
- exact battalion frontage: **MEDIUM**
- exact CP/FDC: **MEDIUM/LOW unless confirmed by flash/radio/patrol**
- unit identity: **LOW / irrelevant**

Retire stale targets.

## H-6 to H-2
No broad remapping.

Use only:
- local patrol;
- flash/sound;

- clear seam closure;

- new major traffic surge.

## H-hour onward
Use mission-type command:
- main axis follows route/terrain objective;
- exploitation teams attack the first confirmed V2 function encountered;
- do not require a pre-known exact headquarters.

# 11. Target certainty scale for the final plan

Use:

### A — fixed / virtually certain
- road;
- rail;
- field;
- harbor;
- village;
- ridge;
- known former facility.

### B — persistent functional area
- artillery belt;
- support park;
- route-control/staging zone.
Likely current but internal layout uncertain.

### C — fresh mobile target
- battery;
- reserve concentration;
- handoff.
Must be <~12 h old or reconfirmed.

### D — inferred / stale
- yesterday's CP;
- yesterday's tank park.
Do not allocate scarce pre-H fire except as a broad harassment box.

Final operation should allocate scarce ammunition primarily to:
**A/B + freshly confirmed C.**

It improves:
- timestamp discipline;
- old/current/stale labels;
- correlation of patrol/air/flash/sound reports;
- common grid conversion;

- distribution of one current target picture to Army/Navy elements.

This is particularly valuable late in the campaign because observation is scarce.

When only a few fresh reports arrive, the Branch is better at preserving:
> which report is current enough to act on.

# 14. Operational implication

The Branch final operation should not be written as:
> Japanese intelligence knows the exact U.S. base deployment and attacks all key nodes simultaneously.

It should be:
> Japanese command knows the terrain and broad functional layout extremely well, has several recently current target boxes, identifies the most likely relief seam from traffic, and sends mission-oriented assault packets toward functions rather than named units.

This makes V2 reach plausible without magic intelligence.

# 15. Historical guard

Historical U.S. records show that by 1–4 Jul:
- Garapan/Tanapag and the central heights were being taken;
- Aslito was already supporting shore-based aircraft;
- the U.S. front and support geometry was moving rapidly;
- the final Japanese attack nevertheless found and exploited an exposed Tanapag/105th Infantry geometry and drove into artillery/support areas.

Thus even the historical Japanese force, with much poorer late C2, was able to exploit a real weak geometry.

The Branch does not need omniscience.
It needs:
- better freshness discipline;
- better local reconnaissance;
- better synchronization;
- a plan that recognizes what can and cannot be known.

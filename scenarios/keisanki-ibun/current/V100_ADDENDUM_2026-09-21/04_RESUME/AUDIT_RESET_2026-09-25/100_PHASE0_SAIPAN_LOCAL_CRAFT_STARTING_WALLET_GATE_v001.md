# 1. Historical observed operational anchor

Historical Saipan did not merely possess barges in inventory.

Around 18 Jun, Japanese forces actually:
- loaded troops into roughly **35 barges / landing craft**;
- launched an amphibious counterattack from the Tanapag/Garapan side;
- lost **13 craft** to U.S. armed landing craft / amphibious fire;
- the remainder turned back.

This proves a real operational fact:

> after pre-invasion carrier/naval attack and several days of battle, historical Saipan could still assemble roughly 35 troop-carrying powered craft for one offensive mission.

This is stronger evidence than a paper establishment.

---

# 2. Type-mix warning

Do **not** translate:
**35 troop-carrying barges**

directly into:
**35 x 14-m Daihatsu-equivalent large ramped craft**.

Known:
- Daihatsu were among the craft involved / present;
- Saipan had local barges, launches and landing craft;
- underwater remains include Daihatsu examples.

Unknown:
- exact 35-craft type mix;
- large vs small powered craft;
- load per craft;
- serviceability distribution;
- whether all 35 came from one local pool or a mixed naval/army harbor pool.

Therefore file 83's use of the historical 35 as a direct large-craft anchor is too strong.

   - Shohatsu-equivalent and similar;

3. **motor launches / harbor / liaison craft**
   - personnel / messenger / casualty / light stores;

4. **special / amphibious vehicles**
   - Ka-Mi etc. separate from boat carrying capacity.

For each:
- physical;
- serviceable;
- crewed;
- fueled;
- MR;
- location;
- repair;
- dated loss;
- dated mission.

Do not force named hull identity if the sources cannot support it; unique-pool accounting is sufficient.

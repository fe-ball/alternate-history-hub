# 7. Interaction between U.S. reinforcement and Japanese final attack

This produces a new important feedback.

A stronger Japanese defense does not necessarily delay the final attack monotonically.

If the U.S. releases 1/22:
- Japanese command can observe a fresh reserve entering the system;
- waiting gives the U.S. time to relieve/reconstitute exhausted battalions;
- U-R improves faster after 12–24h;
- relieved U.S. units begin a 36–72h reconstitution cycle.

Therefore Japanese incentive becomes:
> **attack before fresh U.S. reserve fully converts into restored rotation.**

This can pull T-FA **earlier** relative to a simple “stronger Japan survives longer” model.

At the same time:
- stronger Japanese C2/infantry/fire system allows waiting somewhat longer without immediate disintegration.

These effects oppose each other.

### First-pass result
**Final-operation date remains in a broad early-July-class window, with only modest movement likely.**

Do not center a mid/late-July final attack.

# 9. Final-operation geometry — likely major change

The main change should be **target value**, not simply attack width.

Preferred Japanese geometry:

1. use night/infiltration routes to place a narrow main effort;
2. use secondary axes to fix U.S. battalions;
3. mortar/artillery fire suppresses known reserve/artillery movement;
4. local aviation/Tinian reconnaissance attacks or distracts rear response;
5. penetrate toward:
   - artillery/FDC;
   - reserve assembly;
   - armor/vehicle parks;
   - road intersections;
   - Aslito support/repair/control functions.

Once Japanese troops enter deep enough:
- U.S. artillery experiences local friendly-fire restrictions;
- rear security units are forced into close combat;
- reserve movement becomes less efficient.

### Consequence
A 1–2 km penetration into the **right rear-support geometry** can matter more than a broader 2–3 km advance into tactically empty ground.

Old 0.8–1.5 km center remains reopened.

# 10. T-END — why stronger Japan may not survive much longer after T-FA

This is important.

A stronger final operation consumes:
- the remaining coherent infantry;
- NCO/officer skeleton;
- mortar/MG ammunition;
- route connectivity;
- local mobile reserve.

If the attack fails strategically, it can accelerate the transition:
> coherent defense -> isolated cave/ravine cells.

Thus stronger Japanese combat power can simultaneously produce:
- a more damaging final attack;
- **only a modest extension, or even little extension, in organized-resistance end date after that attack.**

This is why:
> stronger defense != automatically late-July organized resistance.

Late-July formation-scale resistance requires the final attack to be deferred/avoided AND logistics/C2 to remain connected, which is a different branch.

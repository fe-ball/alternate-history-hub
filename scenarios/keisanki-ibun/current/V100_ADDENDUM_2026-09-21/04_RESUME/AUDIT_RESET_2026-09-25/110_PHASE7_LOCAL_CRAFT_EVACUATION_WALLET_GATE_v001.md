# 0. Core finding

The late campaign must use one finite boat system for four successive roles:

1. **inbound relief / supply**
2. **local coastal mobility**
3. **casualty / specialist evacuation**
4. **preservation/escape of the craft and trained boat crews themselves**

The roles overlap in time but the priority changes as the campaign deteriorates.

The exact number of craft and evacuees remains OPEN.

---

# 1. Historical physical floor

Historical Saipan actually concentrated roughly **35 troop-carrying powered craft** for the 17-Jun counterlanding attempt.

Therefore:
- a substantial local powered-craft system definitely existed;
- Branch cannot plausibly have only a token handful absent traced losses.

But:
- the exact type mix is unknown;
- 35 does not equal 35 large Daihatsu;
- large ramped craft / small landing craft / motor launches must remain separate pools.

# 2. Branch loss correction

Branch does not execute the historical mass 17-Jun barge attack.

Therefore:
- the historical **13 craft sunk** are not inherited.

Branch craft losses must come only from traced:
- air attack;
- naval bombardment;
- harbor/ramp attack;
- coastal mission loss;
- grounding;
- machinery failure;
- later receiving/interdiction battle.

This makes the late craft system more robust than a historical path that paid the 17-Jun mass loss.

# 6. Selective evacuation — corrected personnel rule

Before the final-operation gate:

### High priority
- transportable seriously wounded;
- stranded/recovered aircrew with little remaining Saipan combat value;
- specialists whose equipment/system is already gone;
- redundant technical cadres;
- documents / selected staff records.

### Conditional
- engineer/survey/artillery-observer specialists only when their remaining Saipan system no longer needs them;
- experienced officers/NCOs only when not required for final C2.

### Low / NO
- healthy infantry;
- company/platoon command skeletons needed for final defense/attack;
- functioning artillery/signals teams;
- critical Daihatsu crews while shuttle/relief missions continue.

Core rule:
> **evacuate only when future value exceeds remaining Saipan combat value.**

# 7. Boat crews are combat capability

Do not treat boat crews as generic evacuable specialists.

Experienced:
- coxswains;
- engineers;
- beach/boat control;
- night pilots;
- repair crews
are what convert a physical hull into a usable craft system.

Therefore:
- early evacuation of boat crews directly reduces lift;
- late evacuation of the craft without crews is meaningless;
- if a final craft-preservation run is made, hull + trained crew should be treated as one recoverable capability package.

# 8. Why inbound capacity cannot be reused as evacuation capacity

File83 first receiving-cycle planning throughput:
- roughly 1,050–1,500 personnel when concentrated for inbound movement.

Do **not** invert this and claim 1,000+ evacuees/night.

Outbound evacuation has different constraints:
- pickup points under shrinking Japanese control;
- wounded loading time;
- darkness;
- medical handling;
- U.S. radar/armed craft;
- craft dispersal;
- return route to Tinian;
- need to keep some craft for island logistics.

Therefore:
**evacuation throughput must be separately generated.**

The concept remains plausible over multiple nights.

The exact total does not.

Reasons it may be physically possible:
- short Saipan–Tinian crossing;
- prepared bidirectional reception doctrine;
- substantial local powered-craft base;
- no inherited 13-craft historical loss;
- smaller craft can move people without consuming every heavy landing craft.

Reasons not to close the number:
- exact hull survival is open;
- S0/S1/S2 changes late craft count;
- U.S. southern/eastern radar/armed-craft pressure is not closed;
- exact pickup-area ownership is open;
- evacuation competes with supply and local mobility.

Thus:
**“several hundred” = sensitivity / plausible class, not selected total.**

# 10. Final-operation manpower protection

This gate directly corrects the previous “too many personnel evacuated before a death attack?” concern.

Until the final attack is completed/aborted:
- do not drain healthy infantry;
- retain NCO/officer skeleton;
- retain usable mortar/artillery/observer/signals teams;
- retain boat crews required for tactical mobility.

After the final-operation gate closes:
- evacuation priority can shift sharply toward surviving specialists/cadres.

This preserves both:
- genuine suicidal rearguard/final attack;
- institutional knowledge preservation.

# 11. Organized-sea-evacuation end gate

Do not end evacuation on a historical date.

Organized boat evacuation ends when one or more become true:

- no repeatable controlled coastal pickup point;
- no viable Saipan–Tinian route under darkness;
- boat-control C2 collapses;
- MR craft pool becomes too small/fragmented;
- fuel/repair system collapses;
- U.S. radar/armed-craft coverage makes repeated runs prohibitive.

Individual escapes can continue after organized evacuation ends.

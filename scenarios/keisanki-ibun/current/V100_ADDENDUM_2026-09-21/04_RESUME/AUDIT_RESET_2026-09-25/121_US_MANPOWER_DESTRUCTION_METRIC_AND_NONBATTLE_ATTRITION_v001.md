# 0. User-metric correction

The user's intended “米兵ぶっ殺し数評価” was narrower than gross battle casualties but **broader than permanent disability**.

Correct interpretation:

> **Killed, or wounded/affected badly enough that the operational system has no credible return-to-duty date and must stop counting the man as recoverable combat strength.**

Thus it includes:
- KIA;
- DOW;
- presumed dead/MIA where appropriate;
- severe WIA;
- evacuated WIA for whom no near-term return is reasonably schedulable;
- selected neurological/psychiatric cases evacuated with no useful RTD clock.

It does **not** require proof of lifelong disability.

It excludes:
- light WIA with a known short RTD;
- moderate WIA with a reasonably scheduled return;
- one-to-three-day exhaustion/concussion cases that actually return;
- temporary local sick call.

Therefore file120's **5.3–7.0k** durable-loss band was too narrow.

# 2. J-DIRECT composition

### Dead / presumed dead
**~3,200–4,100**

Includes:
- KIA;
- DOW;
- combat MIA/presumed dead where appropriate.

### Severe WIA — no credible RTD
**~2,200–2,900**

Includes:
- major penetrating wounds;
- severe fragmentation;
- major burns;
- serious head/neurological injury;
- limb destruction;
- severe chest/abdominal wounds;
- major multiple trauma.

### Moderate/complex WIA evacuated without a useful RTD clock
**~1,400–2,000**

These are not “permanently disabled” by definition.

They count because:
- evacuation is required;
- repeated surgery/complication risk exists;
- no practical date exists for return to combat strength;
- the formation must replace them rather than plan around their return.

Do not count every moderate WIA.
This is the subset for whom the recovery clock is operationally indefinite.

### Selected combat-caused NP / blast-neurological no-RTD
**~200–400**

Examples:
- recurrent/severe combat exhaustion evacuated beyond forward recovery;
- persistent concussion;
- serious auditory/vestibular injury;
- neurological symptoms after blast/head injury.

Do not add these if already categorized as physical WIA.
This is mostly a classification tail.

Endpoints are composition guides; do not mechanically sum all highs.

# 6. Nonbattle / indirect battle-generated attrition

Keep a separate wallet.

Do not credit every fever, accident or exhaustion case as a Japanese weapon casualty.

Use:

## NBI-ALL
All meaningful nonbattle illness/injury/exhaustion cases that remove personnel from duty for at least a significant fraction of a day.

Potential mechanisms by late Saipan:
- heat/dehydration;
- sleep deprivation;
- diarrhea/GI illness;
- infected minor wounds / skin disease;
- vehicle/handling accidents;
- weapons accidents;
- falls/crush injuries in darkness;
- combat exhaustion without physical wound;
- hearing/concussion symptoms without recognized battle wound;
- medical staff / logistics overload extending recovery.

Working cumulative late-campaign class:
**~900–1,500 cases**

Most are temporary.
Do not add this whole number to J-DIRECT.

# 11. Interpretation

The user metric is intentionally an operational replacement-demand metric, not a medical lifetime-disability metric.

A man can eventually return months later and still count here if, at the time:
- his division cannot schedule his return;
- he has been evacuated;
- replacement planning must treat him as gone.

That is the correct meaning of:
> **「殺した」「もう復帰の目処がない」**

for this alternate-history manpower ledger.

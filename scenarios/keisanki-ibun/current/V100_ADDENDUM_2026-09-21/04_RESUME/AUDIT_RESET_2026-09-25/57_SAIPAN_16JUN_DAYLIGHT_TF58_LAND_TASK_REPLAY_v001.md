## 9. What TF58 "being distracted by land" really means

Not:
- TF58 loses control of fleet air defense;
- dozens of CAP fighters are absent when the Japanese strike arrives;
- carrier-defense authority must be rewritten.

Yes:
- morning deck cycles and fuel are slightly more consumed;
- a few more fighters/attack aircraft are in return/rearm pipelines;
- ground commanders expect fast-carrier help because CVE/NGFS alone are spending heavily on recurring Japanese fire-control cells;
- after the strike, damaged/congested TF58 decks have even less capacity for discretionary land support.

Strategically, the harder Saipan ground fight increases the value of **remaining close to the lodgment**.

Thus the already-approved 14:15 decision not to chase the Japanese carriers west becomes more, not less, coherent.

---

# 0. Core judgment

The U.S. does **not** treat 8/9 Jul as:
> Saipan finished -> same formations and ships immediately roll into Tinian/Guam.

Instead:

> **close Saipan -> evacuate casualties -> classify formations -> release ships by role -> repair/reload -> choose the next assault from the force that is actually reusable.**

The binding problem is no longer raw total U.S. strength.
It is:
- formation integrity;
- specialist/leader replacement;
- landing-craft readiness;
- amphibious-shipping turnaround;
- naval-support fatigue/ammunition;
- which Guam/Tinian force architecture has already been consumed by Saipan.

---

# 1. First 24 h after T-END

## Ground
Immediate orders:

1. stop unnecessary offensive movement;
2. use F0/F1 + U1/U2 for:
   - perimeter security;
   - POW/cave control;
   - route security;
   - rear-area cleanup;
3. pull F2/F3 battalions out of primary movement;
4. establish divisional reconstitution areas;
5. begin mass medical / psychiatric / specialist triage;
6. consolidate company/platoon remnants.

The worst units are **not** “rested in place.”
They need to leave the same terrain/alarms that broke them.

---

# 2. Formation triage

## F3
Action:
- immediate withdrawal;
- no new opposed-assault mission;
- form composite companies/battalions if required for local security only;
- identify whether original battalion can be rebuilt or should be temporarily cannibalized.

Clock:
**not less than several days** to become coherent again,
and some will not be restored as independent battalions before the next Marianas assault decision.

## F2
Action:
- 36–72 h first reconstitution cycle;
- leader/specialist refill;
- weapons sorting;
- sleep;
- replacement integration.

For a new amphibious assault:
**~5–10 day class** is more realistic than “72 h and ready.”

## F1
Can return to useful field duty after:
- 48–96 h rest/maintenance/replacement,
but not automatically to first-wave assault.

## F0
Hold/cleanup/reserve.
Do not burn them simply because they are the only healthy troops.

---

# 3. Leader/specialist repair

The U.S. first uses internal redistribution:

- promote company/platoon replacements;
- shift senior NCOs;
- move FOs/radio/wire personnel between battalions;
- consolidate under-crewed weapons companies;
- redistribute corpsmen;
- move mechanics/recovery crews toward critical equipment.

This produces a hidden cost:
> repairing one broken battalion can weaken another support pool.

Deep replacements arriving later are required to restore the division rather than merely make one composite battalion.

---

# 4. Medical and psychiatric evacuation becomes a shipping task

The campaign leaves:
- very large surgical/evacuation load;
- J-DIRECT ~7,000–8,800;
- serious no-short-RTD nonbattle loss;
- clusters of combat-exhaustion/NP cases.

Therefore backhaul shipping is not empty.

Priority:
1. critical wounded;
2. serious surgical cases;
3. severe neuropsychiatric/exhaustion evacuees;
4. longer-term moderate WIA;
5. selected exhausted specialists whose formation cannot use them immediately.

Ships returning east/rear carry casualties and damaged equipment while freeing forward medical capacity.

---

# 5. Amphibious shipping — release by category

Do not say “the fleet goes home.”

## A. APA / AKA / major troop-cargo transports

These are among the first large hulls that must be released once:
- essential unloading complete;
- casualty backload complete;
- no immediate emergency reinforcement role remains.

Reason:
- next assault requires them reloaded in a deliberate combat load;
- they need boat/gear repair;
- medical cleanup;
- stores accounting;
- landing tables rebuilt.

Working:
**begin release in the first 24–72 h after T-END** in waves.

They move to the appropriate rear/staging point, not necessarily CONUS.

---

## B. LST / LCI / LCT / LCVP / LCM

Split the pool.

### Stay-behind subset
Needed for:
- Saipan sustainment;
- intra-island/coastal movement;
- engineering;
- casualty/logistics support;
- possible Tinian preparation.

### Turnaround subset
Withdraw for:
- engine work;
- hull/propeller/ramp repair;
- replacement crews;
- loading for the next assault.

The old working state already had:
- LCVP/LCM/LCI MR ~70–80% class;
- LVT MR materially lower than physical count;
- boat/shore-party crews fatigued.

Therefore:
**landing-craft readiness, not APA hull count alone, blocks immediate reuse.**

---

## C. LVT / amphibious assault vehicles

These are not just “vehicles ashore.”

Need:
- recovery from beach/shore duty;
- engine/track repair;
- saltwater maintenance;
- crew replacement/rest;
- reassembly into assault companies.

A physically surviving LVT is not automatically Tinian-ready.

This is one of the strongest reasons the Saipan assault system cannot immediately become the next assault system.

---

# 6. NGFS ships — BB / CA / CL / DD

They were fighting too.

After Saipan closes:
- ammunition magazines are depleted unevenly;
- barrels and fire-control gear require maintenance;
- machinery has accumulated operating hours;
- crews have spent weeks at high readiness;
- DDs have screening/ASW/plane-guard fatigue.

Therefore release in layers.

### First releasable
ships not required against:
- surviving Tinian fire;
- local air threat;
- Japanese surface re-entry threat.

### Retained local layer
enough:
- cruisers/DD;
- selected old BB/NGFS;
- screens
to protect Saipan and support the next immediate local decision.

### Turnaround
released ships:
- rearm;
- refuel;
- repair;
- crew rest;
then return to the assault/support pool.

Do not credit them to the next operation merely because they remain physically afloat.

---

# 7. CVE support

CVE direct-support remains until Aslito can replace enough:
- CAP;
- local reconnaissance;
- CAS/on-call support;
- ASW/air cover.

By 8/9 Jul Aslito has stage-5-class tactical usefulness,
but the base is not yet fully mature.

Therefore:
- CVEs can begin **progressive release**;
- not all at once;
- some remain until land-based cycles are demonstrably reliable and Tinian air threat is suppressed.

This is a transfer of functions:
> deck aviation -> Aslito land-based aviation.

---

# 8. Repair/salvage/train ships

These are the opposite of combat ships.

Remain locally or nearby:
- repair;
- salvage;
- tugs;
- fuel/water;
- ammunition handling;
- construction support.

Their job expands after the shooting declines.

Hospital/evacuation hulls:
**leave repeatedly**, carrying the casualty load rearward.

---

# 9. The shipping-turnaround clock

Use a layered clock, not one number.

### 0–2 days
- casualty backload;
- emergency repairs;
- inventory;
- release first transports/escorts;
- retain minimum Saipan cover.

### 2–5 days
- more APAs/AKAs/LSTs clear;
- small-craft maintenance;
- rearm/refuel NGFS groups;
- begin new combat-loading plans.

### 5–10 days
- meaningful fraction of the amphibious system can be assembled again,
provided:
  - destination chosen;
  - assault formations certified;
  - landing craft/LVTs repaired;
  - shipping not diverted elsewhere.

This is a **system regeneration clock**, not just sailing time.

---

# 10. Immediate command decision — freeze old assault dates

On 8/9 Jul:
- historical-style fixed Guam/Tinian execution dates are no longer credible planning assumptions.

Command should order:
**48–72 h force-and-shipping certification pause**

during which:
- division states are reported by battalion;
- shipping MR is reported by hull/craft class;
- ammunition/fuel replacement is checked;
- Guam-force damage from U1/U2 diversion is assessed;
- 77th status is reconciled;
- Tinian threat is reassessed.

This is not indecision.
It is mandatory after the original FORAGER sequential reuse model has broken.

---

# 11. Why ships cannot simply be kept at Saipan

Keeping them indefinitely has real costs:

- next combat load cannot be prepared;
- crews do not rest;
- landing craft do not receive deep maintenance;
- hulls remain exposed to Tinian/local Japanese air/fire;
- escorts remain tied;
- fuel/ammunition train remains forward;
- the next assault force cannot form.

Therefore once Saipan no longer needs a ship's specific function:
**release it immediately into the turnaround pipeline.**

The U.S. advantage is the ability to do this at scale.
The Branch penalty is that more ships are late entering that pipeline.

---

# 12. Why ships cannot all leave immediately

Conversely Saipan still needs:

- local air defense;
- ASW;
- NGFS against residual/Tinian threats;
- logistics;
- medical evacuation;
- engineering lift;
- coastal patrol;
- reserve movement.

And Japan still has:
- Tinian aviation/fire;
- local water-air;
- a nonzero fleet threat.

Therefore:
**progressive release**, not a fleet-wide departure.

---

# 13. Strategic consequence before choosing Tinian or Guam

By roughly 10–12 Jul the U.S. will have a clearer answer:

### Ground
- how many F0/F1 battalions exist;
- which F2 can be rebuilt;
- which F3 must be cannibalized;
- whether 2d/4th Marines can supply another opposed landing force.

### Guam architecture
- 1st Provisional Marine Brigade has already lost U1/U2 slices to Saipan;
- 77th planning has been disturbed;
- 3d Marine Division remains especially valuable because it is one of the least-broken large formations.

### Shipping
- enough hulls may exist,
but not all are:
  - combat loaded;
  - craft-complete;
  - crew-rested;
  - escort-supported.

This forces a genuine **next-objective choice** rather than automatic execution of both historical dates.

---

# 14. Selected immediate U.S. behavior

The most likely U.S. response is:

1. **secure Saipan with the healthiest battalions and fresh U1/U2 elements;**
2. **pull the worst Saipan battalions out of contact immediately;**
3. **evacuate serious wounded/NP cases aggressively;**
4. **release APAs/AKAs and excess escorts in waves as soon as their Saipan task ends;**
5. **keep only the naval/air/logistics layer needed to hold Saipan against Tinian/Japanese re-entry;**
6. **repair landing craft/LVTs and rest crews;**
7. **freeze the old Tinian/Guam assault dates until both formations and shipping are recertified;**
8. **then redesign FORAGER around whichever fresh formation + amphibious package can be made coherent first.**

This is the key:
> the U.S. does not lack ships or men absolutely.
> It lacks enough **coherent men on coherent ships in the right assault package on the original clock.**

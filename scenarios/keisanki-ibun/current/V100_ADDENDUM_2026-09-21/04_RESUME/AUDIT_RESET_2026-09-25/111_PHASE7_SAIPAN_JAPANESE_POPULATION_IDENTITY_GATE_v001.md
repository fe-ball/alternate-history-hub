# 0. Core finding

The historical **31,629** Japanese military personnel on Saipan is a useful historical anchor, but it is **not automatically the Branch 15-Jun denominator**.

Historical composition:
- Army ~25,469;
- Navy ~6,160;
- total ~31,629.

- 118th Infantry lost ~850 men in transit;
- many survivors arrived without weapons/equipment;
- across Jan–early Jun Marianas troop movements, ~12,000 men were aboard torpedoed vessels and ~3,600 died;
- several thousand survivors/stragglers accumulated on Saipan, including personnel whose effective unit state was badly degraded and some whose movement to other destinations was disrupted.

Branch:
- moves 43d Division earlier;
- does not inherit the same late convoy-loss chain by default;
- cross-loads critical equipment;
- has earlier assignment/receiving plans;
- may also deliberately add engineer/specialist personnel.

Therefore Branch can have:
- **different total headcount**
and simultaneously
- **much higher coherent/equipped combat fraction**.

Do not model the Branch as:
historical 31,629
+ restored historical transport losses
+ all historical stranded personnel
+ extra Branch specialists.

That would double count mutually exclusive transport histories.

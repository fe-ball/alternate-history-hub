- approximately 80% of troops in the convoy surviving, but many arriving without weapons/equipment;
- the 118th Infantry Regiment reaching Saipan as a badly disorganized body with very low combat efficiency;
- additional earlier convoy survivors becoming ill-equipped stragglers.

- regimental weapons and ammunition can be assigned into the island defense plan;

### Selected Phase-0 correction

# 3. Historical physical -> usable gap was large

U.S. Marine historical reporting describes:
- large numbers of heavy weapons still in naval depots;
- weapons loaded on railroad cars;
- weapons still in preservative near partially excavated gun positions;
- searchlights and AA weapons also not fully installed;
- incomplete defensive works at the time of invasion.

This means the historical limiting state was often not:
> weapon does not exist.

It was:
> weapon exists physically but has not become an installed, connected, surveyed, supplied combat system.

That is exactly where the Branch has leverage.

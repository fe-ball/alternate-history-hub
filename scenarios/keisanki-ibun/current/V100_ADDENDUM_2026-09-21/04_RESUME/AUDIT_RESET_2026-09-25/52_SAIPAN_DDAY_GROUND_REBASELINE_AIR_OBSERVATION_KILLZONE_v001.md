## 0. Why a ground rebaseline is now required

The approved D-Day ground line assumed good Japanese pre-registration and selective defense, but it under-specified two linked mechanisms:

1. aerial observation can refresh target data in areas poorly visible from ground OPs;
2. U.S. troops/vehicles/105-mm batteries can be held inside already-prepared kill zones long enough for that fresher data to matter.

This is not merely an aviation-side ledger correction.

If observation keeps a target box CURRENT while a U.S. battalion / artillery battery / tank group is still physically constrained by:
- reef and beach exits;
- road/rail cuts;
- Afetna/Susupe seams;
- vehicle assembly;
- artillery setup;
- narrow inland routes,

then the same Japanese ammunition produces more useful fire.

## 1. Kill-zone model

Do not use an abstract fortification multiplier.

For each target box track:

- geometric choke / movement ceiling;
- target freshness;
- observer path;
- fire-data state;
- firing battery available;
- ammunition/access;
- U.S. suppression state;
- dwell time before the target exits/disperses.

Aerial observation mainly improves:
- target freshness;
- recognition that an old target has moved;
- selection of the next target box;
- knowledge of when a handling/assembly area is actually occupied.

It does NOT increase shell supply or gun rate of fire.

## 2. D-Day morning high-value kill zones

### Reef / beach-exit traffic
Target:
- LVT/LCM flow;
- infantry bunching;
- beach-exit congestion.

Ground OPs already see much of this.
Air observation adds:
- which lanes are truly active;
- offshore congestion;
- route shifts behind smoke/terrain.

Selected additional useful dwell/friction:
- **5–15 min** in locally congested boxes.

### 105-mm landing / assembly
Target:
- artillery pieces;
- prime movers;
- ammunition;
- crews assembling around known road exits.

Air observation adds major value because this is behind the immediate shoreline.

Selected:
- **10–25 min** additional displacement / dispersion / setup friction in affected battery groups when Japanese fire arrives on a fresh box.

The approved 10–20 min first-effective-fire delay becomes a floor/center mechanism, not necessarily the entire support penalty.

### Sherman landing / assembly
Air-ground observation improves warning to Japanese tank cells and mortar/artillery.

Selected:
- **5–15 min** local assembly/route disruption in affected groups;
- tank cells receive fresher approach-lane information.

### Afetna / Susupe seam
This is the highest-value recurrent box.

Selected:
- repeated **10–30 min** reorganization periods at different times/sectors rather than one continuous stop;
- target boards shift from beach lanes to reserve / artillery / road movement.

## 3. U.S. first response — pay airpower

The U.S. does not simply accept the added fire.

Once repeated Japanese observed fire is recognized, U.S. local air effort shifts.

Selected D-Day air-task tax, spread through the day:

### Observer denial / local CAP
- **12–20 F6F sortie-equivalents**
- tasked to low/medium-altitude Japanese observation / repeated recon routes and local protective sweeps.

### Artillery / OP / route suppression
- **18–30 strike sortie-equivalents** from CVE / available carrier-support aircraft;
- bombs/strafing against suspected OPs, mortar/artillery zones, road cuts and active firing areas.

These are not extra aircraft magically added to the theater.
They are task reallocations from:
- generic CAS;
- armed reconnaissance;
- reserve CAP;
- other target sets.

Effect:
- Japan loses persistence;
- some OPs / firing cells become temporarily blind;
- U.S. ground forces buy windows to leave kill zones.

Cost:
- less air effort available for other ground targets;
- deck/fuel/ordnance consumption;
- observer-denial sorties need repeated coverage.

## 4. Time-phased D-Day result

### 08:45–11:00 — strongest divergence
Japanese advantage:
- most prepared target boards are still geographically relevant;
- assault troops/vehicles are most channelized;
- U.S. shore artillery is not yet fully functioning;
- Japanese air observation / ground OP correlation is freshest.

Result:
- U.S. infantry/vehicles suffer higher loss and slower exit from selected boxes;
- first-echelon command disruption is somewhat worse;
- U.S. airpower increasingly switches to observer/artillery suppression.

### 11:00–14:00 — U.S. adaptation
U.S. gains:
- more artillery ashore;
- more Shermans;
- better local observation;
- air/NGFS target priorities shift;
- engineers open/bypass some routes.

Japanese target freshness remains useful but firing cells are increasingly suppressed/displaced.

### 14:00–sunset — divergence narrows
U.S. mass and support begin to dominate local throughput.
Japanese defenders trade ground rather than remain exposed.

Thus the largest front-line difference should appear around midday, not grow without limit to sunset.

## 6. U.S. casualty sensitivity

Historical calibration:
- NHHC describes up to ~3,500 U.S. casualties in the first 24 hours of historical Saipan and specifically notes heavy Japanese artillery/mortar fire and dangerous crowding on the beaches.

Branch has:
- better Japanese fire discipline;
- deeper preserved indirect fire;
- fresher target data;
- tank cells used to extend dwell;
- but stronger U.S. adaptive air/NGFS response.

Selected supersession-candidate first-24h U.S. ground casualty band:
- **3,800–4,700 class**
- inclusive of the already-approved 15/16-night incremental 280–450;
- NOT additive to a separate historical number;
- NOT yet authority.

This is a calibration band pending full unit/casualty denominator reconstruction.

The effect is strongest in WIA / temporary combat loss and local leadership disruption, not necessarily KIA alone.

## 7. Japanese loss / exposure counter-effect

Better observation requires aircraft/OP activity and firing.

Therefore Japan also pays:
- observer aircraft losses;
- OP detection;
- more U.S. suppression sorties;
- batteries revealing themselves through repeated effective fire;
- engineer/wire teams exposed during repair.

Do not assume fresher Japanese fire reduces Japanese casualties universally.

Expected:
- some batteries survive longer through better displacement;
- some high-value OP/fire cells are suppressed earlier because the U.S. identifies their operational importance.

This is why the sunset front difference narrows.

## 8. 15/16 night consequence

The approved distributed night attack now begins from:
- a slightly shallower U.S. bridgehead in several sectors;
- higher U.S. first-day casualties/fatigue;
- somewhat more U.S. air/ordnance spent on suppression;
- better Japanese target boards from daytime air observation.

But Japan has also spent 6–9 local aircraft irreversibly during D-Day local-air operations.

Selected candidate dawn difference from current authority:
- generally **50–150 yd** shallower U.S. depth in selected sectors;
- Afetna/Susupe fire-control problem somewhat stronger;
- Aslito remains Japanese either way;
- tank MR band does not need material change yet.

Do NOT overwrite authority until full night replay is rerun.

## 9. U.S. airpower strategic effect

The local result can remain tactically U.S.-favorable while becoming more expensive.

More Japanese observation means the U.S. spends more airpower on:
- observation denial;
- artillery suppression;
- road/OP sweeps;
- repeated same-area strikes after batteries reappear.

This reduces the idea of "free" CVE support.

It can create:
- fewer CAS sorties at low-priority infantry targets;
- more fighter fuel spent over the island;
- higher deck-cycle load;
- more ordnance consumption.

The U.S. can afford this better than Japan, so it does not reverse the campaign.
It does make the initial advance more costly and less smooth.

## 10. Core interpretation

The key Branch divergence is not:
"Japanese artillery shoots more accurately all day."

It is:
- more target boxes remain current;
- the U.S. remains inside some of them longer;
- Japanese fire cells know when to fire and when to move;
- the U.S. then spends airpower and artillery to break that loop.

This observation -> kill-zone -> suppression -> rework cycle is the core land-battle mechanism.

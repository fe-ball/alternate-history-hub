# Marianas Japanese aviation full-role reaudit — 11 to 18 June 1944

Date: 2026-09-26
Status: WORKING REAUDIT / NO CAMPAIGN AUTHORITY PROMOTION
Canonical authority remains Branch B v100 / 1944-06-16T14:15.
Saipan ground authority remains approved through 1944-06-16 dawn.

## 0. Finding

The existing Marianas replay modeled Japanese aviation strongly in:
- fighter defense;
- maritime ISR/recontact;
- fleet-decisive strike participation;
- runway/base regeneration.

It modeled too weakly:
- battlefield observation / artillery cooperation;
- photo renewal of ground target boards;
- attack on amphibious handling / unloading / artillery parks when observed;
- small anti-shipping attacks against fixed/slow support groups;
- counter-observation / observer denial;
- liaison / inter-island reporting;
- SAR / aircrew recovery;
- bombardment-damage survey and repair-priority reconnaissance;
- reverse ferry / re-concentration of surviving carrier-qualified aircraft.

These functions compete for the same finite aircraft, crews, fuel, maintenance and radio/plot capacity.

## 1. Mission taxonomy

Track each sortie under ONE primary mission ID:

A. AIR DEFENSE
- interceptor CAP;
- point defense;
- counter-recon / observer denial.

B. MARITIME ISR
- broad search;
- carrier recontact;
- amphibious / bombardment / transport observation;
- submarine / fleet handoff.

C. GROUND ISR / FIRE CONTROL
- artillery spotting;
- ground-target photo confirmation;
- beach / road / artillery / armor observation;
- bombardment damage survey;
- target-board renewal.

D. OPPORTUNITY ATTACK
- armed reconnaissance attack;
- beach/unloading/handling-area attack;
- artillery / vehicle concentration attack;
- small-craft / LST / transport attack;
- fire-support ship attack if contact is fresh enough.

E. PREPLANNED STRIKE
- carrier attack;
- transport/support-group strike;
- night/dusk harassment.

F. SUPPORT
- liaison;
- SAR;
- weather;
- ferry;
- inter-island personnel / small critical-parts movement.

The same aircraft can change mission on a later sortie, not on the same timestamp without paying the transition.

## 2. Source wallets

### Fighters
Main uses:
- airfield/base defense;
- escort;
- observer denial;
- anti-recon.

Do not use fighter count as free strafing capacity after paying CAP.

### D4Y
Main uses:
- high-speed maritime/ground confirmation;
- photo;
- attack;
- limited armed reconnaissance.

Every D4Y used for photo/confirmation is removed from attack availability until recovery/service.

### B6N / older attack aircraft
Main uses:
- anti-shipping;
- preplanned bomb/torpedo attack;
- limited ground interdiction.

Torpedo attack is not an instant reaction mission. Loading/arming/briefing/target motion matter.

### F1M / E13A / Zuiun-class water-air
Main uses:
- local maritime search;
- artillery spotting;
- battlefield observation;
- liaison;
- SAR;
- short armed reconnaissance / light attack where risk justifies it.

Water-air continuity is especially valuable after runway suppression.

### H6K/H8K
Long-range search/SAR/night-capable roles remain possible but exact local sortie availability must be dated. Do not use them as free tactical bombers.

### Ki-36/Ki-51
Role exists doctrinally for Army frontline observation / artillery cooperation.
Exact June Marianas allocation remains OPEN; center credit remains zero until traced.

## 3. 11 June — initial TF58 sweep

Existing result:
- Japanese all-air irreversible 41–56;
- repair queue 28–42;
- U.S. tactical air superiority achieved;
- local air system not neutralized.

Under-modeled roles:

### 3.1 Bombardment / raid damage survey
After each major raid window:
- surviving water-air / liaison layer can inspect runway damage, fires, blocked roads and actual vs decoy destruction;
- output feeds engineer priority rather than combat directly.

Selected workload:
- 2–4 short survey/liaison sorties across the archipelago in gaps;
- not all over Saipan;
- no extra combat result.

### 3.2 SAR
Water-air/SAR effort should be open for downed Japanese pilots near islands.
Do not award rescues without dated cases, but do not treat all ditching near friendly islands as automatic crew loss.

### 3.3 Opportunity attack
NO centered daylight anti-TF58 strike.
Reason:
- Japanese air-defense battle is consuming fighters;
- contact/target quality and U.S. CAP favor preservation;
- the first day priority is survival/ISR, not a small low-EV counterstrike.

## 4. 12 June — preserve ISR, selective night offense

Existing:

Reinterpret / expand mission context:

## 12. Cargo-handling / unloading attack gate

A visible unloading target is not automatically attackable.

Required:
1. observation freshness;
2. target class and movement;
3. launch base usable;
4. correct ordnance already loaded or arming time available;
5. crew daylight/night qualification;
6. fighter escort requirement;
7. U.S. CAP/AA estimate;
8. recovery base/route;
9. higher-priority ISR/defense task not consuming the same aircraft.

High-payoff target examples:
- clustered LST/LCM;
- visible ammunition/fuel handling;
- artillery assembly;
- tank debarkation/assembly;
- bridge/road choke handling.

Low-payoff:
- dispersed infantry on open ground under strong CAP;
- a single moving destroyer with no fresh track;
- random beach bombing without target confirmation.

## 13. Counter-observation audit

Japanese aviation can attack:
- U.S. artillery spotters;
- naval gunfire observation aircraft;
- low/medium-altitude reconnaissance;
- repeated photo routes.

Effect is not primarily aircraft kill count.

If successful:
- U.S. must allocate fighter cover;
- a firing ship/battery may temporarily use less precise map/area fire;
- Japanese battery displacement and engineer work gain time.

This should be modeled as a sortie/fuel/observation-quality tax on the U.S., not a blanket naval-gunfire accuracy penalty.

## 14. SAR / aircrew preservation

Water-air rescue and local SAR can preserve scarce experienced aircrew.

Rules:
- only credit where ditch/crash is within reachable water and a rescue sortie can actually be launched;
- rescue aircraft pays exposure/fuel and cannot perform ISR simultaneously;
- U.S. CAP/submarine/surface presence can deny recovery.

This matters over 11–18 Jun because crew preservation affects later reconnaissance and night-qualified attack capacity.

## 15. Main audit conclusions

Material omissions:
1. **15-Jun local anti-shipping air reaction** — must be explicitly replayed.
2. **11–18 Jun ground/bombardment BDA and artillery-observation layer** — should be inserted as a persistent but scarce task.
3. **counter-observation / spotter denial** — under-modeled on both sides.
4. **SAR / liaison / repair-priority reconnaissance** — under-modeled resource users.
5. **opportunity attack on unloading/handling** — plausible, especially D-day and later dusk/night, but must pass freshness/crew/ordnance/CAP gates.

Not justified:
- daily large Japanese beachhead strikes;
- constant artillery spotting orbits;
- free Ki-36/Ki-51 local units;
- every water-air observer carrying out a useful attack;
- treating air observation as continuous real-time ground C2.

# 0. Core finding

The late Japanese operation should not center on:
> “break through and reach Aslito.”

By the time the final-operation gate is reached, Aslito is too far south to be a realistic principal ground objective from the compressed northern/central Japanese pocket.

Instead, distinguish:

### Earlier reinforcement-interdiction targets
- Aslito north/support roads;
- Chacha / Laulau / central movement corridors;
- artillery/FDC/vehicle assembly areas;
- fresh-battalion staging and handoff zones.

### Final-operation targets
- Tanapag coastal road/rail corridor;
- Road Junction / route-control nodes;
- 27th Division / attached battalion rear;
- forward artillery/FDC;
- regimental CP / aid / collecting / vehicle areas;
- Marine/Army handoff seams.

Thus:
**Aslito matters mainly in the conversion-interdiction phase.**
**Tanapag / forward rear-support geometry matters in the final operation.**

# 1. Historical terrain skeleton

Use the following physical geometry as a guard.

## West
- Garapan;
- coastal road / rail axis;
- Tanapag Harbor / Flores Point;
- later Tanapag Plain.

Characteristics:
- relatively open movement compared with Tapotchau;
- strong U.S. naval/shore-fire exposure;
- good vehicle/logistics route;
- once the front moves north, contains valuable U.S. rear/support traffic.

## Center
- Mount Tapotchau massif;
- Death Valley;
- Purple Heart Ridge;
- Hashigoru / central high-ground transition.

Characteristics:
- ideal Japanese observation / reverse-slope / mortar terrain;
- slow mass movement;
- excellent for infiltration, fixing, observation and fire-control;
- poor for a broad rapid charge.

## East
- Chacha / Laulau;
- Kagman / Magicienne Bay side;
- Aslito-north/east routes farther south.

Characteristics:
- important U.S. route and division-boundary geometry in the middle campaign;
- valuable for reinforcement movement interdiction;
- progressively less relevant as the whole front shifts north.

# 2. Historical division geometry

By the 22–23 Jun northward phase:
- 2d Marine Division is on the left/west;
- 4th Marine Division on the right/east;
- 27th Infantry Division is inserted into the center.

The 27th fights through:
- Death Valley;
- Purple Heart Ridge;
- central Tapotchau system.

Later:
- 2d Marines push through Garapan / west side;
- 27th moves toward Tanapag Plain in the center;
- 4th Marines remain on the right/east;
- once the 27th reaches the west coast/Tanapag axis, parts of the 2d can be pinched out / rested.

This produces real moving division and battalion seams.

# 3. Historical proof that relief seams matter

On 4 Jul, during a relief between 27th Division elements and 4th Marine Division battalions, a gap opened and roughly a hundred Japanese infiltrated through before the line was repaired.

Therefore:
> **relief / handoff seams are not an invented Branch vulnerability.**

Branch changes:
- Japanese observation/reporting is better;
- night mobility is better;
- higher fraction of surviving infantry remains organized;
- Japanese command can plan around a known handoff instead of merely exploiting it accidentally.

This supports making a relief seam an explicit final-operation target.

# 4. U1 likely employment family

Do not freeze one exact battalion boundary yet.

Operationally, 1/22 is most valuable where it can:
1. take over a holding / security sector;
2. release a depleted 2d/4th Marine or 27th Division battalion;
3. restore a fresh reserve.

That creates three plausible employment families.

## U1-W — west / Garapan-Tanapag support
Pros:
- easy coastal logistics;
- can relieve tired west-side Marine elements.

Cons:
- exposed to Japanese artillery/observation from surviving central high ground;
- later Japanese final axis may directly threaten it.

## U1-C — central / 27th relief
Pros:
- greatest immediate need if Death Valley / Tapotchau remains costly;
- maximizes benefit to U.S. rotation.

Cons:
- strongest Japanese observed-fire/interdiction environment;
- rough movement/handoff.

## U1-E — east / 4th Marine relief
Pros:
- helps the division carrying long Aslito/eastern burden;
- frees Marine battalion for reserve/reconstitution.

Cons:
- road movement from rear areas is observable;
- later final operation may no longer physically reach this sector once the front shifts north.

### Center judgment
**U1-C / U1-E are the more likely first relief missions.**
Not because of unit identity, but because that is where fresh battalion backfill produces the largest rotation payoff.

Do not select exact attachment yet.

# 5. U1 interdiction geometry

If U1 moves before central high-ground collapse:

Japanese observation can identify:
- unusual vehicle columns;
- route-control activity;
- battalion-size assembly;
- old-unit withdrawal traffic;
- new artillery/security patterns.

Best Japanese fires:
- road junction;
- staging area;
- handoff line;
- bridge/defile;
- FDC supporting the relief.

Best Japanese ground action:
- company/platoon infiltration;
- probe the seam;
- force old battalion to stay;
- attack route security after dark.

The operational metric is:
**hours added before the old U.S. battalion actually leaves the line.**

# 6. U2 geometry

If U2 is triggered, it likely lands/arrives after:
- U1 has exposed U.S. relief procedures;
- Japanese central observation has degraded;
- U.S. has dispersed routes/assembly;
- front has moved farther north.

Therefore U2 physical destruction should be lower than U1.

But:
- U2 arrives into a more compressed line;
- fewer alternate U.S. routes may exist;
- Japanese command now knows which seam is critical.

Center:
**U2 interdiction is more seam-focused and less broad-area.**

---

# 7. Tapotchau collapse and the end of the long-range observation phase

Historical central resistance:
- remains decisive through late Jun;
- by around 1 Jul the strong Tapotchau line is broken and Japanese withdrawal north accelerates.

Branch:
- stronger reverse-slope / alternate OP system can shift this clock modestly right;
- but once:
  - crest/flank routes are cut;
  - west/east observation paths are separated;
  - batteries lose repeatable target update,
the campaign changes phase.

Then:
> **Japanese interdiction transitions from observed-fire system to local mortar / infiltration / short-route defense.**

This is the point where U1/U2 begin to convert much faster.

Thus T-US2 depends heavily on whether U1 arrives **before or after this observation-collapse transition**.

# 8. Final operation — primary terrain family

The best final-operation family is **west-central / Tanapag**.

Reasons:
- open enough to mass a larger infantry packet;
- road/rail corridor provides a recognizable axis;
- U.S. rear/support traffic is concentrated;
- historical final attack proved Japanese infantry can penetrate deeply here;
- route leads directly toward battalion/regimental rear rather than tactically empty ridge.

The central high ground should support/fix the attack, not necessarily carry the largest mass.

# 9. Final operation — preferred axis structure

## Main axis A — Tanapag coastal/plain penetration
Force:
- largest coherent infantry packet;
- MG/mortar follow;
- selected engineers;
- limited tank survivors only if route physically permits.

Mission:
1. rupture a relief/handoff seam;
2. overrun forward battalion perimeter;
3. continue toward:
   - route junctions;
   - aid/collecting points;
   - regimental rear;
   - forward artillery/FDC;
   - vehicle/reserve assembly.

## Axis B — Harakiri / central-gulch fixing attack
Force:
- smaller terrain-experienced infantry;
- mortars;
- MG;
- infiltration.

Mission:
- fix adjacent battalion;
- stop lateral reinforcement;
- preserve the main-axis flank;
- exploit caves/ravines for close approach.

## Axis C — ridge infiltration / route cut
Force:
- small selected packets.

Mission:
- cut road;
- attack signals;
- create false reports;
- threaten artillery/rear security;
- increase friendly-fire/no-fire friction.

Do not spread the whole remaining force uniformly.

# 13. Effect on U2 threshold

This terrain pass strengthens U2 when U1 arrives before observation collapse.

### If U1 lands while Tapotchau system still coherent
- U1 conversion is delayed;
- old battalion remains committed;
- U2 threshold rises toward GREEN.

### If U1 lands after observation collapse
- U1 converts faster;
- U2 may be avoided on S0/S1.

Therefore the **earliest causal checkpoint** for U2 is no longer Japanese headcount.

It is:
> **date/state of central-highland observation collapse relative to U1 arrival.**

That is the one dependency worth reopening if U2 must be selected.

---

# 14. Final-operation timing implication

A likely sequence is:

1. U1 arrives while central observation still works.
2. Japanese fire/infiltration delays handoff.
3. U2 is ordered or considered.
4. Tapotchau/central observation begins collapsing.
5. Japanese command recognizes that future reinforcement will convert faster.
6. Remaining coherent infantry is still > final-operation threshold.
7. final operation is released before U2 + reconstituted U.S. battalions fully mature.

This tends to put the operation:
**early July, near the transition from central-highland collapse to Tanapag/northern compression.**

It can move somewhat earlier/later.
It does not naturally move to late July.

# 1. Correct mission logic

The first wave is a fighter sweep.

Do NOT assign it a bomber mission it does not possess.

F6F .50-caliber fire is highly useful against:
- exposed aircraft;
- aircraft in light dispersal;
- vehicles;
- fuel/ammunition handling vehicles;
- light AA;
- exposed AA crews;
- radar antenna / local communications;
- soft buildings;
- boats / shipping opportunities.

It is poor as a primary destruction mechanism against:
- revetted heavy AA;
- 120-mm fixed/DP batteries;
- 75-mm protected battery positions;
- hardened magazines;
- major runway cratering.

Therefore:
> **runway physical closure from the F6F sweep alone is not centered.**

Any runway/taxi obstruction comes mainly from:
- burning/wrecked aircraft;
- vehicles;
- local fires;
- abandoned equipment;
not major crater damage.

Hard targets are left to:
- later bomber attack;
- naval bombardment;
- subsequent strike cycles.

# 3. Heavy / medium AA engagement

Relevant Tinian system:
- Ushi 120-mm DP;
- Ushi 75-mm AA;
- other 120-mm sectors where route geometry permits;
- 76.2-mm supplementary layer.

Constraint:
friendly Japanese fighters are still in / near the air-defense zone.

Therefore heavy AA cannot simply fire at maximum theoretical volume through the entire dogfight.

Best engagement windows:
1. U.S. groups separating from Japanese fighters;
2. F6Fs descending toward the field;
3. egress / climb-out;
4. high-cover groups crossing predictable sectors.

Branch gains:
- earlier useful track assignment;
- altitude/sector preparation;
- better fuze-setting discipline;
- better sustained mechanical reliability.

Still absent:
- proximity fuze;
- U.S.-quality radar gunlaying;
- perfect target tracking.

Selected heavy/medium-AA effect on F6F:

### irreversible / forced ditch / unrecoverable
**1–2**

### additional damaged / mission-abort
**3–5**

Main value:
**force geometry and break repeat passes**, not mass kills.

# 4. 25-mm / light-AA terminal engagement

This is the more dangerous layer for actual strafers.

Exposure:
- 18–24 principal airfield strafers;
- 4–8 suppression/opportunity attackers;
- some Japanese fighters and smoke/ground clutter complicate aim.

Branch improvement:
- local sector assignment;
- hold fire until useful range;
- less wasteful early shooting;
- better ammunition/crew protection;
- revetted positions survive first .50-cal passes more often.

Type 96 limitations remain:
- short useful range;
- magazine changes;
- vibration;
- traverse/elevation;
- exposed crew;
- no magical lead computation.

Selected light-AA effect:

### irreversible / later write-off attributable to terminal fire
**2–3**

### additional repairable damage / abort
**3–6**

The F6F's robustness converts a meaningful share of hits into:
- damaged return;
rather than immediate loss.

# 11. Threshold result

The first sweep achieves:
- local U.S. tactical air superiority;
- serious Japanese fighter attrition;
- some ground-aircraft destruction;
- suppression of some light AA.

It does NOT achieve:
- Tinian airfield shutdown;
- destruction of the heavy AA network;
- destruction of coastal batteries;
- elimination of Tinian fighter reserve;
- elimination of radar/C2.

Therefore the old implicit state:
> “fighter sweep essentially clears Tinian for subsequent operations”

is too strong under the Branch.

Correct:
> **fighter sweep wins the first air battle, but leaves a wounded, still-functioning island-defense system for the bomber wave to attack.**

- neuropsychiatric combat casualties without visible penetrating trauma;

## 5. Neuropsychiatric / non-visible casualty layer

This layer is real but must be kept separate from the physical casualty total.

World War II U.S. medical practice often used **combat exhaustion** as an initial forward-area label.
Many acute cases returned after rest / sedation / reconditioning; some did not.
The event occurs after several days of continuous Saipan combat, so prior fatigue lowers resilience.

### Formally recognized acute combat-exhaustion / neuropsychiatric cases
Selected:
**~35–70 additional men**
center ~50.

These are men without a penetrating wound severe enough to explain the incapacity, but whose behavior / cognition makes continued duty unsafe or impossible.

Typical observed states may include:
- uncontrolled tremor;
- mutism or marked speech disturbance;
- freezing / inability to act;
- panic / uncontrolled flight behavior;
- confusion / disorientation;
- severe insomnia followed by functional collapse;
- persistent vomiting / autonomic symptoms without a surgical injury;
- dissociative-looking or stuporous states;
- impaired judgment / inability to perform a technical task.

Do not retroactively diagnose all of them with a modern chronic disorder.

Selected disposition of these 35–70:
- **~20–40** return to some duty in ~1–3 days after rest / treatment;
- **~10–20** require longer removal / evacuation;
- **~5–10** become persistent / recurrent cases during the near-term campaign and are unlikely to return to their former combat function promptly.

These bands are not additive at the endpoints beyond the 35–70 total.

### Unrecorded / sub-threshold functional degradation
In addition:
**~70–140 personnel class** can be temporarily degraded without appearing as a formal casualty.

Examples:
- slowed reaction;
- exaggerated startle;
- inability to sleep;
- impaired concentration;
- repeated checking / hesitation;
- poor memory for orders;
- emotional numbing;
- short episodes of freezing or confusion;
- loss of confidence in exposed gun / road positions.

Most recover enough to continue duty.
Some later become formal medical cases.
Some remain undocumented.

This is a **combat-efficiency debit**, not a casualty-roll addition.

## 6. Blast / hearing / concussion cases with little visible trauma

Heavy naval shells create another bookkeeping problem:
a man can look externally intact and still be temporarily or permanently impaired.

Selected hidden/low-visible injury sensitivity:
**~30–70 class** among men in the hardest-hit boxes.

Possible effects:
- concussion / mild traumatic brain injury;
- tympanic membrane injury;
- tinnitus;
- temporary or lasting hearing loss;
- disequilibrium / vertigo;
- blast lung in rarer close exposures;
- cognitive slowing / headache / photophobia.

Important accounting rule:
- many of these are already inside the **light/moderate WIA** categories if medically recognized;
- some overlap the combat-exhaustion presentation;
- **do not add 30–70 on top of every other total**.

Selected unrecognized / initially unreported subset:
**~10–30**.
These men may remain on duty while performing below normal and only be identified later, if at all.

## 7. Functional artillery / support effect through time

The human losses amplify the equipment losses from file 68.

### ~18:20–18:50 — peak heavy impact
In the hardest-hit artillery / support boxes:
- effective fire output can fall **~60–80%** for short intervals;
- some individual batteries are completely silent;
- silence comes from cover + casualties + observation loss + ammunition / prime-mover disruption, not just destroyed guns.

Across the broader Aslito west/north support system:
- fire output reduction centers around **~30–45%** during the main heavy phase.

### ~18:50–19:30 — attempted U.S. reconstitution under continuing fire
Hardest-hit boxes:
- remain roughly **~40–60% below normal useful output** while crews / FDC / wire / towing are reorganized.

Broader sector:
- centers roughly **~20–35% below normal**.

The key mechanism is that Type-3 / CA / Japanese island fire can attack the process of recovery itself.

### ~19:30–20:15 — partial recovery, Japanese exploitation window
Some batteries return.
Others remain:
- physically destroyed;
- crew-short;
- without observation;
- without ammunition movement;
- without prime movers;
- displaced onto rougher data.

Broader sector immediate useful artillery capacity remains roughly:
**~10–25% below the no-bombardment case**,
with local boxes worse.

This is sufficient to create the Japanese 45–90 min regeneration / exploitation window without claiming permanent U.S. artillery paralysis.

### After ~20:15
The U.S. system continues to recover.
But the dead, severe WIA, destroyed guns, lost vehicles and missing specialists do not regenerate overnight.

The persistent debit into the night is therefore:
- fewer experienced gun crews / observers / signals personnel;
- 6–10 guns physically / long-term out;
- 12–20 additional guns delayed for shorter/medium intervals;
- 35–60 soft/service vehicles damaged or destroyed;
- disrupted command / observation chains;
- a fatigue / neuropsychiatric tail.

## 8. Operational meaning

The bombardment does **not** destroy the U.S. Saipan force.

It does something narrower and operationally valuable:

1. kills roughly a hundred-plus men outright or shortly afterward;
2. removes roughly another hundred severe wounded from the campaign;
3. removes several hundred more for treatment lasting from days to weeks;
4. temporarily degrades another large light-wound / concussion cohort;
5. produces dozens of recognized combat-exhaustion casualties and a larger undocumented fatigue/stress tail;
6. disproportionately hits the specialists needed to turn guns, vehicles and communications back into a functioning fire-support system;
7. creates a temporary interval in which Japanese ground batteries recover faster than the U.S. support system does.

This is why “casualty count” alone understates the effect.
The same 600 casualties spread randomly through tens of thousands of men would matter less than 600 concentrated around artillery / FDC / communications / towing / road nodes.

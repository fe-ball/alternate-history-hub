## 1. Question

Does adding Fuso / Yamashiro materially attract the U.S. third anti-surface wave away from Yamato / Musashi / Nagato / Mutsu?

Selected answer:

**YES, but only moderately.**

They are not higher strategic-value hulls than Yamato-class.
They are, however, high **immediate mission-value / low disable-cost** targets because:
- they carry twelve 35.6-cm guns each;
- they are closing toward a Saipan relief / bombardment operation;
- their 24–25 kt class speed is enough for the mission but leaves less maneuver / damage margin;
- their organic AA / combat-system realization is weaker than the best Japanese capital-ship boxes;
- one torpedo / steering / machinery casualty has a higher chance of removing them from the current-night timetable even if the ship survives.

The U.S. objective is Saipan protection, not capital-ship prestige scoring.

## 3. Why not ignore Fuso/Yamashiro

If the mission were “destroy the Japanese battle fleet,” Yamato/Musashi would dominate value.

But the 20-Jun third-wave mission is closer to:
**prevent heavy Japanese surface units from reaching / supporting the Saipan relief battle.**

For shore bombardment:
- 24.7 kt vs 25.7 kt is nearly irrelevant after arrival;
- twelve 35.6-cm guns remain a large fire-support threat;
- old armor does not make them harmless.

Thus an old BB that can be mission-killed with one or two underwater hits can have better short-horizon expected value than dropping bombs on Yamato and merely damaging topside equipment.

Therefore Fuso/Yamashiro attract a meaningful share, not the entire third wave.

## 6. Weapon logic

Japanese heavy surface combatants are difficult to sink with bombs alone.

For battleship mission kill:
- torpedo / underwater damage is more valuable than simply accumulating topside bomb hits;
- bombs remain useful for AA / directors / communications / fire / maneuver disruption.

Selected U.S. allocation therefore biases a larger fraction of available TBM torpedo effort toward L2/L1, especially L2.

Dive bombers are split more broadly between:
- slow line;
- H1/H2;
- escorts / opportunity targets.

No assumption that every TBM carries / successfully drops a torpedo survives terminal combat.

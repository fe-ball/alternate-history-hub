# Marianas local aviation ferry / regeneration network — 14 to 18 June

Date: 2026-09-26
Status: SELECTED WORKING SYSTEM LEDGER / NO AUTHORITY PROMOTION

## 0. Correction

Japanese Marianas air strength is not a one-way attrition counter.

Within the island chain, physically short ferry distances allow:
- aircraft to move away from the most threatened runway;
- repaired aircraft to move to the node with fuel/crew/ordnance;
- crews / specialists to be recombined with available airframes;
- carrier-qualified aircraft to move between shore nodes and later back west.

This does not create aircraft.
It changes where serviceable aircraft become mission-ready.

## 1. Network nodes

Primary:
- Saipan
- Tinian
- Guam

Secondary:
- Rota
- Pagan / other emergency nodes where physically usable
- water-air anchorages / lagoons

Distances are operationally short compared with aircraft endurance.
The limiting factors are:
- runway/sea-state;
- U.S. CAP/search;
- fuel;
- navigation;
- receiving maintenance;
- timing / warning.

## 2. Ferry modes

### Pre-dawn / dusk cell transfer
- **2–6 aircraft per movement cell**
- low/medium altitude / weather routing where useful
- used to balance attack/defense/recon requirements.

### Night ferry
- only qualified crews;
- lower U.S. fighter threat but greater navigation/landing risk;
- do not assume universal night capability.

### Water-air relocation
- runway-independent;
- depends on anchorage, tenders/boats, fuel and sea state.

## 3. Daily working throughput

Under heavy U.S. pressure:
- roughly **6–12 aircraft-class physical relocations/day** across the Marianas network is a reasonable center workload;
- this is gross movement, not net reinforcement;
- the same aircraft moved twice is two movements but one airframe.

During a severe suppression day the number can fall sharply.

## 4. 14/15 June use

Before D-Day:
- local command can pre-position a small attack/recon cell toward Tinian/Guam based on invasion confirmation;
- selected physical redistribution **3–6 aircraft-class** before the 15-Jun morning attack;
- no change to regional total MR.

This helps explain how a bounded morning anti-shipping package can launch from viable nodes without requiring every type permanently based there.

## 5. 15-Jun day use

After morning attack:
- surviving aircraft do not all return to the same launch node if one field is under heavier pressure;
- damaged but flyable aircraft may divert to the better repair node;
- observation aircraft can stage between Tinian/Saipan-side locations.

Selected gross movements:
- **4–8 aircraft-class** during the day/evening.

Effect:
- reduces probability that one U.S. strike catches the entire local attack/recon pool;
- improves repair-to-MR conversion at the cost of receiving-base congestion.

## 6. 15/16 night

Priority:
- pair available aircraft with rested crews;
- move selected D4Y/water-air to nodes needed for 16-Jun ISR;
- move repairable aircraft toward the better maintenance/fuel node;
- do not ferry every aircraft toward Saipan.

Selected gross movement:
- **4–7 aircraft-class**.

## 7. Relation to Bonins replacement

Bonins -> Marianas physical arrival 10–16 and immediate MR 6–11 is a separate inflow.

Ferry network decides where that MR is most useful:
- mainly Tinian/Guam;
- some can subsequently redistribute.

Do not count ferry relocation as another reinforcement.

## 8. Airpower preservation effect

The network changes attrition conversion:

Without network:
- a runway strike can turn flyable aircraft into trapped ground losses / long mission-kills.

With network:
- warning + short ferry can move a subset before closure;
- repairs can be concentrated;
- mission-ready aircraft can migrate toward functioning bases.

Selected effect:
- **2–5 aircraft-class/day** can remain or become operationally useful that would otherwise be delayed/trapped, conditional on warning and receiving capacity.

This is relative availability, not physical new production.

## 9. U.S. counter

Once the U.S. recognizes inter-island air movement:
- CAP / sweep routes can cover likely ferry corridors;
- Tinian/Guam/Rota suppression has more value;
- radar/visual pickets can report low-level ferry traffic.

Thus ferry is not safe.

The U.S. can pay fighter sorties to reduce Japanese redistribution freedom.

## 10. Ground-battle interaction

Ferry/regeneration supports the ground battle by keeping:
- water-air observation;
- D4Y photo;
- liaison;
- counter-observer sorties

alive even after one runway is suppressed.

Therefore Japanese air observation should decay with cumulative losses, but not disappear simply because Saipan runway operations are interrupted.

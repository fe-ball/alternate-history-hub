# 0. Historical anchor

Historical TF58:
- had originally planned the first Marianas carrier attack for dawn 12 Jun;
- arrived early;
- launched the opening fighter sweep at ~1300 on 11 Jun instead;
- deliberately changed the usual dawn pattern to obtain tactical surprise;
- launched ~208–225 fighters plus guide/navigation aircraft from roughly 200+ miles east of the Marianas.

Historical outcome:
- the attack achieved substantial tactical surprise;
- Japanese fighters did get airborne and AA did engage;
- U.S. losses were ~11 Hellcats, with official/USN retrospective material noting many losses to ground fire.

Therefore reject both extremes:

Wrong:
> Japan has no warning and aircraft remain casually parked until strafed.

Wrong:
> Japan knows the exact 1300 launch and has every fighter airborne at altitude before contact.

The correct problem is:
> **high strategic alert + imperfect target information + finite tactical radar warning.**

# 2. Alert-state definitions

## ALERT-0 — routine
Not applicable on 11 Jun.

## ALERT-1 — theater warning
Enemy carrier movement suspected / known but no attack-quality local cue.

Actions:
- radar continuous watch;
- airfield watch increased;
- selected fighter readiness;
- routine dispersal maintained;
- AA crews available but not continuously at full battle stations.

## ALERT-2 — attack expected today
Selected Tinian dawn/forenoon state.

Actions:
- standing CAP rotation;
- fighter alert sections fueled / armed;
- second-line fighters prepared but crews rested near aircraft;
- vulnerable aircraft dispersed off main apron;
- towing tractors assigned;
- fuel bowsers / ammunition carts broken into smaller groups;
- maintenance prioritizes “return to fly now” aircraft;
- AA plotting / telephone circuits checked;
- heavy-AA crews on short stand-to;
- 25-mm positions manned by rotation;
- alternate runway / recovery surfaces checked;
- radar / visual reports use numbered tracks and timestamps.

This is a sustainable hours-long posture.

## ALERT-3 — inbound raid probable
Triggered by:
- radar track;
- visual/search report;
- correlated fast inbound formation.

Actions:
- first alert fighters scramble;
- standing CAP is vectored;
- second scramble order prepared;
- all AA to battle stations;
- runway operations prioritized for defense;
- exposed aircraft tow/dispersal accelerates;
- fuel transfer on exposed apron stops;
- ammunition / fuel vehicles leave obvious concentration points;
- repair crews clear vulnerable surfaces;
- coast/DP batteries enter AA priority.

## ALERT-4 — raid terminal
Formation class / sector sufficiently clear.

Actions:
- second scramble only if runway/time allows;
- no attempt to launch every serviceable aircraft;
- 120/75-mm batteries accept assigned altitude/sector tracks;
- 25-mm/local automatic weapons hold for terminal envelope;
- towing across active runway stops;
- crews shelter where aircraft cannot be moved;
- fire/rescue/repair parties stage under cover;
- alternate strip/recovery plan becomes active.

# 4. What can physically be accomplished with 25–40 min warning?

Credible:
- launch prepared fighter sections;
- vector existing CAP;
- man all principal AA;
- swing DP guns into AA priority;
- tow some exposed aircraft short distances;
- stop fuel/ammo concentration activity;
- move vehicles;
- put repair/fire crews under cover;
- close hangar doors / disperse local ground personnel;
- activate alternate communication / recovery plan.

Not credible:
- tow every aircraft into a new revetment;
- move a fixed heavy AA battery;
- empty large fuel stores;
- construct new defenses;
- rearm every attack aircraft as a fighter-defense asset;
- launch every MR aircraft;
- move aircraft under major repair;
- completely conceal activity from U.S. reconnaissance.

# 5. Fighter-readiness structure — no exact count frozen yet

Do not yet freeze exact Tinian fighter numbers.

Use readiness layers:

### F-A — standing CAP
Already airborne before raid warning.

Role:
- first contact;
- classify / delay;
- preserve altitude where possible;
- do not chase decoys far from base.

### F-B — first alert scramble
Engines / crews / weapons at immediate readiness.

Expected to contribute to first major merge if warning is ~25–40 min.

### F-C — second scramble
Requires:
- runway capacity;
- engine start / warm-up;
- taxi;
- warning time.

Likely joins later / lower / fragmented if warning is short.

### F-D — serviceable reserve
Not automatically airborne in first 20–30 min.

Reasons:
- maintenance;
- fuel;
- crew;
- other mission assignment;
- dispersed location;
- deliberate preservation.

This structure is consistent with file92's regional discussion:
- standing CAP 45–60;
- immediate alert 60–80;
- second join 35–50
across the wider Marianas system,
without pre-booking Tinian's exact share.

## 25-mm
At ALERT-3:
- crews manned;

- avoid long wasteful firing at impossible range;
- priority:
  - strafers;
  - low/medium attack aircraft;
  - aircraft committed to second pass.

Branch fire discipline primarily improves:
**opportunity use and ammunition economy.**

# 7. Ground-protection initial action

Branch dispersal means the main apron is not the whole aviation system.

At raid warning:
- aircraft already in revetments stay;
- exposed high-value aircraft get first tow priority;
- repairable aircraft on open maintenance hardstands are moved only if tow-ready;
- fuel / ordnance vehicles disperse;
- crews leave obvious hangar/apron clusters;
- decoys / empty prepared areas remain visible enough to consume U.S. target attention.

No automatic aircraft-loss credit is booked here.

# 8. Japanese command intent on first raid

The rational defense is not:
> win an air battle at any cost.

It is:

1. prevent a free strafing massacre;
2. preserve enough fighters for later raids;
3. keep at least one launch/recovery surface functioning;
4. protect radar / C2 / maintenance;
5. make U.S. aircraft pay for repeated low-altitude target work;
6. keep Tinian usable as a regional node after the first sweep.

Therefore fighters should not all be committed to one high-altitude furball if U.S. groups split among:
- fighter sweep;
- strafing;
- reconnaissance / guide elements.

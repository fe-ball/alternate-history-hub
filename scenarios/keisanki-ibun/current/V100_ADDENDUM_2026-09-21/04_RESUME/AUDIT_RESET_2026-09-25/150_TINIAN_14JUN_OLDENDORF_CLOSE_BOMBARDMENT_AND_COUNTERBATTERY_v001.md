# 0. Historical hard anchors

On 14 Jun:
- the old battleships, cruisers and destroyers of the Joint Expeditionary Force took over much of the close shore-bombardment mission;
- their shore-bombardment training and combat experience were materially better than the fast battleship force's;
- Fire Support Unit Seven bombarded Tinian's west coast throughout the day;
- Fire Support Unit Eight watched Tinian's east coast to protect adjacent U.S. units from Japanese shore batteries;
- Pennsylvania operated off northeastern Tinian with a specific mission to neutralize batteries that could fire on the Saipan landing area;
- Terry's Tinian task unit had the dual mission of silencing northern Tinian guns and rendering Ushi Point airfield unusable.

Historical resistance remained real:
- Tinian batteries opened fire across the channel around the Saipan UDT/support operation;
- Cleveland was straddled;
- California and Braine were hit;
- contemporary reporting therefore proves that dedicated 14-Jun suppression did **not** produce complete neutralization.

Use this as a hard contradiction against any model where the Tinian heavy-gun system is simply erased before D-Day.

# 1. Why 14 Jun is more effective than 13 Jun

Compared with Lee's 13-Jun fast-battleship bombardment:

## Better
- closer firing geometry;
- more shore-bombardment experience;
- slower, more methodical adjustment;
- tighter target bookkeeping;
- repeated point-target engagement;
- more willingness to expose fire-support ships to coast-defense range.

## Still constrained
- Saipan is the main invasion objective;
- Tinian receives a bounded but important share of fire-support effort;
- target identification remains imperfect;
- caves / revetments / camouflage survive near misses;
- mobile guns / alternate OPs can shift;
- counterbattery firing reveals Japanese positions only after they fire.

Thus 14 Jun is:
**the first genuinely systematic Tinian suppression day**
but not:
**the day Tinian is physically disarmed.**

# 7. Japanese counterbattery behavior

Branch Tinian should not continuously duel U.S. battleships.

Selected doctrine:
- remain silent while U.S. shells are searching known positions;
- fire short bursts when:
  - UDT/support ships enter registered boxes;
  - cruisers/DD close;
  - landing-support geometry becomes favorable;
- shift observation / communications after counterbattery response.

This uses the Branch advantage correctly:
**precomputed firing data reduces the need for prolonged ranging fire.**

It does not create invisible guns.

# 11. 14/15-night repair and reactivation

This is where Branch fortification matters most.

During darkness:
- wire is respliced;
- alternate OPs assume sectors;
- ready ammunition moved;
- some 25-mm positions remanned;
- mobile 75-mm guns shift;
- suppressed fixed-gun crews reoccupy surviving emplacements.

Return clocks:
- simple wire/observation interruption:
  **tens of minutes to ~2 h**
- crew shelter / local power / access:
  **1–4 h**
- significant emplacement damage:
  **4–12 h / next night**
- direct mount kill:
  **does not return**

Therefore:
a battery marked SUPPRESSED at 1500 on 14 Jun is not assumed silent at 0700 on 15 Jun.

# 13. Main finding

14 Jun is materially more effective than 13 Jun.

But the proper result is:

> **U.S. close bombardment suppresses Tinian heavily and damages the northern defense complex, yet the very fact that Tinian batteries historically scored hits during this suppression proves that destruction and neutralization are not the same thing.**

Branch fortification and precomputed fire data widen that distinction.

The D-Day problem therefore becomes:
- fewer guns firing at once;
- shorter firing windows;
- but repeated reappearance from a still-coherent second fire node.

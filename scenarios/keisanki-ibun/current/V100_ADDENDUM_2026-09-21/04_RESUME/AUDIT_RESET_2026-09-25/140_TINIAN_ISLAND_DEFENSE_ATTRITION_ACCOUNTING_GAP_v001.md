but do not always close these causes separately under the post-91 defense state.

# 2. Why the island contribution matters

A stronger Japanese fighter wallet can increase U.S. losses directly.

But a stronger island also changes the air battle indirectly:

- fighters can operate under denser local AA;
- attackers must spend more time or altitude outside ideal attack geometry;
- some U.S. escorts are tied to CAP/sweep instead of attack escort;
- repeated passes against protected/dispersed targets increase exposure;
- target dispersion lowers destruction per sortie;
- AA remains dangerous even after some fighters are depleted.

Thus:
> **the island is not merely a static target behind the air battle.**
>
> **It is part of the air-combat system.**

# 5. Most likely direction of correction

Do not assume every reopened number moves in Japan's favor.

Likely tendencies:

### U.S. losses
Probably somewhat higher where:
- more fighters survive;
- AA is stronger / more persistent;
- protected targets require deeper exposure.

### Japanese aircraft losses
Could also be higher in some windows because:
- more Japanese aircraft actually participate;
- more are exposed in combat;
- U.S. F6F/CIC remains strong.

### Japanese ground losses
Likely lower irreversible destruction ratio:
- more dispersed;
- more revetted;
- more repairable;
- more alternate surfaces.

### U.S. target effect
More often converts from:
- destroyed
to:
- damaged / suppressed / delayed / displaced.

Thus the key change may be:
**higher mutual air attrition + lower U.S. destruction efficiency against the island system.**

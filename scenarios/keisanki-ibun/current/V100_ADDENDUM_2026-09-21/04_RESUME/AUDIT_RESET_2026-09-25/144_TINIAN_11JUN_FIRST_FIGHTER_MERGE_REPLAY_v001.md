# 2. Tactical doctrine

Japanese Branch doctrine does NOT center:
- prolonged horizontal turning contest;
- all fighters charging the first visible F6F group.

Use:

### F-A
- preserve altitude;
- slash / identify;
- force U.S. top cover to commit;
- avoid chasing eastward.

### Ki-44 / high-energy layer
- attack climbing / separated U.S. elements;
- one-pass / zoom;
- re-enter only with energy.

### Zero-family
- protect lower airfield zone;
- exploit U.S. aircraft that descend / overshoot / separate;
- avoid trying to run from F6F in level flight.

### F-C
- enter lower / later;
- attack U.S. elements committed to strafing or low-altitude pursuit;
- preserve some fighters for follow-on raid.

U.S.:
- retain altitude reserve;
- use mutual support;
- refuse low-speed turning traps;
- exploit dive / speed / armor;
- attack Japanese climb stream where possible.

# 6. Exchange interpretation

Irreversible fighter exchange:
roughly **1.8–2.5 Japanese fighters lost per F6F** in the Tinian air-to-air phase.

This is far better for Japan than historical June-1944 Marianas fighter combat,
but still a U.S.-favorable exchange.

Why:
- warning and altitude remove much of the historical free bounce;
- Branch Japanese fighters are faster / better protected / better controlled;
- experienced leaders are deeper;
- Tinian provides short recovery;

but:
- F6F remains highly competitive or superior in the decisive high-speed regime;
- U.S. formation/radio discipline is stronger;
- U.S. pilots can disengage more safely;
- numerical depth remains on the U.S. side.

# 8. Important coupling to the next phase

The old historical logic:
> win fighter sweep -> freely strafe airfield

does not apply cleanly here.

The U.S. now enters a second decision:

1. stay high and continue fighter suppression;
2. descend to strafe aircraft / runway support;
3. attack AA / radar / coast-battery targets;
4. disengage before fuel/ammunition margin erodes.

Descending creates:
- 120/75-mm exposure during approach where geometry permits;
- 25-mm exposure in terminal passes;
- opportunity for surviving Japanese low fighters / F-D launch.

Therefore the island-defense phase may produce a meaningful fraction of total U.S. losses.

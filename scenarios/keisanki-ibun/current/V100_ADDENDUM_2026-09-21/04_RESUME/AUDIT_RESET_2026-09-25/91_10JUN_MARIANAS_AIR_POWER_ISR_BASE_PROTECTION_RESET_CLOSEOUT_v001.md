## 5. Base protection taxonomy

Never collapse “aircraft capacity” and “aircraft protection.”

Track separately:

### P0 — raw parking
Aircraft can physically be parked.  
No meaningful blast/fragment protection.

### P1 — dispersed parking
Satellite aprons, taxi-edge parking, separated parking areas, decoys, towing routes.  
Reduces concentration loss; not a hardened shelter.

### P2 — revetment
Earth/stone/coral blast walls and separated pens.  
Reduces fragmentation, strafing, fire spread and adjacent-aircraft loss.  
Does not make an aircraft safe from a direct bomb hit.

### P3 — covered / cut-in hide
Terrain-cut, camouflaged, partially roofed/covered or deeper protected aircraft hide.  
Higher protection from observation, fragments and near misses; materially more engineer effort.

### P4 — hardened aircraft shelter
Heavy reinforced/protected shelter.  
Rare and expensive. Do not give this to the whole air force.

### Hangars
Maintenance/handling capacity, not automatically protective shelters.  
A large visible hangar can be an excellent target.

### Selected 10-Jun land-air protection mix

For land-based aircraft, working center distribution:

- raw exposed/main apron: **10–15%**
- dispersed exposed parking: **25–30%**
- revetment: **40–45%**
- covered/cut-in hide: **8–12%**
- hardened aircraft shelter: **1–3%**

For a ~550-aircraft land-based center this is approximately:
- ~70 exposed;
- ~150 dispersed-exposed;
- ~235 revetted;
- ~55 covered/cut-in;
- ~10 hardened.

These are protection states, not extra aircraft.

Protection priority:
1. ISR/special aircraft, photo/radio equipment and selected high-value cells;
2. spare engines / repair / communications / C2;
3. general fighter/attack aircraft by dispersal and revetment.

### Water-air protection is separate

Water-air aircraft use:

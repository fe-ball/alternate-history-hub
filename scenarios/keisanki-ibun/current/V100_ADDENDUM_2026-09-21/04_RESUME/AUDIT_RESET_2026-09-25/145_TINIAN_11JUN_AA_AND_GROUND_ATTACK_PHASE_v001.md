- +75 degree elevation;
- time-fuzed HE available.

Do not interpret this as U.S.-style radar-directed VT-fuze lethality.
Branch radar is warning / track support, not precision radar gunlaying.

# 2. Why only 24–30 descend

The 11-Jun package is a fighter sweep, not a dedicated heavy strike.

F6F .50-cal fire is effective against:
- aircraft;
- trucks;
- exposed personnel;
- fuel bowsers;
- light AA;
- radar antennas;
- thin structures.

It is poor at permanently destroying:
- protected 120-mm positions;
- revetted 75-mm batteries;
- deep ammunition;
- cave / cut-in positions;
- hardened command sites.

Therefore rational U.S. pilots do not spend repeated low passes against protected heavy guns merely because those guns exist.

The mission center is:
> **destroy aircraft / prevent rapid regeneration, then leave before fuel and AA exposure become disproportionate.**

# 3. Heavy / medium AA engagement

Relevant Branch Tinian:
- 120-mm DP: 10 physical island-wide, sector-constrained;
- 75-mm AA: 5–6 useful;
- 76.2-mm DP supplement;
- improved warning / track / fuze-setting discipline;
- no proximity fuze;
- no U.S.-equivalent radar gun director.

Engagement opportunities:

### approach / first merge
Heavy AA has only bounded freedom because:
- Japanese fighters occupy the same airspace;
- friendly-fire / identification concerns;
- geometry changes rapidly.

Selected:
**limited / intermittent fire**, not a full uninterrupted barrage.

### post-merge descent / attack setup
Better opportunity:
- U.S. groups become easier to classify;
- surviving Japanese fighters are lower / displaced;
- attack axes become clearer.

### egress
One additional medium/high-altitude opportunity as U.S. aircraft reform and leave.

Selected heavy/medium-AA result against the Tinian package:

- F6F irreversible: **1–2**
- additional damaged / forced-abort: **2–4**

Main effect:
- break attack runs;
- force altitude / heading changes;
- discourage repeated pass.

# 4. 25-mm / terminal layer

Ground-attack pool:
**24–30 F6F**

Not all enter every 25-mm envelope.

Branch improvements:
- crews already at stations;
- local sectors assigned;
- better ammunition discipline;
- revetted positions survive near misses more often;
- fire held until useful range rather than opened wastefully at long range.

Historical limitations remain:
- 15-round magazines;
- reload interruption;
- vibration;
- mediocre sights;
- poor high-speed tracking;
- limited effective range.

Therefore:
the Type-96 does not become a Japanese Bofors.

But against:
- low strafers;
- aircraft committed to a straight firing pass;
- second-pass aircraft,
it remains dangerous.

Selected terminal-AA result:

- F6F irreversible: **1–3**
- additional damaged / mission-abort: **3–6**

Most kills occur on:
- committed low-level attack;
- pull-out;
- second pass,
not on high cover.

- fuel bowsers;
- trucks;
- workshops;
- radio/radar antenna;
- parked aircraft;
- exposed maintenance.

They cannot crater a runway in the manner of bombers.

Selected 11-Jun first-sweep effects:

**first sweep reduces sortie-generation efficiency but does not close Tinian air operations.**

# 8. AA / battery damage from F6F strafing

Ground-attack fighters can suppress exposed crews.

Selected:

## 25-mm
- **2–5 positions** temporarily suppressed / crew-degraded
- **0–2 mounts** irreversible / long-out center-range

## 75-mm
- crews may be suppressed;
- **0–1 gun long-out** center

## 120-mm
- no permanent gun loss center from .50-cal strafing
- local crew/communication suppression possible

## radar / command
- one local component / antenna damage sensitivity
- no full-network collapse center.

The heavy AA system therefore survives into the next raid unless bombed.

# 9. Second-pass decision

This is the key island-defense effect.

Without strong island defense:
U.S. pilots can make repeated strafing passes and convert damaged/dispersed aircraft into kills.

With current Tinian:
- surviving fighters remain;
- heavy AA still fires;
- terminal 25-mm remains distributed;
- fuel for a ~200-mile carrier recovery matters;
- formation cohesion is already degraded.

Selected:
- most G-ATTACK fighters make **one committed pass**;
- a minority make a second;
- no broad third-pass hunting.

This cuts U.S. ground-destruction conversion substantially.

The island's value is therefore larger than the 2–5 direct AA kills.

# 11. Operational interpretation

The 11-Jun U.S. result remains a tactical success:

- U.S. obtains local air superiority;
- Japanese first defense suffers heavy fighter loss;
- ground aircraft are destroyed/damaged;
- Tinian sortie generation drops.

But Branch correction changes the mechanism from:
> sweep destroys the island air force

to:
> **sweep wins the air fight, pays a serious AA bill, destroys only the exposed fraction, and leaves a defended repairable air-base system that must be hit again.**

This is exactly why 12–14 Jun target allocation matters.

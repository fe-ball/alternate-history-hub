## 0. Purpose

Recover the pre-invasion fortification work that was already established in current authority/reference material, then turn it into a combat-time ledger.

This file does NOT revive the cancelled post-dawn ground reconstruction.

The key distinction is:

- prewar/prelanding works create **prepared capacity**;
- combat destroys, exposes, bypasses or invalidates part of that capacity;
- nightly/daylight rework converts surviving material, survey, labor and terrain into a new useful defense.

No national "fortification multiplier" is used.

## 2. Ledger by function

### A. Coast/direct-fire belt

Pre-D-day:
- beach guns, MGs, AT weapons and observed-fire positions exist;
- selected alternate bays/positions are prepared;
- exposed positions deliberately do not contain the entire fire plan.

16-Jun-dawn state:
- primary exposed line = HEAVILY DEGRADED;
- surviving guns/weapons = dispersed local cells, short firing windows, fire-and-displace where physically possible;
- role shifts from "stop landing at waterline" to "delay movement into inland fire zones."

### B. Reverse-slope mortar/artillery system

Pre-D-day:
- alternate firing points and map grids prepared in priority sectors;
- beach exits, road junctions, likely artillery/armor assembly areas and choke lanes pre-registered where observation/survey permits;
- ammunition not held only at one waterfront dump.

16-Jun-dawn:
- majority of the useful value is still inland;
- some observers/wire routes lost;
- old beach-registration data remains valid only for areas still tactically relevant.

### C. Command / signals / observation

Pre-D-day:
- duplicate/alternate command posts;
- multiple wire routes;
- common grids/target numbers for Army–Navy combat I/O;
- alternate power/radio arrangements.

Combat rule:
- "HQ alive" is not enough;
- each sector separately tracks:
  - primary CP;
  - alternate CP;
  - at least one usable report path;
  - one or more surviving observation sources.

A destroyed OP can make an intact battery temporarily tactically blind.

### D. Logistics / water / ammunition

Pre-D-day:
- stores subdivided by defense sector;
- inland/mountain/rock storage in selected areas;
- roads remain primary tactical circulation;
- narrow-gauge rail used for bulk/time-insensitive movement where track survives.

Combat rule:
- cache survival != accessibility;
- each sector tracks:
  - physical stock;
  - route open/closed;
  - handling manpower;
  - observation/fire exposure.

### E. Armor

Approved 16-Jun dawn:
- 9th Tank Regiment MR ~35–40.

Doctrine retained:
- 1–3 and 3–6 vehicle cells;
- multiple hide/firing positions;
- flank fire against halted U.S. armor;
- no mass daylight charge.

Combat rework:
- every significant front shift requires new:
  - covered approach;
  - hull-down/partial-defilade point;
  - escape route;
  - range card;
  - infantry/mortar coordination.

### F. Engineer/rework capacity

Retained Branch advantages:
- better surveying/quantity takeoff/work sequencing;
- more reliable plant/tools;
- better delivered construction material than historical same-date;
- preassigned work packages and joint priority tables.

Hard limit:
- combat does NOT create new deep tunnel systems in a night.

During battle, engineers can realistically:
- repair/redirect wire;
- clear or crater routes;
- rebuild overhead cover;
- extend existing cave mouths/traverses locally;
- reopen blocked entrances;
- dig new field positions;
- emplace mines/obstacles;
- move ammunition/water;
- prepare alternate firing points;
- restore drainage/pumps/power locally.

## 3. Re-survey / fire-data rule

Prepared firing data ages when the front moves.

Each sector maintains a timestamped ground-fire board:
- friendly forward line;
- known enemy line;
- no-fire / friendly-risk boxes;
- target number;
- source of location;
- last observation time;
- weapon family able to engage;
- confidence.

### Expiry triggers

A target solution becomes STALE when:
- U.S. line changes materially;
- a known assembly area disperses;
- observation post is lost;
- road/bridge is cratered or rerouted;
- friendly troops infiltrate into the old target box.

### Re-registration

A surviving indirect-fire cell can rebuild useful firing data through:
- surveyed terrain reference;
- forward observer;
- patrol report;
- flash/sound report;
- tank/infantry report;
- repeated U.S. traffic pattern.

No "map coordinate = current target" shortcut.

Selected work standard:
- same-night priority is **1–3 new high-value target boxes per surviving fire cell**, not complete remapping of the front;
- higher priority: artillery positions, tank assembly, beach/road exits, seam/boundary gaps, supply traffic, command/aid concentrations;
- low-priority old beach targets are allowed to expire.

## 4. Field-position rework clock

### 0–2 hours
- weapon displacement to an existing alternate;
- emergency wire reroute;
- ammo/water redistribution inside a sector;
- local mine/obstacle closure;
- update range cards from known landmarks.

### 2–8 hours
- new shallow weapon pits / shell-hole conversion;
- overhead splinter cover;
- new MG/mortar lateral links;
- road/rail-cut ambush points;
- alternate OP;
- local cave-entrance reopening;
- tank hide/firing route preparation.

### 8–24 hours
- stronger field revetment;
- more complete traverse/covered approach;
- expanded existing rock position;
- larger route repair/demolition;
- relocation of heavier ammunition/maintenance nodes.

### Multi-day
- meaningful new gallery/tunnel network;
- major concrete work;
- large underground fuel/ammunition relocation.

These cannot be credited overnight.

## 5. Priority work boards after 16-Jun dawn

### Afetna seam
Priority:
1. restore cross-boundary observation and wire;
2. register U.S. artillery / reserve movement behind the seam;
3. preserve inland fire wedge;
4. prepare 1–3 vehicle tank cells for flank fire, not countercharge;
5. create fallback positions if the west edge is lost.

### Susupe / Chalan Kanoa east exits
Priority:
1. mortar/MG fire on road exits and causeways;
2. tank ambushes against Sherman movement;
3. rail/road movement for ammunition after dark;
4. new no-fire boxes as Japanese infiltrators operate near the U.S. line.

### Aslito approaches
Priority:
1. do not defend the entire runway edge equally;
2. survey west/north approach lanes;
3. prepare anti-armor crossfires and mortar concentrations;
4. pre-plan runway/taxiway denial/cratering where it does not block Japanese own movement prematurely;
5. move critical stores east/southeast of exposed airfield facilities;
6. prepare fallback fires that can interdict the airfield after physical loss.

### Southern/Agingan sector
Priority:
1. delay coastal expansion;
2. protect inland routes toward Aslito/Nafutan;
3. avoid trapping the remaining tanks against the coast;
4. retain observation of U.S. south-coast logistics.

### Red/Green/northern bridgehead
Priority:
1. preserve high-ground/reverse-slope fires;
2. prevent direct observation from being converted immediately into permanent neutralization;
3. shift from beach stopping to ravine/road/foothill delay.

## 6. Combat-work accounting rule

For every future ground interval, track separately:

- **Position physical** — exists/destroyed/bypassed;
- **Position usable** — weapon/crew/ammo/route/observation all present;
- **Fire data** — CURRENT / STALE / LOST;
- **C2** — primary / alternate / local autonomous;
- **Logistics** — stock vs accessible stock;
- **Engineer task** — queued / active / completed;
- **Armor cell** — physical / serviceable / crewed / MR / assigned;
- **U.S. support exposure** — naval gunfire / CVE CAS / ground artillery / tank.

This is the ground equivalent of physical -> serviceable -> crewed -> mission-ready accounting.

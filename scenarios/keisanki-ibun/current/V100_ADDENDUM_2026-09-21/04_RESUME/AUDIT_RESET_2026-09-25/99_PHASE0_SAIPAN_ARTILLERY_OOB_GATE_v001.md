# 1. Historical mobile-artillery anchor

U.S. wartime technical reporting gives Saipan mobile artillery as:
- 75-mm: **39**
- 105-mm: **8**
- 150-mm: **12**
- unidentified: **4**

The same reporting states:
- observation positions were well situated;
- registration had been completed before the landing.

A separate U.S. War Department amphibious-defense study reports **79 field-artillery pieces found** on Saipan.

Do not force these two numbers into one artificial total:
- the 39/8/12/4 count is the cleaner **mobile-artillery caliber breakdown**;
- the 79 figure may use a broader field-artillery classification including additional light/infantry/regimental pieces.

### Current use
For comparison:
- retain 39 / 8 / 12 / 4 as the historical mobile-artillery reference;
- keep 79 as a broader captured-field-artillery cross-check;
- exact Branch weapon-by-weapon mapping remains OPEN.

# 2. Historical named Army artillery structure

Official U.S. campaign OOB confirms:
- 3d Independent Mountain Artillery Regiment: **845 personnel**;
- 47th Independent Mixed Brigade artillery unit: **323 personnel**;
- 47th Brigade artillery organization included one field-gun battery and two howitzer batteries;
- additional divisional/regimental artillery pieces existed outside this brigade artillery unit.

A secondary detailed organizational reconstruction suggests:
- 3d Independent Mountain Artillery Regiment carried a 75-mm mountain-gun element plus 150-mm howitzer crews/weapons;
- III/10 Field Artillery contributed 75-mm field guns and 105-mm howitzers.

Exact piece allocation among every Army unit remains to be reconciled against the 39/8/12/4 captured/caliber totals.

Do not promote the secondary detailed weapon breakdown as authority yet.

---

# 3. Historical mobility failure

Historical U.S. reporting is unusually explicit:
- Saipan field artillery had inadequate horses / prime movers;
- road trucks were the principal non-manpower towing option;
- withdrawal therefore caused abandonment of many guns.

### Branch correction already justified
Branch state:
- more reliable trucks/tractors;
- better accompanying stores/spares;
- explicit artillery-towing priority;
- narrow-gauge rail used for bulk/time-insensitive movement;
- pre-surveyed alternate positions.

Therefore:
- field-artillery mobility shifts from **historical LOW/brittle** to **Branch MEDIUM/road-bound**;
- this improves displacement/recovery;
- it does not create cross-country mechanized artillery.

Exact prime-mover count remains OPEN but is not required to establish the qualitative direction.

# 4. The decisive fixed/naval-gun problem

U.S. War Department reporting after Saipan found a very large unfinished physical stock:
- **3 x 140-mm guns** still on railway cars;
- **23 x 120-mm dual-purpose guns** still in preservative/cosmoline;
- **36 coastal / dual-purpose pieces** still at Garapan naval depot.

Other historical reporting likewise describes:
- heavy weapons in depots;
- weapons near incomplete emplacements;
- searchlight / AA systems incompletely installed.

The same War Department study states that no coastal-defense or antiaircraft position was fully complete in the historical line.

This creates the largest Phase-0 danger.

---

# 5. Branch implication

The Branch has exactly the improvements needed to reduce this physical->usable gap:
- earlier A1 priority;
- more delivered construction material;
- engineer/fortification reinforcements;
- shipping/unloading specialists;
- more plant uptime;
- better survey and work sequencing;
- more complete optical/fire-control packages;
- better electrical/communications distribution;
- earlier 43d integration frees fewer emergency reorganization tasks in June.

Therefore it is **not plausible** to carry the historical installation failure unchanged.

But the exact conversion cannot be guessed.

For each candidate coast/DP battery, the audit must know:
- actual gun type;
- mount/pedestal availability;
- emplacement state;
- ammunition;
- crew;
- sights/rangefinder/director;
- electrical/power need;
- communications;
- ammunition handling;
- excavation/concrete/steel requirement;
- work-hours;
- firing arc and target value.

# 6. FREEZE decision

**Exact additional installed naval/coast/DP gun count = FREEZE.**

Reason:
installing even a modest fraction of the historically idle 3 + 23 + 36 stock may materially alter:
- prelanding naval bombardment;
- U.S. minesweeper/UDT/LCI/LVT exposure;
- landing-craft losses;
- fire-support ship standoff;
- D-day beach congestion;
- AA / local air-defense load;
- Japanese ammunition expenditure and survivability.

This is too consequential to insert as a small numerical correction.

### Until the fixed-gun sub-audit closes

Allowed:
- say Branch has a materially higher **installation/readiness conversion rate**;
- retain stronger fire-control/optical completeness for already-active batteries;
- retain better repair/recovery.

Not allowed:
- add a specific number of 120/140/150/200-mm firing guns;
- add D-day casualties from them;
- reduce U.S. naval gunfire effectiveness by an arbitrary percentage.

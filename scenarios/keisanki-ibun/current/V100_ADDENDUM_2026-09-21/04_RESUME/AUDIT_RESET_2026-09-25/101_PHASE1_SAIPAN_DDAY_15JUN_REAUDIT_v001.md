# 3. Boat mobility enters the replay, but not as a mass D-day attack

Phase 0 establishes that Saipan owns a real powered-craft/coastal-mobility system.

This must appear on 15 Jun.

However, D-day is a poor time for a large offensive counterlanding:
- U.S. amphibious/naval concentration is at maximum;
- western approaches are under intense observation/fire;
- the U.S. beachhead is still shallow and dense, giving Japanese land fires excellent targets;
- committing a large craft mass risks destroying a scarce future relief/reception wallet.

### Selected D-day craft policy

**Preservation-heavy, not inert.**

During daylight:
- disperse/hide craft where possible;
- move harbor/boat assets out of obvious exposed concentrations;
- local short utility movement only.

After dusk:
- limited small-craft coastal movement is allowed for:
  - signals / liaison;
  - wounded;
  - ammunition / medical;
  - small engineer parties;
  - selected local troop transfer between Japanese-held coastal nodes;
  - reconnaissance of future flank-insertion / reception lanes.

### Not selected on 15/16 night

- no 35-craft mass historical-style counterlanding;
- no brigade-scale “true” reverse landing;
- no Tinian mass reinforcement crossing by fiat.

Reason:
the current Afetna/Susupe land attack already has a useful land-route geometry and Japanese fire plan; the marginal value of risking the heavy boat reserve is not yet high enough.

This preserves a later OCL option.

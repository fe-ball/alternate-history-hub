Purpose:
close whether the current Saipan campaign under-models Tinian as an integrated artillery / observation node, and identify the four principal missing effects:
1. Tinian batteries in gun duels / anti-ship combat;
2. U.S. ship/air suppression effort diverted onto Tinian batteries;
3. target competition during U.S. Tinian air raids;
4. Tinian support to Japanese naval operations;
plus integration with the Mt Tapotchau / Saipan observation network.

---

# 0. Core finding

**YES. All four effects are under-modeled in the current Saipan working replay.**

The omission is not primarily a missing casualty multiplier.

It is a missing **second fire node**.

Current Saipan replay models:
- Saipan internal artillery / OP system;
- local aviation / water-air observation;
- Tinian aviation;
- late/final-operation Tinian fire.

But it does not consistently model through 15 Jun–early Jul:
> **Saipan fire system + Tinian protected battery system + shared air/water-air ISR + Army/Navy common grids**

as one archipelago fire-control problem faced by the U.S.

---

# 1. Historical scale of U.S. attention confirms the issue

Historical U.S. fire against Tinian was already a Saipan-campaign task, not merely Tinian invasion preparation.

From 20 Jun through 9 Jul, while the Saipan battle was still underway:
- XXIV Corps Artillery fired **331 missions / 7,571 rounds** onto Tinian;
- roughly one fifth of its own Saipan-period expenditure was directed south;
- observation aircraft flew daily over Tinian to register fire / collect target intelligence.

Therefore a campaign model that treats nearly all U.S. artillery/air/naval suppression effort as being directed only against Saipan necessarily overstates the support available for Saipan itself.

---

# 2. Missing effect A — Tinian battery gun duel / anti-ship combat

Tinian battery employment is not limited to firing at Saipan land targets.

Relevant target classes:
- Saipan Channel DD patrols;
- fire-support BB/CA/CL;
- NGFS ships close to Agingan/Nafutan/southern Saipan;
- landing/support craft where geometry permits;
- ships conducting Tinian suppression.

Historical Tennessee damage proves this interaction exists.

Branch T-BAT fortification makes the engagement less like:
> exposed battery vs overwhelming fleet fire

and more like:
> short firing pulse -> counterbattery -> protected silence -> later reappearance.

Operational effects:
- U.S. ships maneuver / alter station;
- ships expend counterbattery ammunition;
- spotters and radar/optical teams are tasked south;
- some NGFS windows against Saipan are interrupted or shortened;
- DD screen duty becomes a real Tinian-battery mission.

Do not center repeated capital-ship kills.
Center:
**station disruption + ammunition/time tax + occasional damaging hit.**

---

# 3. Missing effect B — U.S. naval/air suppression is divided

Current Saipan ground files correctly charge the U.S. for:
- Saipan artillery counterbattery;
- CVE suppression;
- NGFS;
- observer denial.

They insufficiently debit the same wallets for Tinian.

During Saipan battle the U.S. must also spend:
- DD / cruiser shore-fire;
- carrier/CVE strike sorties;
- later Saipan-based artillery;
- observation/recon sorties
on Tinian batteries and airfields.

Thus:
> **every Tinian suppression mission is capacity not available against Tapotchau, Saipan artillery, Saipan roads, or Japanese infantry.**

This does not eliminate U.S. material superiority.
It reduces the margin by which that superiority can be concentrated on Saipan.

---

# 4. Missing effect C — Tinian air raids have target competition

Existing air re-audit emphasizes:
- runway/apron;
- parked aircraft;
- repair/support;
- dispersal;
- AA.

But a Branch Tinian with protected artillery creates another high-priority target set:
- known 140/120/152-mm batteries;
- battery OPs;
- communications;
- ammunition;
- suspected cave apertures;
- dummy/alternate positions.

Therefore U.S. Tinian strikes face a target-allocation problem:

## strike airfields heavily
-> Tinian batteries survive more often;

## strike batteries heavily
-> more aircraft / support facilities survive and continue:
- CAP;
- ISR;
- artillery observation;
- ferry / repair.

This is a genuine nonlinear coupling.

Do NOT convert it into a universal reduction in U.S. bombing effectiveness.
Use:
**more target boxes competing for finite sorties.**

---

# 5. Missing effect D — Tinian as cover during naval / relief battles

Tinian batteries cannot protect Japanese carriers hundreds of miles west.

They can influence the **terminal / inner Marianas geometry**.

Useful tasks:
- cover Saipan Channel;
- fire on U.S. DD/CL/CA penetration routes;
- harass or displace local NGFS;
- engage ships suppressing Tinian;
- illuminate / report / provide bearing data;
- support the final approach / withdrawal of Japanese relief craft where arcs permit.

During Japanese fleet-created relief windows:
- U.S. light forces cannot treat Tinian shoreline as a passive flank;
- Tinian gunfire adds a fixed threat axis while U.S. attention is divided by Japanese surface units / aircraft.

Therefore the 20/21 and 25/26 naval-relief geometries should read:
> **Japanese fleet/screen + local Tinian shore-fire threat**
not:
> fleet/screen operating next to an inert island.

Again:
this is not free decisive fire.
U.S. counterbattery remains very strong.

---

# 6. Tapotchau–Tinian integration gap

Current file117 treats:
- Tapotchau / central high ground as the main late Saipan observation engine;
- aerial/Tinian assistance mainly after the central network collapses.

That is too sequential.

The correct Branch structure is partially overlapping.

## Tapotchau / Saipan high-ground layer
Best at:
- Saipan land-front shape;
- west/central road traffic;
- artillery flashes;
- local tactical changes;
- persistent observation where line of sight exists.

## Tinian layer
Best at:
- Saipan Channel;
- southern Saipan / Agingan-Nafutan sector;
- Aslito broad support activity;
- offshore support shipping;
- southern road / base movement;
- cross-island broad confirmation.

## air / water-air layer
Best at:
- reconciling the two pictures;
- checking blind zones;
- refreshing mobile-target boxes;
- BDA.

With Army/Navy common grids / target numbers:
- Tinian does not magically command Saipan Army batteries;
- Tapotchau does not directly control every Navy coast gun;
- but both can feed a shared target board / liaison path.

Therefore:
> **loss of one observation layer should degrade the fire system, not necessarily blind it.**

This strengthens the graceful-decay logic already applied to Saipan.

# 7. Why Tinian fortification has unusually high marginal value during the Saipan battle

Saipan fortification is progressively:
- overrun;
- bypassed;
- cut off;
- deprived of local routes;
- compressed northward.

Tinian fortification is not physically captured during the Saipan battle.

Therefore a protected Tinian battery / OP:
- retains geographic depth;
- retains its ammunition/crew base longer;
- can reappear repeatedly;
- keeps imposing a U.S. suppression bill even as Saipan positions disappear.

Thus per unit of prewar/Branch preparation:

> **Tinian battery-hardening can have a longer campaign-wide useful life than a comparable exposed Saipan position.**

This does not mean Tinian is the stronger island fortress overall.

It means:
**for the specific Saipan campaign, Tinian fire-node investment has unusually high marginal persistence.**

# 8. Immediate corrections to Saipan working interpretation

No full casualty replay yet.

Apply these corrections:

## 15 Jun
- Tinian anti-ship / shore-battery threat is active;
- a bounded fraction of U.S. fire support and air suppression is diverted south;
- do not add a new bridgehead-destruction effect.

## 16–19 Jun
- Saipan artillery suppression remains dominant U.S. effort;
- but Tinian batteries / airfields consume a persistent parallel support wallet;
- Aslito conversion delay gains an additional external cause.

## 20–26 Jun
- U.S. Saipan-based 155-mm fire onto Tinian becomes an explicit capacity diversion;
- Japanese relief windows gain Tinian as a bounded terminal-cover / observation layer;
- synchronize Tinian fire with relief where useful rather than wasting ammunition on continuous harassment.

## late Jun–5/6 Jul
- Tapotchau collapse does not equal total Japanese broad-target blindness;
- Tinian + air/water-air retains an external broad-observation layer;
- precision on mobile Saipan targets still decays sharply.

# 11. Working close

The present Saipan re-audit is therefore incomplete in one specific systemic sense:

> **Tinian has been modeled as an aviation/logistics node and a late fire-support cameo, but not strongly enough as a persistent protected fire-and-observation node throughout the Saipan battle.**

The likely correction is not a much stronger Japanese front.

It is:
> **less U.S. support concentration, more repeated suppression work, more resilient Japanese observation, and a more expensive Aslito/base-conversion clock.**

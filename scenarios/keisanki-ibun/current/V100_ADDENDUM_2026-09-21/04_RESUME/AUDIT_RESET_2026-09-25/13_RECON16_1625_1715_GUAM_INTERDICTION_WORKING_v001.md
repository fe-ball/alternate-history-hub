Of the original 24 attack aircraft, roughly **17–20** reach usable weapon-release opportunities after air combat, jettison/abort and formation disruption. Not every release is accurately aimed.

Selected Guam effects:

- **2–4 aircraft** on the ground are irrecoverably destroyed;
- **5–8 additional aircraft** become short-term unavailable from fragments, towing accidents, servicing-area damage or blast effects;
- **12–18 aircraft-class** of servicing/redistribution work experiences roughly **1–2 h** additional queue/displacement friction;

- the principal Guam warning/plot path is **not mission-killed** in the centered result. Local communication/power interruptions may occur, but alternate routing preserves warning and plotting.

The Branch dispersion/tow/repair system materially reduces aircraft destruction compared with an exposed apron. It does not prevent runway cratering or servicing delay.

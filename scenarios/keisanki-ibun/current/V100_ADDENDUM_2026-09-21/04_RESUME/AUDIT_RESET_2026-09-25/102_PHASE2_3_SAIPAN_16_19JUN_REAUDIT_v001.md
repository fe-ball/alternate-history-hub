# 3. Artillery loss classification — first real Phase-0 consequence

Beginning 16 Jun afternoon, every Japanese indirect-fire loss should be classified as:

1. **PHYSICAL DESTROYED**
2. **CREW-OUT / CREW-DEGRADED**
3. **DISPLACED / POSITION LOST**
4. **OBSERVATION PATH LOST**
5. **WIRE/RADIO CUT**
6. **AMMUNITION ACCESS CUT**
7. **TOW/ROUTE FAILURE**
8. **TACTICALLY AVAILABLE**

Phase-0 functional completeness changes the distribution among these states.

Compared with a historical-like brittle system:
- fewer intact mobile guns are immediately abandoned for want of tow/organization;
- more batteries have a surveyed alternate;
- more alternate OPs possess actual optics/communications;
- more local ammunition stocks remain accessible after waterfront loss.

This does **not** reduce U.S. suppression.
It increases later return-to-fire probability.

# 5. Historical-style mass barge counterattack — NO-GO

Historical Saipan actually attempted an amphibious counterattack in the early morning of 17 Jun and lost 13 troop-carrying barges to U.S. armed landing craft.

The Branch must make an explicit decision rather than simply omit the event.

### Decision
**NO-GO for the historical-style mass barge assault.**

Reasons:
- U.S. western naval/landing-craft screen remains dense;
- no temporary Japanese sea/fire-control window exists;
- land routes still permit meaningful Afetna/Susupe pressure and Aslito defense;
- the Branch has an established relief/reception doctrine and therefore values the heavy craft wallet;
- losing a large fraction of the local boat pool now would directly damage the later 20/21-Jun relief option.

This is not passivity.
It is a deliberate preservation decision.

### What boats do instead
Night 16/17:
- liaison;
- wounded;
- ammunition;
- signals/batteries;
- small engineer parties;
- bounded troop redistribution between still-Japanese coastal nodes;
- reconnaissance / pilotage of future OCL and reception lanes.

This is the first formal propagation of **boat mobility** into the ground replay.

## 0. Verdict

The full-role reaudit in documents 41–45 is structurally sound.

Three corrections are required:

1. The 15-Jun morning local anti-shipping attack should be treated as a **preplanned alert mission enabled by 14/15-night invasion confirmation**, not primarily as a cold reaction to a post-H-hour observer report.
2. Observation-to-air-attack timing must distinguish **Tinian/Saipan-adjacent launch** from **Guam launch**.
3. The 15-Jun evening local-air attack should be promoted from OPEN SENSITIVITY to a **SELECTED WORKING event**, because both historical physical calibration and the Branch's surviving local-air wallet support it.

No approved ground-front supersession is required by these corrections.

## 2. Reaction-time correction

Approximate geometry:
- Tinian -> Saipan area: ~10–15 nm;
- Guam -> Saipan area: ~115–120 nm.

At practical tactical transit speeds, Guam adds roughly 30–45 minutes of flight time; Tinian adds only several minutes.

### D4Y short ground/support attack
Cold-start / alert:
- Tinian-side: **~30–60 min** observation-to-target class;
- Guam-side: **~55–95 min**.

If prearmed/prebriefed and awaiting a final go/no-go update, launch delay can be materially shorter, but the preparation was paid earlier.

### B6N torpedo mission
Cold-start:
- Tinian-side: **~50–100 min**;
- Guam-side: **~80–140 min**.

This includes mission preparation / arming / briefing plus transit class.
A torpedo mission is therefore inappropriate as a snap reaction to a FRESH-10 target.

### Ground artillery
Existing 5–30 minute class exploitation for current firing data remains valid as the fastest response layer.

## 4. Selected 15-Jun evening event

### Mission generation
By late afternoon Japan has:
- confirmed Saipan as the invasion objective;
- repeated visual/air/ground reporting on the U.S. support system;
- morning attack experience/BDA;
- observed carrier/support flight activity;
- battlefield/unloading target information.

The evening strike is planned during the afternoon, not generated instantaneously at dusk.

### Package
Selected:
- **10–14 attack aircraft**, 2–3 groups;
- **0–2 fighter escorts** only if they can be paid without weakening local point defense;
- D4Y use limited to roughly **2–4** in the center to preserve fast ISR;
- remaining attack strength biased toward B6N / older attack-capable aircraft whose exact type split remains OPEN.

Do not import P1Y merely because historical U.S. observers thought they saw Frances.

### Target choice
Center:
- primary = carrier/support group during recovery / late flight operations.

Branch alternative:
- if a 16:30–17:15 ground-air observer reports a persistent high-value handling/artillery/ammunition cluster and carrier contact is stale, one 2–4 aircraft subelement may divert to that semi-fixed target.

Do NOT give both target systems a full strike.

### Timing
Selected:
- launch / group departures ~17:15–18:00 depending on base;
- terminal windows ~18:05–19:15.

### U.S. defense
- radar/F6F intercept 30–60 nm class can occur;
- inner AA remains strong;
- dusk/recovery activity creates handling friction but not radar blindness.

### Center result
- Japanese irreversible: **5–8**;
- additional damaged/abort: **1–3**;
- U.S. F6F irreversible: **0–1**;
- **4–7** Japanese aircraft achieve usable attack/release geometry.

Ship outcome:
- no major-hull direct hit;
- no carrier mission kill;
- dangerous close approaches / near misses possible.

Operational:
- carrier/support recovery or maneuver sequencing disruption **10–25 min** in the affected group;
- if a small handling-zone subelement is selected, local unloading/artillery handling may pause roughly **10–20 min**, but no centered ammunition/fuel catastrophe.

## 6. Compatibility with 16-Jun and later WORKING line

The new debit does NOT invalidate the later replay.

Reasons:
- the 13-Jun-dawn 31–42 attack/recon ready band is regional/nonfighter and does not require every D-Day sortie to use unique aircraft;
- morning survivors can be serviced for evening use;
- the selected evening package limits D4Y consumption to preserve reconnaissance;
- only two local D4Y are required for the 16-Jun afternoon recontact case;
- the 17-Jun Tinian D4Y package can draw heavily from 16-Jun carrier-origin survivors recovered ashore, not only pre-D-Day local D4Y;
- fighter-defense requirements are not heavily debited by the two local attack packages.

However:
- any future claim of a large independent Marianas strike on 16-Jun morning/early afternoon must now pay this 9–15 irreversible attack-air debit;
- local B6N/older-attack depth is materially lower than in a model that omitted D-Day air attacks.

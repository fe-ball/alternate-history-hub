# Marianas Japanese aviation operational-value matrix

Date: 2026-09-26
Status: WORKING DECISION AID / NOT CAMPAIGN AUTHORITY

## 0. Question

What does Japanese aviation actually accomplish in the Marianas besides fleet search and decisive carrier battle?

Answer by mission effect, not kill count.

## 1. Maritime ISR
Leverage: VERY HIGH.
Cost: low aircraft count, high crew/airframe value.
Effect:
- invasion identification;
- fleet handoff;
- carrier recontact;
- transport/support disposition;
- surface-night geometry.

This remains the highest-value use of scarce long-range reconnaissance aircraft.

## 2. Battlefield observation / artillery cooperation
Leverage: HIGH per sortie.
Cost: moderate exposure, low ordnance expenditure.
Effect:
- target freshness;
- road/artillery/armor updates;
- faster retirement of stale fire plans;
- Aslito interdiction after physical loss.

Best under U.S. air superiority because one short observation can feed many ground guns.

## 3. Bombardment-damage survey / engineer prioritization
Leverage: HIGH cumulative.
Cost: low-to-moderate.
Effect:
- distinguish destroyed vs suppressed;
- select wire/road/OP/gun repairs;
- avoid wasting night labor on dead positions.

This is one of the least visible but most important Branch uses.

## 4. Opportunity attack on unloading / handling
Leverage: HIGH if target fresh and clustered; otherwise LOW.
Cost: high CAP/AA loss risk.
Best targets:
- artillery/tank assembly;
- ammunition/fuel handling;
- LST/LCM concentrations;
- congested road exits.

Preferred when:
- observer report is fresh;
- aircraft already armed;
- target is semi-fixed;
- U.S. CAP momentarily weak/displaced.

Not a routine daily mission.

## 5. Anti-shipping local attack
Leverage: MODERATE-HIGH episodically.
Cost: HIGH.
Value:
- force evasive maneuver;
- disrupt flight/unloading/fire-support cycles;
- possible torpedo/bomb damage.

D-Day 15 Jun is the clearest missing opportunity window.

## 6. Night/dusk harassment
Leverage: MODERATE.
Cost:
- night-qualified crew;
- navigation;
- fatigue;
- recovery risk.

Value often lies in:
- general quarters;
- illumination/AA expenditure;
- interrupted unloading/recovery;
- sleep/repair disruption;
- forcing ship dispersal.

Do not require a ship hit for operational value.

## 7. Counter-observation / observer denial
Leverage: MODERATE-HIGH.
Cost: fighter sorties/fuel.

Targets:
- naval gunfire spotters;
- artillery observation aircraft;
- photo recon;
- repeated low-altitude observation tracks.

Effect:
- forces F6F escort;
- can temporarily reduce observed-fire quality;
- buys displacement/repair time.

## 8. Liaison / communication
Leverage: MODERATE, resilience-oriented.
Cost: small sortie count.

Value:
- Army/Navy grid/report transfer;
- island-to-island status;
- courier movement when cable/radio unreliable;
- specialist/personnel movement.

## 9. SAR
Leverage: LOW visibility / HIGH personnel value.
Cost: aircraft exposure.

Saving one experienced recon/night/formation crew can matter more than saving one ordinary airframe.

## 10. Ferry / regeneration
Leverage: HIGH systemic.
Effect:
- disperse aircraft before suppression;
- move repaired aircraft between islands;
- recover carrier-origin aircraft ashore;
- later move carrier-qualified survivors west.

This converts geography into aviation endurance.

## 11. Light armed reconnaissance
Leverage: LOW-MODERATE.
Best use:
- soft small craft;
- isolated vehicles;
- exposed handling;
- targets of opportunity during an existing recon sortie.

Risk:
turning every reconnaissance aircraft into a bomber destroys the ISR system.

## 12. Priority by phase

### 11–12 Jun
1. air defense
2. survival/dispersal
3. maritime ISR
4. BDA/SAR
5. selective night attack

### 13–14 Jun
1. ISR / invasion identification
2. bombardment BDA / repair survey
3. counter-observation
4. limited night attack
5. preserve strike pool

### 15 Jun
1. landing/support observation
2. bounded anti-shipping reaction
3. ground-fire support
4. CAP/base defense
5. dusk/night opportunity attack
6. SAR/liaison

### 16–18 Jun
1. fleet recontact / carrier battle support
2. recovery-field defense
3. ground observation
4. base regeneration/ferry
5. selective local strike only when opportunity is unusually favorable.

## 13. Core interpretation

The Marianas air system is not just:
"fighters + carrier scouts + one decisive strike."

It is a scarce **information / fire-control / interdiction / regeneration network**.

Its best contribution under U.S. air superiority often comes from causing:
- better Japanese ground-fire timing;
- worse U.S. observation;
- more U.S. CAP sorties;
- more ship maneuver;
- slower unloading/repair;
- preserved Japanese aircrew;
rather than from direct ship or aircraft kills.

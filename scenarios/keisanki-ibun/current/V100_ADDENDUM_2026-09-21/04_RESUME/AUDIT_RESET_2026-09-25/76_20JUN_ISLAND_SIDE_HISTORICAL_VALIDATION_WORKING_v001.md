## 1. Historical Saipan itself — strongest direct check

Contemporary U.S. Navy reporting for the night of 20 Jun (West Longitude Date) states:
- several Japanese aircraft dropped bombs near U.S. transports and along the shore;
- no damage was reported from that attack;
- Japanese shore batteries fired sporadically at U.S. ships;
- exposed emplacements were quickly knocked out.

Separate U.S. Navy operational summary states that Japanese aircraft from other Marianas / Caroline bases continued to harass ships off Saipan through 7 Jul; raids were generally small, but an LCI was sunk, Maryland was damaged, and several other ships received lesser damage.

Implication for current Branch:
- a **small 3–5 aircraft night harassment package** is directly plausible;
- **0 major ship hit center** is not artificially pessimistic;
- shore batteries can matter tactically without dominating the sea fight;
- rapid U.S. counterbattery against revealed coast guns is mandatory.

Current file-75 / file-73 treatment:
**PASS.**

No extra air hit or coast-gun hit is added.

Sources:
- U.S. Navy Department / CINCPAC communiques, 22 Jun 1944;
- U.S. Navy, Second Official Report / Marianas Operations.

## 2. Savo Island — island observation + floatplane illumination

The 9 Aug 1942 Savo Island night action provides a strong observation-chain analogue.

Official U.S. Marine history records:
- Japanese cruiser-based aircraft dropped flares before / during the attack;
- subsequent prisoner information indicated the attacking force was aided by observation from Cape Esperance;
- flare light and fires helped make the battle area visible.

Implication:
- island observation and shipborne / water-air illumination can materially improve a night surface force's shared battle-area picture;
- the effect begins after the dark approach is partly spent;
- it does not require a modern fused CIC;
- illumination also exposes positions and is not a one-way accuracy bonus.

- after muzzle flashes and illumination, Tapotchau / coast / DD / water-air reports improve the Japanese common track.

**PASS.**

This is a particularly close historical analogue to the current observation logic.

Source:

## 3. Cape Esperance / Tassafaronga — Nisshin heavy reinforcement under separate cover

11/12 Oct 1942 is the strongest transport-architecture analogue.

Official Marine history records:
- Chitose + Nisshin + destroyers carried and landed **728 men**;
- cargo included **four 150-mm howitzers, two field pieces, one AA gun, radio equipment, and ammunition**;
- a separate Japanese cruiser force had the mission of bombarding Henderson Field and covering the reinforcement;
- the U.S. surface force intercepted the cruiser force, but the transport element succeeded in landing.

Implication:
- Nisshin as a fast heavy-transport rather than only a seaplane tender is historically grounded;
- separate fast combat / heavy-gun cover and transport layers are historically grounded;
- heavy artillery / radio / ammunition cargo is exactly the sort of load for which Nisshin adds value beyond DD transport;
- successful unloading does not require winning a general fleet battle first.

Difference from current Branch:
- historical transports were not themselves pressed by an SG-equipped 1944 interception system during the final unloading;
- the current 20/21 Jun replay therefore imposes a harsher close-interdiction environment than the 1942 precedent.

Files 61 / 71 / 72 / 73:
**PASS.**

No increase to the selected 280–440 t useful Saipan cargo is justified merely from the historical 1942 success.

Source:
- USMC official Guadalcanal campaign history, Cape Esperance / October reinforcement account.

## 4. Guadalcanal 13/14 Oct — ground artillery + naval bombardment + spotting aircraft

The Henderson Field bombardment provides a useful combined-fire analogue.

Official history records:
- Japanese land artillery on Guadalcanal fired on Henderson Field and temporarily denied its normal use;
- later the same night Kongo and Haruna bombarded for about 80 minutes;
- observation / spotting aircraft dropped flares and supported accurate fire;
- U.S. 5-inch defense guns were later used in counterbattery work and silenced several Japanese medium-artillery pieces.

Implication:
- a land artillery system and a mobile naval heavy-gun system can act on the same operational target without being one centrally aimed battery;
- spotting aircraft / flares can materially improve night fire;
- counterbattery progressively erodes the island contribution;
- island fire can create operational interruption without permanently destroying the supported system.

Current Saipan replay:
- ground artillery attacks U.S. shore fire-control / movement nodes;
- ships counter-interdict U.S. naval pursuers;
- water-air / illumination helps reacquisition;
- U.S. naval / shore counterbattery progressively suppresses exposed Japanese coast cells.

**PASS.**

The current model is less destructive than the Henderson-field example and therefore not an obvious overreach.

Source:
- USMC official Guadalcanal campaign history.

## 5. Wake Island — coast guns as an upper-bound close-range case

Wake, 11 Dec 1941, is not a direct Saipan analogue but is a useful upper bound.

Official U.S. Navy history records:
- Marine shore batteries withheld fire until Japanese ships were well within useful range;
- shore fire badly damaged Yubari;
- Hayate was hit in the magazine and destroyed;
- other ships were damaged;
- the first Japanese landing attempt was aborted;
- surviving Marine fighters then attacked the retiring force.

Implication:
- coast-defense guns can be decisive against ships that enter a well-prepared short-range belt;
- preplanned geometry / fire discipline matter;
- air + coast artillery can compound.

Why current Saipan should be weaker:
- U.S. 1944 radar/fire-control/counterbattery is much stronger;
- Japanese coast batteries have already endured days of suppression;
- current U.S. ships are not blindly closing into an intact unsuppressed battery belt.

Therefore current Saipan rule:
- coast guns matter only inside roughly 7–9 nmi-class useful geometry;
- they constrain maneuver;
- no automatic direct ship hit;
- revealed cells are counterbatteried.

**PASS, conservative.**

Source:
- NHHC Wake Island H-Gram / official Wake history.

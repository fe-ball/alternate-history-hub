## 0. Method correction

Do not define fleet fuel recovery as "how many times did it rendezvous with an oiler?"

Fleet operational fuel margin can improve by four distinct mechanisms:

1. **absolute transfer** — oilers transfer fuel to hulls;
2. **burn-rate reduction** — groups reduce speed / suspend unnecessary flight-operation maneuver;
3. **role rotation** — a less-depleted group takes the threat-side CAP/search/screen role while a depleted group moves rearward;
4. **screen/escort rotation** — high-consumption DDs peel off for fuel and are temporarily relieved by fresher escorts.

Only (1) adds fuel to tanks.
(2)–(4) recover **relative operational margin** by spending the remaining fuel more efficiently and moving fresher units into the mission that matters.

Aviation gasoline/ordnance and ship bunker fuel are separate wallets. A carrier whose ship fuel improves does not thereby regenerate its air group.

## 1. Fuel-state notation

Because the exact named-hull bunker ledger and oiler cargo ledger are not yet closed, avoid false tank percentages.

Use operational states:

- **F-GREEN**: one more normal operational day plus a bounded high-speed combat cycle is supportable before replenishment becomes binding.
- **F-AMBER**: defense/withdrawal and one bounded maneuver are supportable; renewed prolonged eastern offensive should wait for replenishment.
- **F-AMBER-RED**: withdrawal/rendezvous/defense are supportable; a renewed eastward offensive is not rational.
- **F-RED**: replenishment or mission relief is required before further high-speed combat use.

These are mission states, not literal percent-full readings.

## 3. The first rotation happens before the first fuel hose is connected

17:30–21:30 selected maneuver:

- first-line carrier body continues WSW at roughly 18–20 kt toward the support system;
- second echelon reduces westward speed / holds a more easterly relative station;
- the 40–70 nm separation closes over several hours;
- around 20:30–22:00 the two carrier echelons can overlap/parallel enough for a **leapfrog role exchange**.

Role exchange:
- second echelon becomes the more threat-side mobile carrier layer;
- it assumes the larger share of long-range CAP/search-ready responsibility;
- first-line carriers pass farther west toward replenishment;
- the first line can reduce flight-operation maneuver and deck alert burden for several hours.

No fuel has yet been created.
But the fleet's **forward carrier aviation** is now being provided by the less-depleted echelon while the more-depleted one enters a replenishment cycle.

This is the main "relative fuel recovery" mechanism.

## 4. Escort and fast-vanguard rotation

Do not force the entire fast vanguard to wait for one synchronized refueling.

Selected process:
- lowest-margin DD sections peel west in pairs/small divisions when tactical contact is absent;
- fresher carrier-screen/rear-screen DDs temporarily cover part of the gap;
- cruisers/heavy units of the fast vanguard remain threat-side longer;
- after DD top-off, individual divisions can rejoin the forward screen.

This reduces the chance that low-endurance destroyers become the hard limit on the whole surface group.

It costs:
- escort density around the oilers;
- temporary reduction in forward torpedo-screen strength;
- command/formation friction;
- extra ASW responsibility.

No DD is counted in two screens at the same time.

3. remaining first-line carriers;

- ASW geometry;
- emergency acceleration.

The second echelon's forward role protects the first line during this vulnerability.

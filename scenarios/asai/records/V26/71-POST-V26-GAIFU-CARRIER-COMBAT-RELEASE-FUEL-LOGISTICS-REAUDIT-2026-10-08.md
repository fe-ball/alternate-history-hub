## 3. Different fuel is a shipboard integration gate

Gaifu E6-S-T uses aviation GT middle distillate rather than ordinary avgas.

National fuel availability does not solve carrier integration automatically.

A front-line carrier detachment requires:
- segregated clean Grade-A storage or packaged tankage;
- dedicated transfer/pump/filter/water-control procedure;
- contamination control between avgas and middle-distillate systems;
- deck fueling gear and markings;
- replenishment accounting aboard oilers/supply chain;
- starting/external-power support;
- hot-section/core inspection tooling;
- reducer/governor/propeller spares and technicians;
- salt/corrosion/FOD operating procedure;
- fire/deck crew training for two aviation-fuel families;
- sortie-cycle proof under carrier tempo.

Grade A is less volatile than avgas, which can help fire safety, but that does not erase cleanliness/segregation and support-system requirements.

No current authority closes this complete shipboard package for a combat carrier air group by Apr-May 1942.

## 4. Carrier qualification is not unit combat release

By spring 1942 it is plausible that:
- individual Gaifu aircraft have made deck-qualification work;
- selected pilots are carrier-qualified;
- a small evaluation detachment can operate from a carrier or shore carrier-training system.

That does NOT prove:
- 8-12 aircraft can deploy forward;
- the carrier can sustain repeated CAP/escort cycles;
- fuel/spares/core maintenance can be supported during a fleet operation;
- the unit should be risked in the decisive MI operation.

For a new, scarce, still-maturing different-fuel fighter, the Navy has a strong reason to keep development assets in the home-water evaluation/training system until the support package is proven.

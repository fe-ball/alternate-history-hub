## 1. Why Gaifu has less marginal combat value here than against a historical-stock Zero force

If the carrier fighter baseline were historical-stock A6M2, a second fighter type could buy a larger qualitative gap in:
- response speed;
- climb;
- communication/formation reliability;
- repeatability;
- high-energy interception options.

But the worldline A6M2 already carries:
- ~543-550 km/h class speed;
- design-origin mature constant-speed prop path;
- EX2/early-EX3 thrust-exhaust path;
- better governor / engine-prop matching;
- better cooling/vibration instrumentation;
- production/QC repeatability;
- improved radio reliability / formation handling;
- better boresight / re-zero repeatability;
- improved reacceleration / section integrity in V26 operational use.

Gaifu still has a speed/climb advantage, but:
- wing loading is much higher;
- power loading overlaps;
- MI central gun count is not higher;
- protection advantage is not closed;
- endurance / service maturity favor A6M;
- Gaifu carrier fuel/maintenance/service-release costs are high.

Therefore the incremental value of a small Gaifu detachment is narrower than a historical-stock comparison suggests.

## 2. The major system trade

A small mixed detachment can gain:
- somewhat earlier high/intercept contact;
- a second maneuver/energy envelope;
- limited tactical uncertainty for the opponent.

But it pays:
- separate Grade-A fuel chain;
- separate starting / core / reducer / governor / prop support;
- separate spares and technicians;
- separate pilot qualification;
- mixed-type deck handling;
- mixed-type controller estimation under already-coarse Type21/radio C2;
- opportunity cost of risking scarce development aircraft and cadre.

For a decisive carrier operation in spring 1942:
**the support/C2 cost can plausibly exceed the marginal fighter-performance gain.**

This strengthens V26:71's Gaifu0 central decision.

## 3. Homogeneous A6M CAP has its own system benefit

Do not treat "all A6M" as losing every benefit previously assigned to heterogeneous CAP.

A single-type CAP allows:
- common fuel / spares / deck procedures;
- common performance assumptions for controllers;
- easier section merging / reforming;
- easier relief/replacement;
- fewer handoff errors;
- greater pilot interchangeability;
- simpler readiness accounting.

Under coarse fighter direction, this can be operationally valuable.

The differentiation becomes:
**same aircraft, different state/task**, not different aircraft types.

## 2. FT damage mechanism — WORKING close

### 2.1 SAP is not "small HE"

The FT/SAP branch must be modeled as a high-mass penetrative projectile, not primarily by explosive fill alone.

Baseline FT mass remains roughly 760-820 kg class.

Damage mechanism:

1. high-speed projectile entry;
2. local shell / frame / structural destruction along the penetration path;
3. equipment, cable, pipe, ventilation and aviation-support damage from the body and secondary fragments;
4. short-delay SAP internal burst;
5. for ramjet standard FT, possible propulsion-derived secondary fire from hot propulsion hardware and residual liquid fuel.

Do **not** convert FT/SAP directly into a heavy naval AP shell penetration equivalent:

- diameter is large;
- impact speed is lower than heavy naval shell muzzle / terminal speeds;
- body construction is not a battleship AP projectile;
- wings/tail/body can deform, strip and absorb energy.

Main armor belts remain poor targets. Light/unarmored side, upper hull, hangar side, bulkheads and internal support spaces are much more natural targets.

### 2.2 HE role

FT/HE remains especially useful for:

- AA / director / exposed command suppression;
- aviation-support damage;
- hangar-side / ventilation / electrical damage;
- light combatants / transports;
- opening a mixed-weapon attack by degrading defensive systems.

### 2.3 Fire effect

Ramjet heat / residual fuel is **not** treated as extra explosive fill or TNT equivalence.

It does change the prospective damage model:

- penetrative hits have a higher probability of becoming persistent local fires;
- fuel can spray along the penetration path and create multiple ignition points;
- damage-control load increases because fire, ventilation, electrical isolation, fuel isolation and structural damage occur together.

Exact fuel type/load/residual mass is still OPEN.

FT-G / FT-GR fire behavior must not be automatically copied from standard ramjet FT.

## 10. GT fuel semantic correction — mandatory for future replay

Previous conversation wording “kerosene logistics” is superseded.

Current V16 technical authority:
- the worldline does **not** create a separate jet-fuel island;
- it uses a graded military **middle-distillate pool** shared across diesel and GT users.

Grades:
- Grade A: aviation GT fuel with tight low-temperature / water / cleanliness / thermal-stability control;
- Grade B: standard military middle distillate for fast diesel, vehicles, generators, ground GT and approved lower/derated aviation GT use;
- Grade C: broader/lower grade for tolerant diesel/stationary/selected marine use.

Higher grade can be down-used. Lower grade can be up-used only under approved derated schedules and inspection penalties.

E5/E6 fuel guards inherited from V16:
- clean straight-run kerosene/gas-oil can be broadly usable;
- ordinary diesel/gas-oil on E6 JF: derated roughly 810–825 C class;
- ordinary diesel/gas-oil on E6 pure-J: roughly 820–835 C ceiling class;
- selected-Ni / 920 C high rating is prohibited on low-grade fuel;
- inspection interval shortens before TBO is automatically redefined.

The operational advantage to preserve is:
**GT aircraft can accept a broader middle-distillate pool when the fuel meets the minimum approved condition, trading rating / temperature margin / inspection burden against fuel quality.**

Therefore the Port Moresby GT startup is **not** gated by the arrival of one narrow dedicated kerosene product.

Correct local sequence:
middle-distillate stock
→ water/salt/particulate/quality treatment and inspection
→ approved fuel grade / local acceptance
→ allowable turbine-temperature/rating schedule
→ sortie release
→ shortened inspection / core-life debit where applicable.

Do not equate “Grade A unavailable” with “E6 cannot fly.”

## 1. Family architecture — propulsion/control and warhead are separate dimensions

Do not use "FT" as one homogeneous round.

### Propulsion / guidance family

**FT-G**
- unpowered glide branch;
- simplest / least propulsion-dependent member;
- short-range employment;
- useful where target value does not justify a standard ramjet round;
- naturally suited to important transports, tankers, auxiliaries and selected low/medium-defense targets;
- not the preferred high-speed-carrier weapon.

**FT-GR**
- glide + short-duration rocket branch;
- short-range / medium-short attack tool;
- useful for screen suppression and important light/medium targets;
- GR/HE can be assigned to a key destroyer/light cruiser when opening an attack corridor is more valuable than attacking every escort;
- do not copy standard-ramjet residual-fuel / hot-propulsion fire behavior automatically to GR.

**standard FT**
- ramjet branch;
- principal high-value open-ocean anti-ship member;
- carrier / cruiser / capital-ship mission use where standoff, attack-axis separation and post-release aircraft survival justify the round;
- supports HE and SAP mission roles;
- can use RUN-ON or programmed CUT / SELF-DESTRUCT terminal corridor logic;
- no autonomous target reselection after a miss.

**FT-IR**
- standard FT with one-dimensional terminal IR yaw correction;
- premium / scarce branch;
- intended to reduce lateral/cross-track miss against moving targets when geometry is difficult;
- particularly valuable against healthy maneuvering high-value ships, especially fast carriers;
- not an all-aspect seeker;
- not a modern multi-target seeker;
- does not identify targets by itself at night;
- no automatic retarget after missing the first predicted intersection.

**FT-GR-IR**
- short-range GR branch with the same limited one-dimensional terminal yaw correction;
- premium special-use short-range round;
- useful where a short attack corridor and maneuvering target justify spending scarce IR hardware;
- not the routine fleet-carrier high-value round unless local geometry specifically favors it.

### Warhead / fuze family

Propulsion/control family does not determine warhead automatically.

**HE / blast-fragmentation**
Natural roles:
- destroyer / light combatant;
- transport / auxiliary;
- carrier hangar-side and exposed aviation systems;
- AA galleries / directors;
- command / communications;
- ventilation / electrical systems;
- exposed personnel / topside equipment.

HE is a suppression / system-damage weapon.
Do not treat an instantaneous HE impact on a heavy main belt as an efficient capital-ship kill.

**SAP / short-delay**
Natural roles:
- light/unarmored side structure;
- upper hull;
- hangar side;
- internal aviation-support / electrical / ventilation / auxiliary spaces;
- exploitation after target maneuver, AA or flight operations have already been degraded.

SAP is not a heavy naval AP shell.
Model mass/velocity penetration, structural damage and short-delay internal burst, then secondary fire where standard ramjet propulsion remains hot/fueled.

**terminal-dip / very-short water-contact branch**
- remains LIMITED / non-routine;
- may improve lower-side impact distribution if later qualification closes;
- no sustained underwater run;
- no guaranteed waterline hit bonus;
- do not use as an ordinary combat replay modifier.

**RF / proximity**
- test-only / non-ordinary forward issue for late 1941 and spring 1942 central planning;
- do not use as a normal MI round.

## 3. Core mixed-weapon principles

### Principle A — do not stack all attack modes on one bearing

FT and Type91 should normally arrive from separated bearings / phases where command and geometry allow.

Reason:
- Type91 induces conventional torpedo-combing maneuvers;
- FT can exploit the changing side aspect;
- FT/HE can degrade AA/command before a lower/slower torpedo approach;
- D3A pressure can force turns / distract AA;
- different damage modes are orthogonal.

This is not perfectly synchronized modern missile doctrine.
Radio/signaling, weather, target motion, CAP and formation breakup remain real limits.

### Principle B — FT-first and Type91-first are both valid

**FT-first** is preferred when:
- the target has strong AA / command / aviation-system defenses;
- suppressing directors / flight operations / electrical / ventilation first materially improves the following attack;
- target maneuver is not yet the main problem.

Typical sequence:
FT/HE suppression -> D3A or continued air pressure -> Type91 underwater attack -> FT/SAP exploitation if useful.

**Type91-first** is preferred when:
- forcing a target turn is more valuable than early suppression;
- the target is already under sufficient AA/air-pressure disruption;
- a side-aspect change can materially improve standard/IR FT geometry.

Typical sequence:
Type91 threat / combing turn -> FT from separated axis -> SAP/HE exploitation.

### Principle C — mother-aircraft survival matters before release

Radar/CAP/AA can break FT effectiveness by preventing the mother aircraft from reaching a valid setup/release state.
Do not apply a magical post-release hit multiplier.
Count:
search/contact quality -> attack setup -> CAP/AA disruption -> valid release -> FT cross-track/terminal behavior -> hit/damage.

### Principle D — RUN-ON is geometry, not autonomous guidance

A missed standard FT may continue along a programmed terminal corridor.
This can threaten another ship in a formation only if that ship physically intersects the corridor.
No seeker-based second-target selection.

Use CUT / SELF-DESTRUCT where:
- friendly shipping;
- coast / harbor;
- neutral traffic;
- formation geometry
makes a long run-on undesirable.

## 4. Target-class attack cards — spring 1942

### Fast fleet carrier

Preferred family:
- standard FT;
- selective FT-IR;
- Type91;
- D3A pressure.

Central logic:
1. establish moving track;
2. attack from multiple bearings where possible;
3. HE can suppress AA/directors/hangar/flight-deck support;
4. Type91 attacks underwater mobility / buoyancy;
5. SAP or IR-SAP exploits a maneuver-constrained / damaged carrier.

IR is premium and most rational here because healthy carrier maneuver creates high lateral-miss risk.

Do not assume every carrier strike uses IR.
Do not intentionally wait for night merely to exploit IR.

### Battleship / battlecruiser

Preferred family:
- standard FT HE/SAP;
- Type91 as principal underwater sinking / mobility weapon;
- conventional bombs as available.

Daylight IR central allocation = low/zero unless geometry specifically requires it.

Default logic:
FT/HE suppression -> Type91 main underwater damage -> SAP exploitation.

FT does not replace Type91 as the primary robust sinking mechanism.

### Heavy cruiser

Preferred:
- standard FT HE/SAP;
- Type91 where underwater mobility kill is operationally valuable;
- D3A may be more economical if a clean dive-bomber attack is already available.

Do not spend FT merely because it exists.

### Light cruiser / key destroyer screen ship

Preferred:
- FT-GR/HE or standard FT/HE depending range and defense;
- only attack if removing that screen ship opens a higher-value corridor or has direct operational value.

Small highly maneuverable destroyers remain poor unguided-FT targets in general.

### Transport / tanker / auxiliary

Preferred:
- FT-G / FT-GR HE where the target is important enough;
- conventional bombs remain rational for low-value or easy fixed targets.

Use cheaper/simpler family members when standard FT opportunity cost is not justified.

### Harbor / fixed target

FT is not central when:
- conventional bombs;
- shallow-water Type91;
- ordinary dive bombing
already provide an efficient solution.

Do not consume scarce standard/IR rounds to decorate an already efficient strike.

## 5. IR doctrine guard

IR terminal correction is one-dimensional yaw correction.

Use selectively when:
- target is moving and maneuver-capable;
- visual geometry is degraded enough that lateral correction has value;
- target value justifies scarce calibrated hardware.

Do not infer:
- all-aspect homing;
- automatic range control;
- target classification;
- mature night identification;
- seeker-based retarget;
- routine all-weather attack.

### Night IR

"Wait for night to use IR" is not a central plan.

Reasons:
- target identification remains difficult;
- one-dimensional yaw correction is not night target acquisition;
- carrier deck launch/recovery risk rises sharply;
- assembly/signaling/navigation burden rises;
- 1942 IR inventory remains scarce/calibration-limited.

Night IR can remain a voiced technical desire / exceptional local opportunity, not standard doctrine.

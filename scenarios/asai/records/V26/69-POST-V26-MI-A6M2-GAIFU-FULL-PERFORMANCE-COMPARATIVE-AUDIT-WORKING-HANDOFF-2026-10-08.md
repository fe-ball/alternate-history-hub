## 7. Acceleration / energy recovery

A6M2 strengths:
- very low mass;
- favorable power loading;
- mature prop/governor integration in this worldline;
- V26 explicitly carries improved reacceleration / section integrity.

Gaifu:
- more total power;
- much greater mass;
- short-rating can bring power loading near A6M;
- turboprop / prop-governor transient response is a different system and is not numerically closed.

Therefore:
- do NOT grant Gaifu automatic acceleration superiority at all speeds;
- low/medium-speed reacceleration may be competitive rather than dominant;
- higher-speed acceleration / repositioning can benefit from the higher attainable speed, but exact transient curves remain OPEN.

This is an important correction to a simplistic "faster fighter = faster re-engagement" rule.

## 8. Sustained turn / instantaneous turn

A6M2:
- much lower wing loading;
- historical design optimized for maneuver;
- worldline changes do not remove that basic aerodynamic character.

Gaifu:
- substantially higher wing loading;
- automatic slats and Fowler/slotted high-lift system mitigate low-speed lift/stall behavior;
- approach speed remains carrier-compatible despite higher loading.

Working interpretation:
- **A6M2 should retain the stronger sustained low/medium-speed turning economy**;
- Gaifu high-lift devices can improve instantaneous / near-stall lift and keep handling usable;
- Gaifu should NOT be given "Zero-like sustained turn plus more speed" for free;
- using high-lift devices also carries drag / energy cost.

Exact turn-rate/radius curves remain OPEN.

## 9. Roll / control at high speed

Worldline A6M2:

Gaifu:

Therefore:
- A6M high-speed roll weakness remains a plausible model constraint;
- Gaifu high-speed roll advantage must remain **OPEN**, not assumed.

This is one of the highest-priority technical unknowns for SBD/F4F interception.

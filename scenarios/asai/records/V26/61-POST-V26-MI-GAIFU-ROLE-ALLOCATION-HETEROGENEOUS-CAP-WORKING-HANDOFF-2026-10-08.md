## 1. Why the V26:47 eight-Gaifu escort is not automatically optimal

At Cell 3 launch time:
- TF16 is already located;
- a separate third-carrier group is identified;
- Japanese command must expect a U.S. carrier strike even if its exact launch time is unknown;
- Type21 warning exists only on selected Japanese carriers;
- Gaifu is concentrated on Zuikaku and cannot be replaced elsewhere during the battle.

Therefore Gaifu has two competing high-value uses:

A. anti-carrier escort:
- protect B5N/B6N/D3A;
- contest F4F CAP;
- improve strike survival.

B. fleet defense:
- create outer/high-energy interception layer;
- preserve A6M for inner/low-altitude defense;
- respond to late or split U.S. waves.

The marginal value of the last Gaifu assigned to escort is not automatically higher than its marginal value in CAP.

## 4. Decision criterion

Choose among G-E / G-B / G-D using expected marginal value, not aircraft prestige.

Escort marginal value increases when:
- enemy CAP density over Saratoga is high;
- B5N/B6N must penetrate deeply;
- FT mother aircraft need clean setup;
- strike route is long enough that escort endurance remains adequate.

Defense marginal value increases when:
- enemy strike launch is likely;
- warning is coarse / late;
- U.S. waves are split over time;
- Japanese CAP needs rapid sector changes;
- low-altitude Type91-defense and high-altitude SBD-defense must coexist.

At ~0700 MI central, both sides have launch-quality contact and Japanese command should expect an inbound strike.

Therefore:

## 8. Nonlinear mixed-type value

The gain is not additive.

Five Gaifu in the correct outer layer can be worth more defensively than:
- five generic extra fighters,
because they may preserve a larger A6M inner layer.

But if warning is late or vectoring fails:
- those five may arrive no earlier than A6M;
- the mixed-type advantage collapses.

Thus use a conditional "layer preservation" effect rather than a kill multiplier.

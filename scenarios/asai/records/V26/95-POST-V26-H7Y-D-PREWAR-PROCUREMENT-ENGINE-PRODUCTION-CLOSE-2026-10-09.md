## 1. New explicit procurement decisions (closed retrospectively)

1. **1938 H1 — imported engine order:** Navy directs acquisition of **six Jumo 205-series complete engines** plus drawings/maintenance/test documentation and spares rights/material. Four engine positions for the first H7Y experimental airframe; two comparison/contingency engines. This is a **worldline additional purchase**, not a claim that six were historically imported. Procurement and delivery target: 1938 H2–1939 early enough for 1939 experimental operation. Import paperwork is a separate Navy/merchant contract and not generated by Asai's turbine core output.
2. **1939 H2 — development gate:** naval aeronautical establishment approves **two corrected development flying boats (D02/D03)**, domestic diesel qualification and a **four-engine domestic trial lot**. Jumo-derived domestic manufacture requires drawings, tooling, material and endurance validation; intellectual-property/technical-data access is a program cost, not assumed free.
3. **1940 H1 — pilot production contract:** **Kawasaki Aircraft Industries** designated domestic aviation-diesel production prime, with pilot machining/assembly/bench operations initially at **Kobe**. **Akashi** becomes a production/qualification contributor only **after its actual autumn-1940 startup window**. Engine target **12 domestically built in calendar 1940**, inclusive of spares and destructive/endurance test allocations.
4. **1940 H1–H2 — airframe pilot contract:** Navy retains H7Y configuration authority; **Kawanishi** designated manufacturing/industrialization prime for **two additional pre-series boats D04/D05**, with Navy arsenal prototype support for D01–D03. This is a funded special production cell, NOT free diversion of existing H8K slots. Five physical boats by Dec 1941, of which D01 is experimental and D03 limited/under work-up.
5. **1941 H1 — six-airframe forward order:** Navy places firm pre-series continuation order **D06–D11 = six flying boats**, including long-lead structures, fuel/radio systems and aircraft-diesel engines. This precedes Pearl Harbor results. Eight 1941-built engines earmarked specifically for D06/D07; remaining airframes do **not** automatically have engines accepted.
6. **1941 — domestic production gate:** Kawasaki pilot cell accepts **24 complete engines** during calendar 1941, after dedicated rigs, precision two-crankshaft/gearing, opposed-piston fuel injection/scavenging and batch QC. Engine line is separate from Asai jet/TP core throughput, aircraft radial-engine counts and conventional marine diesel counts.
7. **1942 Jan — success-driven scale order:** after Pearl/Enterprise/Wake reconnaissance, Navy orders **D12–D17, six more airframes firm; D18–D19, two options**; increment engine/spares line and trained crews. This order does not retroactively create spring aircraft. Existing 1941 D06–D11 order controls first 1942 deliveries.

## 2. Industrial ownership and chronology

- **Customer / design authority:** Navy air technical establishment; test envelope, structural fixes, takeoff/taxi/porpoising, long-range navigation, COM/radio and acceptance checks.
- **Engine prime:** Kawasaki aircraft-engine organization. Kobe pilot shops handle late-1930s prototypes and early 1940 assembly; Akashi opens in 1940 and becomes the main small-lot qualified engine and test cell in 1941. This is **new worldline contracting**, not a historical Kawasaki Jumo-205 license claim.
- **Diesel technical support:** Kawasaki + specialist machinists; Asai industrial/test/QC support for fatigue, crankshaft, bearing, injection, turbo/scavenging, tolerances and instruments. Asai is not credited with inventing diesel basics or bypassing engine test hours.
- **Airframe prime:** Navy prototypes D01–D03, Kawanishi special pre-series D04 onward; main structure remains conventional metal, selectively improved seals/drains/noncritical secondary panels. Separate small fixtures/inventory booked rather than free H8K capacity.
- **Production line difference:** E5/E6/E7 gas turbine manufacturing is **NOT** automatically an aviation-diesel production line. Diesel high-precision crankshaft + geared counter-rotating/paired shafts, injection, liner, cooling and marine-air corrosion endurance are distinct qualified bottlenecks.

## 5. H7Y-D diesel installation vs DT and TP research

- Service four-engine **H7Y-D**: target takeoff ~**4 × 675–750 hp**; ~20 t experimental weight class; original mission low drag, diesel efficiency, ~300–330 km/h max speed and ~240–265 km/h economical cruise are planning envelopes, not independent factory acceptance results.
- Early imported comparison engines may have lower performance: D01 is **NOT** presumed to prove the later engine / water takeoff envelope.
- **DT (diesel turbocharged) trial**: a distinct boosted/scavenging product investigation; ~4 × 800–850 hp short-rating is an engineering trial target, **not** December-1941 mass service. Confirm intake/back-pressure, boost transient/water run, nacelle vibration and endurance. A proposed D03 trial conversion would remove that airframe from operational totals while under work.
- **TP**: E5/E6 small shaft and E7 follow-on are test engines, NOT automatic replacements in long-endurance service. At partial power, existing turbine outlook implies substantially worse fuel burn than the mature Jumo-derived diesel. 1942 ground installation/takeoff tests can be authorized independently; no 1942 combat-ready H7Y-TP assumed.
- Do not claim fixed turbine-product engine-count = new H7Y product count; any TP flight article requires explicit donor/new hull and must be debited once.

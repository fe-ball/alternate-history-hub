## 3. Shipboard AA and fire-control working closure

### Heavy AA

Type 89 12.7 cm remains the principal modern heavy-AA gun.
The worldline improvement is mainly the chain:

range/target observation -> director -> calculator -> electrical angle transmission -> mount follow-up -> fuze setting -> crew drill.

Selected modern ships reach FCS2 with FCS3-like integration:
- improved synchro/resolver/electrical transmission;
- better calibration and stable reference;
- shorter reset/reacquisition delay;
- better target-allocation switchboard practice;
- more reliable mechanical time-fuze setting/command updates.

This is not Mk37, not full RPC, not radar blind-fire, and not VT-fuzed.

10 cm/65 Type 98 is technically attractive but MI carrier-group combat-present remains 0 central. It belongs to later/new-build fitting.

### 37 mm intermediate AA

A new Japanese 37 mm intermediate-AA branch is retained as a WORKING-CLOSE spring-1942 system.
It is not simply a navalized German Type Ra/Pak.

Development logic:
- 25 mm is retained as light close defense;
- 1938-40 comparison work establishes a 35-40 mm requirement;
- Japanese Navy develops a powered twin 37 mm mounting and dedicated automatic-gun ammunition;
- 1941 trials lead to low-rate 1942 service release.

Working first-series characteristics:
- 37 mm;
- projectile roughly 0.62-0.68 kg;
- muzzle velocity roughly 780-820 m/s;
- 6-8-round clip class;
- practical rate roughly 90-110 rpm per barrel;
- powered twin mounting central;
- local sight + Type-95-line director / electrical lead assistance;
- HE/self-destruct type ammunition;
- no VT fuze.

End-May working inventory:
- physical about 32-40 guns;
- service accepted about 28-34;
- combat-fitted about 14-16 twin mounts fleet-wide.

The role is to fill the 12.7 cm -> 37 mm -> 25 mm layered-defense gap.
It does not replace 25 mm fleet-wide.

### 25 mm

Type 96 25 mm remains numerous and retains its major physical limitations:
- 15-round magazine;
- mount inertia / traverse-elevation limits;
- vibration and muzzle-flash problems;
- small projectile and limited sustained fire.

Worldline benefit is better sight/calibration, powered-mount reliability on priority ships, bearings/gears/QC, director cueing and ammunition-handling drill.
Do not turn it into a Bofors-equivalent weapon.

### Proximity fuze

No combat-present proximity/VT fuze is granted for MI.
Mechanical time fuzes remain the heavy-AA norm.

## 5. Surface gunnery / night fighting / torpedo FCS

Worldline improvement is primarily:
- calibration;
- stable reference;
- electrical angle/data transmission;
- target designation/switchboard;
- reduced reset/reacquisition friction;
- maintainability and power continuity.

Do not apply a generic accuracy multiplier.

### Night fighting

Japanese historical night-fighting doctrine remains the doctrinal anchor.
Worldline gains are the information chain:
contact -> ship/group report -> target designation -> optical range/ID -> fire-control solution.

Surface radar can provide early contact/range-bearing cue, but optical confirmation and conventional FCS remain central.

### Type 93 torpedo employment

Type 93 body performance remains essentially historical.
The worldline improvement is the employment system:
torpedo + launcher + rapid reload + target-motion calculation + gyro-angle transmission + night optics/comms + formation synchronization.

Long-range uncertainty remains dominant.
At 15-20 km, fleets may deliberately distribute aim points across a target-motion uncertainty band rather than concentrate every launcher on one ideal prediction.
This is not a hit-rate multiplier.

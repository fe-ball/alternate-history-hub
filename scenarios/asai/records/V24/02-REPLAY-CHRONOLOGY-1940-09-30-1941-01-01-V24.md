# V24 REPLAY CHRONOLOGY — 1940-09-30 THROUGH 1941-01-01

- Japanese railway/communications recovery is better than history after Hundred Regiments disruption, but bridges, mines, tunnels, yards and major infrastructure damage remain serious.

- Gia Lam receives enough support for test/short reconnaissance by 29–30 September;

## 1940-10-01 to 10-15

### North China

Japanese forces transition from emergency restoration toward more active counter-sweeps. Better radio, transport, engineering and recovery improve persistence on supported axes.

The Eighth Route Army increasingly disperses before long daylight concentration becomes too expensive.

### Central China

The 11th Army begins practical reconnaissance/logistics preparation for a medium autumn operation rather than a giant Chongqing offensive.

### Indochina

The main problem is no longer obtaining permission to land two aircraft. The airfield-support problem becomes:

- prepared/drained surface;
- FOD and dust control;
- filtration;
- fuel;
- external power;
- oxygen;
- photo processing;
- communications;
- line maintenance and core handling.

### Diplomacy

British decision to reopen the Burma Road and continuing US pressure are treated as political support signals to Chongqing, not immediate tonnage miracles.

## 1940-10-16 to 10-31

- US scrap/iron/steel restrictions take effect 16 October.
- Burma Road reopens 17 October.
- There is still no full US oil embargo.

### Guanjianao

The Eighth Route Army can still surprise and encircle an approximately 500-class Japanese detachment.

Worldline delta is not foreknowledge:

- Japanese communications identify the scale of the emergency several hours to roughly half a day sooner;
- E6 reconnaissance improves repeated updates on approaches/outer blocking forces;
- ordinary aviation and relief forces receive better routing/target information;
- relief contact can occur approximately hours earlier, not days earlier;
- the Eighth Route Army withdraws earlier to avoid growing relief/air pressure.

No absolute casualty count is recorded.

Strategic consequence: both sides learn that prolonged concentration is less attractive. Japanese forces see that better ISR/relief can save isolated detachments; the Eighth Route Army accelerates short-duration, night, dispersed, sabotage/ambush-oriented methods.

## 1940-11-01 to 11-24

### North China

Counter-sweeps continue into Taihang/Taiyue-type rear areas but do not annihilate the Eighth Route Army. The North-China E6 detachment remains small; its value is route/outer-blocking ISR and response coordination rather than mass CAS.

### Central China

The 25 November operation remains active.

Preparation includes:

- road/bridge work;
- ammunition/fuel/engineer stock;
- artillery and mobile support;
- repeated photo reconnaissance;
- Fifth War Zone reserve/road/airfield observation.

A V24 working operation pool of Twin 28–30 / Single 22–24 in Central China is permitted as a local replay allocation. It is not a discovered exact OOB.

### Diplomacy, 22–24 November

Peace-channel representation/authority may be authentic enough to test. The political conditions still do not overlap enough to justify suspending the operation.

Japan does not abandon Nanjing as a precondition; Chongqing sees external-support upside to waiting.

## 1940-11-25 to 11-30 — Central Hubei operation

The offensive is launched.

### 25–26 November

- initial multi-axis advance proceeds somewhat more smoothly than a historical-type execution;
- reconnaissance provides fresher information on reserve movement;
- Chinese forces do not become visible as a complete order of battle and retain night/dispersal advantages.

### 27 November

Chinese counterattack/reserve commitment still matters.

Japanese difference:

- more warning of portions of the counterattack;
- better artillery/air target updates;
- stronger rear/side security preparation.

The Chinese counterattack limits the operation but is less likely to cause a sudden disorganized collapse.

### 28–30 November

Japan recognizes that the enemy is not remaining in a large encirclement and contracts deliberately.

- armored/recovery/engineer support reduces equipment abandonment;
- rear guard is more coherent;
- Chinese pursuit continues but is more dispersed/night-oriented;
- the operation ends near the original line without destroying the Fifth War Zone.

The information/operational lesson is larger than the map change.

## 1940-11-30 — Nanjing recognition

Japan formally recognizes the Wang Jingwei/Nanjing government.

This route therefore enters December with stronger Japanese political lock-in to the Nanjing structure than the former peace route.

## 1940-12-01 to 12-15

### South China / Indochina

The Nanning/Guangxi salient is reduced/abandoned more orderly because northern Indochina provides a better southern aviation/political position.

Gia Lam shifts toward routine forward-base use.

V24 working state:

- E6 Twin JF 8 assigned-class;
- roughly 6–7 mission-capable at an ordinary H2 checkpoint.

Mission doctrine expands:

- short-range 0.30–0.40 t internal light strike against distributed targets;
- armed reconnaissance;
- BDA/photo;
- Kunming-class long armed reconnaissance/harassment with reduced load, normally camera + one HSC-100F.

The E6 advantage is high sortie/target-location density, not heavy bomb mass. G3M remains superior when the purpose is to put several tons on one target.

### Central China

The 11th Army rests/refits and begins South-Henan study.

## 1940-12-16 to 12-31

### South-Henan preparation

November lessons drive a doctrine shift:

- do not merely attack to discover a hidden main force;
- use reconnaissance to identify movement;
- force reserves to move with multi-axis pressure;
- reacquire movement;
- attack routes, reserves and withdrawals;
- contract rather than overextend when no decisive encirclement is forming.

### KMT–CCP

The New Fourth Army route/control dispute again becomes dangerous because no peace-route ceasefire-management architecture exists.

This file is a new-year snapshot. It separates operational hardware from prototype, development and research states.

## 1. German exchange / transfer

### 1.1 State

The German E5 acquisition branch survives V24 because its causal chain is already a 1940 event.

By 1941-01-01:

- German technical channels have verified that Japanese turbine aircraft are reproducible service systems;
- Germany seeks a full system rather than an engine curiosity;
- Japan protects current-front E6 technology and offers E5 as the transferable generation;
- the package includes E5-J/JF, E5 Twin production/service information, drawings, process specifications, tooling/gauges, NDT/inspection, life/failure records, maintenance and training;

- physical reference aircraft/engines/CKD/tooling/personnel transfer spans late 1940–1941H1;
- first Germanized E5-D flight remains a 1941H1 event.

No later E5-D Eastern-Front lesson or E6 transfer is available to 1941 Japanese actors.

## 10. Starter-system side note

The exact E5/E6 starter architecture was not previously closed.

V24 engineering default for future formalization:

- piston fighters may retain inertia/electric-inertia starter families where historically appropriate;
- E5/E6 aircraft GT: electric starter-generator with ground electrical power as the normal base-service method and onboard battery as backup/independent start;
- E6 JF starts only the gas-generator spool; the free turbine/aft fan spools from gas flow and requires no direct starter;
- mobile GT generator: battery-electric start is the natural default;
- ordinary 20–30 hp gasoline/diesel auxiliary engines are sufficient as ground starter-generator prime movers; the hard problem is standardized voltage/current, connectors, cables, clutch/overrun logic and procedure rather than prime-mover horsepower.

Exact voltage, current and starter package remain OPEN until a dedicated start-system closeout.

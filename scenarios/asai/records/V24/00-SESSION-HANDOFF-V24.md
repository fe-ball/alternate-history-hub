## 1. Branch premise

The V24 route deliberately diverges from the prior peace route.

- Chongqing receives stronger US/UK political support signals and therefore has greater reservation value for continuing the war.
- Tokyo's commitment to the Wang Jingwei/Nanjing regime is stronger.
- Peace contacts may be authenticated and tested, but Japan does not suspend military operations to create a ceasefire process.
- The core incompatibility is political as much as military: Japan will not readily discard Nanjing and its occupation-state gains, while Chongqing has little reason to accept a settlement that preserves them when external support appears to be improving.

This is a continuation replay, not a denial that peace contacts existed.

For detailed replay, however, 1940-09-30T24:00 is the preferred cutpoint because exact source files close September at a much higher resolution than October–November.

## 4. October replay

### 4.1 North China

Japanese forces move from emergency repair toward deeper counter-sweeps. Motor transport, tractors, engineers, recovery, radio and aviation improve persistence along roads and supported axes; mountains and poor roads remain hard limits.

The Eighth Route Army increasingly disperses before prolonged daylight concentration becomes expensive.

### 4.2 Guanjianao working adjudication

The historical-type encirclement of an approximately 500-class Japanese detachment still occurs; E6 does not provide omniscience.

Worldline difference is narrower:

- better radio/communications and E6 reconnaissance identify the scale of the encirclement several hours to roughly half a day earlier;
- relief decisions and route adjustments occur earlier;
- ordinary Army aviation receives more repeated target updates;
- Chinese concentration remains dangerous but becomes costly sooner;
- the Eighth Route Army withdraws earlier than in the historical-type case rather than being destroyed.

No absolute casualty number or personal-event closure is stored. The important result is behavioral: the Eighth Route Army learns that prolonged concentration against even a small isolated Japanese detachment can expose outer blocking lines, rear concentrations and approach routes to reconnaissance/air/relief pressure.

### 4.3 Political/economic anchors

- US scrap/iron/steel controls take effect on 16 October; there is no full oil cutoff.
- Burma Road reopens on 17 October; it restores a real supply route but does not remove trucking, spare-parts and management bottlenecks.
- North-Indochina airfield support grows from a two-aircraft proof toward sustainable small-unit operations.

## 5. November replay

### 5.1 1–24 November

North-China counter-sweeps continue without destroying the Eighth Route Army. Chinese asymmetricization deepens.

Central-China preparations for the planned 25 November operation continue:

- road/bridge work;
- ammunition and fuel forward movement;
- artillery/engineer concentration;
- repeated photo reconnaissance;
- tracking of Fifth War Zone reserve movement and transport routes.

For this replay the Central-China E6 planning pool may be treated as roughly Twin 28–30 / Single 22–24 around the operation, derived from the September working allocation plus modest operational reinforcement. This remains a local WORKING allocation, not a source-discovered OOB.

Peace representation/authority can still be authenticated on 22–24 November, but no ceasefire order follows.

### 5.2 Central Hubei operation, 25–30 November

The operation is executed.

Worldline differences:

- first-day attack is somewhat smoother because reconnaissance and logistics are better;
- Chinese counterattack and reserve movement still occur;
- Japanese reconnaissance identifies portions of the reserve movement earlier, reducing surprise on 27 November;
- the Chinese counterattack halts/limits Japanese progress but is less likely to force a sudden disorganized retreat;
- from 28 November Japan contracts deliberately when it becomes clear that a large encirclement will not close;
- recovery, engineer and rear-guard support reduce abandonment of vehicles, guns and equipment;
- Chinese pursuit becomes more dispersed/night-oriented and therefore harder to strike, but also slower.

Result:

- no decisive destruction of the Fifth War Zone;
- no large permanent territorial gain;
- better Japanese information;
- lower Japanese irreversible/abandonment loss than a historical-type execution;
- Chinese daylight concentration is more expensive, reinforcing further dispersion.

Absolute casualty totals remain OPEN.

### 5.3 30 November political closure

Japan formally recognizes the Nanjing government. In this route the act is more internally consistent than in the prior peace route because military operations were not suspended for a peace test.

The political structure therefore hardens toward:

- Japan <-> Nanjing institutional commitment;
- Chongqing <-> US/UK support expectation.

## 6. December replay

### 6.1 South China / Indochina

Nanning/Guangxi salient reduction is completed more orderly than history because northern Indochina reduces the need to hold the salient and improved logistics reduce abandonment.

North-Indochina air operations are upgraded from proof-of-concept to routine forward-base use.

A V24 working allocation of **8 E6 Twin JF assigned at Gia Lam by late November/December** is used for planning. At H2 serviceability this implies roughly 6–7 mission-capable aircraft at an ordinary checkpoint. This is a replay allocation, not an inherited exact ledger value.

Role split:

- short/medium-range light attack against distributed targets;
- armed reconnaissance;
- Kunming-class long armed reconnaissance/harassment;
- BDA/photo;
- liaison/high-value movement.

E6 Twin is no longer treated as reconnaissance-only. Its 1940-qualified internal load families allow 6x50/60 kg, 4x100 kg/HSC, or 1x250 kg-class load configurations within the ~0.40 t mission-bay limit. At Kunming-class radius the normal long-range armed-recon configuration remains camera + one HSC-100F rather than full 0.40 t strike load.

G3M remains the heavy bomb-mass carrier; E6 provides high-frequency, multi-location reconnaissance/harassment and target updating.

### 6.2 Central China

The 11th Army spends the first half of December resting, repairing and studying the November operation, then begins preparing the South Henan operation.

The emerging doctrine is:

1. reconnaissance identifies reserve/route changes;
2. multi-axis ground attack forces the enemy to move;
3. reconnaissance re-acquires moving reserves and routes;
4. artillery/air/ground pressure is applied to movement and withdrawal rather than assuming the enemy will remain for a decisive frontal battle.

### 6.3 KMT–CCP

Unlike the prior peace route, no ceasefire-management architecture exists to defuse the New Fourth Army movement problem. By the end of December the route/command dispute is therefore again a serious January crisis. Exact culpability and exact casualty outcome are not pre-recorded.

## 7. 1941-01-01 strategic interpretation

The Japanese side has learned two things simultaneously:

1. the new reconnaissance/logistics/service system materially improves operational persistence and reduces disorganized loss;
2. medium-size operations still do not compel Chinese forces to remain in a decisive encirclement.

This pushes planning toward broader multi-axis/route-control operations rather than a simple search for a larger frontal battle.

The Chinese side increasingly pays a logistics/coordination tax to remain dispersed, mobile and night-oriented, but retains the strategic benefit of refusing decisive destruction.

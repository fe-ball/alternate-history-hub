# V26 REPLAY CHRONOLOGY — 1941-08-12 TO 1941-12-07 14:30 HST

Status: CURRENT replay bridge and opening-war chronology. Exact micro-political incidents between August and November are intentionally not over-specified where the conversation did not replay them.

## August–September 1941

- Diplomacy and military preparation continue in parallel after the 11 August V25 checkpoint.
- Limited US licensing demonstrates a real but narrow economic carrot.
- Tokyo concludes that small licenses do not restore strategic freedom; Washington concludes that partial relief does not stop the China war or preparation.
- Reciprocal "you move first" logic hardens.
- Southern Indochina occupation proceeds around late August / early September, roughly one month later than historical July timing.
- The one-month delay leaves December southern-base depth somewhat thinner in reserves/repair/AA than history, but opening sortie generation is near historical scale.

## October–November

- Political hardening is cumulative rather than the product of one author-selected shock.
- Army/Navy/China/Nanjing/Chongqing/US/Allied commitments narrow the space for a mutually survivable concession.
- The macro Japanese Hawaii-operation calendar converges on the historical sequence.
- 5 Nov: historical-type operational order.
- 7 Nov: Y-day set for 8 Dec Japan time / 7 Dec Hawaii local time.
- 22 Nov: main carrier concentration complete.
- 26 Nov: Kido Butai departs.
- 1 Dec: formal war decision.
- 2 Dec: execution signal.

## 6 Dec HST evening / 7 Dec early morning

- H7Y-D1 departs on the Oahu-west carrier-return search mission.
- H7Y-D2 departs for the Hawaii–Midway / Lexington-probable corridor.
- Pre-X-hour transmissions are minimized.
- Around 05:30: Tone/Chikuma local reconnaissance aircraft launch for Lahaina/Pearl confirmation.
- Around 06:05–06:15: first-wave carrier launch and formation.
- Central first-wave package = 49 B5N level bombers + 40 B5N torpedo aircraft + 51 D3A + 43 A6M = 183.

## 06:37–07:55

- Ward detects/attacks a Japanese midget submarine in historical fashion.
- Opana radar detects the incoming Japanese mass formation around historical timing, but the warning is misinterpreted and not converted into effective island-wide air defense.
- Lahaina is reported empty; Pearl contains major battleships but no carrier.
- D1/D2 remain outside the local Pearl reconnaissance role.
- 07:55: first-wave attack begins; H7Y outer carrier search becomes active after surprise has already been spent.

## First wave

Worldline coordination/repeatability improves attack conversion efficiency without changing the basic aircraft identities.

Central close:

- 40 torpedoes dropped;
- 39–40 normal entries/runs;
- 21–23 torpedo hits;
- 49 AP-bomb aircraft produce about 9–10 direct hits central, 8–11 band;
- airfield suppression is historical-level or slightly more complete;
- Japanese aircraft losses 6–8 central.

Major ship outcomes remain broadly historical in shape: Arizona catastrophic explosion, Oklahoma capsize, West Virginia bottomed, California heavily flooded, Nevada damaged but still mobile.

## Second wave

- Planned 171 aircraft; technical-abort tail is reduced, giving 170–171 attack-capable central.
- US defense is now fully awake: dense AA, smoke and some fighters.
- Better Japanese radio/formation/QC pushes attack-chain completion upward but cannot erase the harder defense.
- Nevada remains the conspicuous moving target and is heavily bombed / beached.
- Navy Yard/drydock targets receive somewhat better deconfliction.
- Japanese second-wave losses: 15–17 central.
- Port fuel farms and major repair infrastructure remain substantially intact; they are not retroactively inserted as the original second-wave objective.

## 08:17–08:20 — Enterprise contact

- D1 finds TF 8 in the Oahu-west corridor.
- Report: one carrier, three heavy cruisers, multiple destroyers, course/speed.
- Contact is assessed as Enterprise with high confidence.
- D2 does not find Lexington in the central branch.
- Enterprise already knows Pearl is under attack and raises CAP/strike readiness, but does not know Kido Butai's exact location.
- D1 and follow-on E13A seek to maintain a usable track.

## 08:20–12:20 — counter-carrier preparation

- Second Pearl wave cannot be retasked; it is already committed.
- Kido Butai immediately starts anti-ship rearm planning for returning first-wave aircraft.
- Pearl infrastructure third-wave priority drops below the live enemy carrier.
- First/Second Carrier Divisions are prepared as main strike decks; Fifth Carrier Division carries more CAP/recovery burden.
- Lexington remains unknown, so Kido Butai does not strip its own CAP.
- Enterprise conducts CAP and additional search; central branch does not locate Kido Butai.
- H7Y/E13A track survives intermittent US fighter pressure.

## 12:22–12:35 — Japanese counter-carrier launch

Central strike:

- 18 A6M2
- 27 D3A1
- 27 B5N2
- total 72

The force flies to a predicted intercept point rather than the stale last-known coordinate.

## 13:52–14:25 — first Pacific-war carrier-on-carrier air battle in this branch

- Enterprise radar detects the incoming mass formation around 13:52 at roughly 60 nmi-class range.
- About 12 F4F conduct the main outer interception, with additional local participation possible.
- Japanese A6M escort limits, but does not eliminate, CAP disruption.
- D3A attack slightly precedes the torpedo attack.
- D3A: about 19–21 valid releases; 3 bomb hits central.
- B5N: about 20–22 valid torpedo runs; 2 torpedo hits central.
- Japanese strike losses: 11–13 aircraft.
- F4F losses / forced losses: 5–7 class.
- Enterprise remains afloat but loses flight operations, burns/floods heavily and slows to about 10–15 kt class central.
- US armed-search SBDs do not find Kido Butai in the central branch.

## 14:25–14:30 — end state

- Japanese flash BDA assesses Enterprise as heavily damaged and flight-operation-disabled, not confirmed sunk.
- Sixth Fleet receives position/course/damage information.
- Submarine orders shift selected boats toward future retreat geometry.
- I-74 is the strongest first west/southwest interception candidate.
- I-2/I-3 cover a more northern/Kauai return option.
- I-68/I-69/I-75 are relevant to a southwest-Oahu/Pearl return line.
- No submarine contact/result is closed yet.

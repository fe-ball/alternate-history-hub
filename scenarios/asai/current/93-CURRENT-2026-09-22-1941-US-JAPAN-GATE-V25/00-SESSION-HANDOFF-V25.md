## 3. Chinese air/transport pressure

By spring 1941 the air campaign has moved beyond occasional bombing.

The existing chain is:

wide-area strip/photo -> develop/index/metadata -> stereo/mosaic/change detection -> coordinates/weapon suitability -> attack -> re-photo/BDA -> relocation/repair detection.

E6 Twin normally provides high-speed ISR, armed reconnaissance, BDA and selected light attack; conventional bombers provide bomb mass. E6 does not magically become the heavy-bomb payload carrier.

Gia Lam late-1940 working allocation remains 8 E6 Twin JF, ordinary mission-capable roughly 6–7. By spring 1941, working monthly activity can reach roughly 70–90 sorties from this detachment across deep photo/armed reconnaissance, south-Yunnan roads/airfields, BDA/change detection and weather/liaison. Sorties are not target-visits and are not unique targets destroyed.

Chinese response increasingly uses:

- warning;
- dispersal;
- night movement/work;
- fake parks and decoys;
- decentralized repair;
- selective interception rather than continuous unfavorable fighter battle.

Parent planning bands retained as **conditional analytical aids**, not measured national losses:

- sortie-generation tax about 15–25%;
- local mission-capable penalty about 3–8 points;
- Kunming processing friction about 8–12% in ordinary pressure and 12–18% in heavy-raid weeks;
- irreversible fighter loss can be materially lower than an aggressive-interception counterfactual because China rationally preserves aircraft.

## 5. Southern French Indochina: deferred, not abandoned

V25 corrects an earlier over-simple assumption that the Konoe government can simply cancel occupation.

Political-system interpretation:

- 25 June and 2 July decisions already created substantial institutional commitment;
- by 21–24 July Vichy acceptance, local details and force preparations are advanced;
- the prime minister cannot simply reverse this unilaterally;
- however, policy and exact execution date are not identical, and Japanese decision-making often uses procedural delay/non-decision to preserve coalition agreement.

The branch center therefore becomes:

- Third Konoe Cabinet and Foreign Minister Toyoda genuinely seek a compromise;
- the military-facing explanation is not "we surrender the policy" but "the United States has made a concrete counterproposal; wait briefly to see whether it is real";
- 25th Army and naval preparations remain;
- the first stop is 48–72 hours, later extended in small increments as concrete US responses appear.

By **28 July**, Japanese forces have **not** occupied southern Indochina in V25. This is an implementation delay, not a renunciation.

## 6. US freeze, licensing and the visible red line

V25 does not assume that no Japanese occupation means no US freeze.

Working center:

- US asset freeze can still be imposed around 25 July because preparations and Allied coordination are already advanced;
- the freeze places transactions under licensing control rather than mechanically specifying zero petroleum in the legal instrument;
- because Japan visibly stops short of southern Indochina, the subsequent license policy is less automatically total than in history;
- 1 August general oil-license restructuring/cancellation still creates a real administrative stop;
- by roughly 5–11 August, small/selected licenses, ship fuel and lower-priority petroleum products can begin to move or receive approval;
- high-octane aviation fuel, TEL and premium military inputs remain strongly controlled;
- bulk crude/diesel/full normal trade remains unresolved and politically contested.

The strategic lesson seen in Tokyo is double-edged:

1. **carrot:** the United States is not necessarily seeking immediate strangulation if Japan stays outside the southern-Indochina/Thailand/DEI military line;
2. **limit:** the United States is not thereby accepting Japan's China settlement terms, Nanjing political gains or unrestricted southern strategic freedom.

This visible red line strengthens both compromise advocates and military hard-liners.

## 7. Japan is not a unitary rational actor

V25 explicitly rejects "Japan became rational and therefore peace followed."

By August:

- Konoe/Toyoda: a real diplomatic exit may exist; do not destroy it without testing;
- Planning Board/Finance bureaucracy: avoiding full petroleum cutoff is valuable, but a narrow license trickle is not a long-term national strategy;
- Army Ministry: a short delay is tolerable; political liquidation of China gains is not;
- Army General Staff: China is visibly losing modern-war capital; do not terminate a war that is finally imposing cumulative operational/economic costs;
- China Expeditionary Army: improved operations are producing real effects even though political surrender has not followed;
- Navy Ministry: keep oil flowing and avoid a premature US war;
- Naval General Staff: if war may still occur, waiting worsens the naval balance; preparation cannot stop;
- Kwantung Army: Germany-USSR developments keep the northern option alive.

Military acceptance of delay is therefore partly **exploratory**: observe whether the United States wants only to stop southern expansion or to remove Japan's strategic options one by one.

## 9. China by August: not collapsed, but paying in hidden capital

China is not close to disappearing as a state in August 1941.

The important deterioration is not simply headcount:

- heavy artillery, anti-tank/AA, vehicles, radios, optics, medical/maintenance equipment and trained technical personnel are thin and hard to replace;
- withdrawals can preserve manpower while abandoning the expensive layer that makes formations capable of modern counteroffensives;
- air/transport pressure makes concentration, repair and daytime movement more expensive;
- the Burma Road can physically function while ton-km efficiency, trucking, spares, fuel and handling remain the bottleneck;
- war finance increasingly relies on money creation and in-kind extraction.

The direction is toward a force that can still defend, disperse, delay and fight locally but has a growing fraction of formations that are closer to **light-infantry armies** than modern combined-arms formations.

This makes two opposite political interpretations possible in Tokyo:

- "China still does not surrender; the war is structurally unwinnable at acceptable cost."
- "China is materially thinning; withdrawing now would abandon gains just as cumulative pressure is finally working."

Both are allowed. Do not choose one by authorial fiat.

# V25 REPLAY CHRONOLOGY — 1941-01-01 to 1941-08-11

This chronology records the conversation replay. Dates/bands marked WORKING remain simulation closures rather than archival exactitudes.

## January

**3–14 Jan — Wannan / New Fourth Army crisis**
- Route/command crisis escalates without the sibling peace-route defusal.
- Organized New Fourth Army headquarters is destroyed.
- Large organized breakout fails; 2–3k escape/disperse class remains a working band.
- Ye Ting capture centered on 14 Jan; Yuan Guoping death around 13 Jan.
- Xiang Ying/Zhou Zikun remain alive at the January endpoint; later March deaths are not backdated.

**15 Jan onward — South Henan preparation**
- Japanese reconnaissance/logistics preparation continues while Fifth War Zone uses depth, delaying positions and reserve movement.

**24–25 Jan — South Henan operation opens**
- Japanese multi-axis advance begins.

## February

**30 Jan–1 Feb**
- Reconnaissance and movement indicators show that a grand encirclement is not reliably closing.
- Japan limits objectives instead of forcing a bad encirclement.

**1–6 Feb**
- Fangcheng/Baihe/Nanyang axis becomes a time-limited raid.
- Nanyang taken around 4 Feb; planned withdrawal begins about 5 Feb.
- Chinese recovery follows rather than a second Japanese grand pursuit.

**11–12 Feb**
- Main operation closes/returns toward front restoration.

## March–early April

**15 Mar–early Apr — Shanggao**
- Historical-type operation occurs.
- Around 19 Mar, changing reinforcement/route geometry causes Japan to limit the 34th Division's deep push.
- No requirement to reproduce the historical dangerous overextension.
- Gao'an can be temporarily occupied/contested.
- Japanese irreversible losses lower directionally; Chinese movement losses higher directionally; exact casualties remain open.

## April

**8 Apr**
- Kunming heavy raid retained.
- Target emphasis shifts toward airfields, repair, vehicles, fuel, communications and transport-node functions.

**13 Apr**
- Soviet-Japanese Neutrality Pact.

**19 Apr**
- East-Zhejiang and roughly simultaneous Fuzhou/coastal operations.
- Permanent retention is biased toward ports, corridors and network nodes rather than every protrusion.

**29 Apr**
- Repeat Kunming attack creates explicit repair-suppression cycle after re-photo/change detection.

## May–June

**7 May — Zhongtiaoshan opens**
- Terrain/route geometry actually supports encirclement, so Japan uses it.
- E6/high-speed ISR assists route/ford/reassembly knowledge; conventional aviation carries most attack mass.

**9–15 May**
- Organized large southward escape is badly disrupted.
- Personnel can still escape in small groups, but heavy weapons, radios, supply and command cohesion are often abandoned.
- Operation is largely decided by mid-May.

**15 May–15 Jun**
- Japan consolidates roads, passes, Yellow River crossings and major nodes rather than occupying every mountain.
- Chinese regular salient is heavily reduced; guerrilla/small-unit return remains possible.
- Formal operational close around 15 Jun.

**17 Jun**
- Dutch East Indies resource talks fail.

**22 Jun**
- Barbarossa begins.

**23–28 Jun**
- Tokyo does not immediately attack USSR.
- Northern readiness is strengthened conditionally on demonstrated Soviet collapse.

## July

**2 Jul**
- Broad national policy retains China war, southern preparation and conditional north option.
- No immediate Soviet war.

**Early–mid Jul**
- Airpower audit shows a large conventional aviation mass plus a growing E5/E6 layer.
- E6 provides ISR/high cover; conventional bombers and long-range naval aircraft still provide most bomb tonnage.

**16–18 Jul**
- Second Konoe Cabinet falls; Third Konoe Cabinet forms with Toyoda as Foreign Minister.
- This creates a political window for renewed US engagement but does not erase prior military/state commitments.

**21–24 Jul**
- Vichy acceptance/local details for southern Indochina are advanced.
- Roosevelt presents a neutralization/resource-access concept.
- Toyoda/central government treats it as a real new input rather than an automatic basis for surrender.

**24–28 Jul — implementation pause**
- Government argument to military: do not renounce policy; wait 48–72 hours to test whether the American offer is real.
- 25th Army/naval preparation remains.
- Delay is extended in small increments as US responses continue.
- 28 Jul: no Japanese occupation of southern Indochina in V25.

**25 Jul**
- US asset-freeze controls can still activate.
- Tokyo distinguishes the freeze mechanism from an automatic legal declaration of zero oil.

## August through 11 Aug

**1 Aug**
- General petroleum licensing becomes much more restrictive/administratively discontinuous.
- Tokyo cannot treat "no full embargo" as normal commercial flow.

**2–4 Aug**
- Japanese military pressure rises: how long can readiness and shipping be held while Washington has not restored normal oil?
- Government shifts from "wait N days" toward "wait for concrete observable US concessions."

**5–7 Aug**
- Small/selected licenses, ship fuel and lower-priority petroleum become evidence that the American valve is not absolutely shut.
- This gives diplomacy advocates a concrete result but does not satisfy military planners.
- Konoe-Roosevelt summit concept gains importance.

**8–11 Aug**
- Heavy China air pressure continues while Japan-US diplomacy remains active.
- The coexistence is deliberate: southern expansion is paused, China war is not.
- US interpretation becomes: Japan has shown real restraint in the south but is still using force in China.
- Japanese military interpretation becomes: the US may be attempting to constrain Japan's option set, not merely one Indochina move.

**11 Aug 24:00**
- Current V25 clock.

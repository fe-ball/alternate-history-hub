## 0. Purpose

Study a torpedo-size, air-launched, high-speed anti-ship flying body closer to a later missile / rocket weapon than to a conventional aerial torpedo.

The design goal is not a water-entry torpedo. It is an **airborne anti-ship projectile** that:
- can use Type 91-class carrier handling and aircraft interfaces as far as practical;
- obtains launch speed from the carrier aircraft;
- uses ramjet propulsion after release;
- descends from release altitude, lights the ramjet, then recovers to a low terminal height;
- uses a radio altimeter for sea-height control;
- eventually carries HE and semi-armor-piercing / armor-piercing-high-explosive-type warheads.

Guidance, terminal target-seeking and fuze design remain separate gates.

## 2. Central design requirement

The intended operational carrier is the **next carrier attack aircraft / B5N class**, not a special high-speed jet aircraft.

Therefore:
- fit within roughly Type 91 carrier mass/length/diameter ecology where possible;
- retain Type 91-like suspension-point / CG / ground-handling compatibility as a design objective;
- allow a dedicated cradle/ejector because wings, inlet geometry and aerodynamic loads make literal torpedo-rack identity unrealistic;
- E5 Twin may be overloaded or specially rigged for high-speed development tests only.

Key rule:

**Twin success is not weapon qualification. Qualification must return to the B5N / next-carrier-attack-aircraft interface.**

## 4. Fixed wing during carriage

A folding/deploying wing is not required for the first article.

Preferred early logic:
- wing is fixed;
- wing incidence relative to weapon/body datum is near zero;
- at cruise / launch preparation the carrier aircraft is at small angle of attack, so the weapon wing produces limited extra lift;
- during takeoff the carrier aircraft angle of attack rises, naturally giving the weapon wing positive effective angle of attack and allowing it to carry a fraction of its own apparent weight.

This is not intended to make the B5N a better aircraft. The objective is to keep the new weapon from making takeoff materially worse than a normal 800 kg torpedo load.

Working order-of-magnitude expectation:
- low-speed local flow ~40–55 m/s;
- weapon-wing lift can plausibly offset roughly tens to low-hundreds of kgf depending CL / propwash;
- added induced/profile drag remains a real penalty.

Do not pre-record an exact takeoff-distance improvement. The success criterion is **near-Type-91 takeoff burden**, not a free aerodynamic bonus.

A later oblique / X wing or oblique attachment geometry is allowed if:
- ground clearance;
- propeller slipstream asymmetry;
- carrier-deck handling;
- separation clearance

favor it.

## 5. Cradle and spring ejector

The ordinary torpedo interface needs a dedicated cradle because the weapon has wings and aerodynamic moments.

The spring system is a **positive-separation ejector**, not a forward accelerator.

Working band for an ~780–850 kg article:
- two-point synchronized ejector;
- stroke ~0.10–0.15 m;
- total average downward force ~5–10 kN;
- peak force ~10–20 kN class;
- stored energy ~0.5–1.5 kJ;
- complete cradle/ejector mass increment roughly ~20–40 kg class after housings, latches, synchronization and structural margin.

Purpose:
- guarantee immediate clearance from the carrier aircraft;
- control initial pitch/roll;
- avoid relying on random local flow in the first fraction of a second.

It is not intended to add meaningful forward airspeed.

## 6. Baseline launch concept — no RATO

RATO is not the baseline.

The weapon instead buys lower mechanical complexity and deck-handling risk by converting launch altitude into airspeed.

Preferred sequence:

1. carrier approaches at medium-low altitude with the weapon armed/prepared;
2. carrier enters a shallow descent and builds speed;
3. ramjet ignition preparation is enabled;
4. a low-flow pre-light / pilot-flame confirmation while attached is allowed as a working option;
5. **full thrust is inhibited until positive separation unless dedicated testing later proves attached full-light safe**;
6. weapon separates through the two-point ejector;
7. main fuel schedule / full light-off is enabled immediately after separation;
8. weapon maintains a programmed descending attitude;
9. as ramjet thrust becomes useful, the weapon accelerates;
10. it performs a gentle programmed pull-out;
11. radio-altimeter height control takes over;
12. it continues at the warhead/mode-specific terminal height.

This preserves the desired "ignition confidence before release" without imposing full ramjet thrust and hot exhaust on the carrier rack.

## 7. Release altitude and speed — working band

The control burden is acceptable if the weapon is allowed to descend after release.

Working B5N-class baseline:
- release speed: ~350–380 km/h class where aircraft condition allows;
- release altitude: **~600–1,000 m central initial-test band**;
- weapon descent: roughly ~15–20 degrees;
- light-off can begin in the upper-300 / low-400 km/h region if the combustor permits;
- useful self-acceleration may require somewhat higher speed;
- pull-out begins only after the propulsion / dynamic-pressure gate is satisfied;
- initial stabilized low-altitude flight need not be single-digit meters above the sea.

If tests show that the ramjet only produces useful acceleration at much higher speed, the program may:
- raise release altitude toward ~1,000–1,400 m;
- demand more carrier dive speed;
- or reopen the RATO option.

Do not assume RATO is required before this gate is tested.

## 8. Control sequence

The early weapon does not need a modern onboard computer.

A feasible mechanical/electromechanical state sequence is:

**STANDBY  
→ LAUNCH-CONDITION  
→ SEPARATED  
→ IGNITION / DESCENT  
→ THRUST / DYNAMIC-PRESSURE GATE  
→ PULL-OUT  
→ RADIO-ALTIMETER HEIGHT CAPTURE  
→ TERMINAL RUN**

Candidate hardware:
- spun-up gyro before release;
- course/attitude gyro;
- pitot/dynamic-pressure switch;
- timer/cam sequencer;
- simple hydraulic/electric servos;
- combustion-pressure / flame confirmation logic;
- radio altimeter for the terminal height loop.

The difficult part is not logical complexity. It is robust transition sensing and avoiding excessive pitch/roll during separation and pull-out.

## 9. Radio altimeter decision

**WORKING DECISION: use a radio altimeter as the intended sea-height sensor.**

Rejected as mainline:
- hanging wire / mechanical sea feeler at high speed;
- acoustic "listening to surf" or downward sonic ranging as the primary system;
- barometric altitude alone for very-low sea-height control.

Barometric altitude may remain a coarse backup / transition instrument.

The exact radio-altimeter architecture, frequency, antenna geometry, sea-clutter behavior, minimum reliable height and production clock remain OPEN and require a dedicated sensor audit before qualification.

## 10. Warhead / height doctrine — only partially closed

Two warhead families remain plausible:

### HE / blast-fragmentation family
- can tolerate a somewhat higher terminal flight path;
- natural against destroyers, auxiliaries, transports, carrier upperworks and exposed topside systems;
- later warhead shaping / directional-fragmentation ideas are allowed as a separate warhead-design gate.

### semi-AP / APHE-like family
- prefers a lower path when the intent is a side / upper-hull impact on a larger ship;
- uses a stronger nose/body and less explosive than the HE variant;
- high terminal kinetic energy contributes to local structural damage but is not to be treated as the equivalent of a purpose-built heavy naval AP shell.

**Working doctrine decision:** the early semi-AP/APHE-type is **not a routine anti-destroyer weapon**. If its programmed height lets it pass over a small destroyer, that trade is acceptable in exchange for a geometry better suited to larger ships.

Exact terminal-height numbers are OPEN.
